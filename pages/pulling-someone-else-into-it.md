# Why do I bring a third person into conflicts instead of dealing with them directly?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Because the third person is available and cheap and the second one is expensive. The recruiting fires before you have decided anything, and it delivers most of what the direct conversation was supposed to deliver. You get heard. You get agreed with. And the pressure that was going to push you into saying it drains away.
</div>

The call ended at ten past four on a Sunday.

Your mother said one sentence in it that you are still carrying. The rest of the call was ordinary. One sentence, near the end, about the christening and who had and had not been consulted, delivered in the particular flat tone that means it had already been said to somebody else first.

Seventeen minutes later your sister has heard it.

Nobody decided that. There was no fork in the road where you weighed saying something to your mother against saying something to your sister and picked the second one. You put the phone down, you moved around the house for a while doing nothing in particular, and at some point you were sitting in the car on the driveway with a voice memo running. Four minutes long. The sentence, the tone it was said in, and the two pieces of history that make the tone mean what it means.

She replies inside the hour. She says the tone is unbelievable. She says she would have said something. She says it is exactly what happened before the wedding.

And something in you goes quiet.

That quiet is the whole subject of this page, because of what it does next. It does not make you readier to have the conversation with your mother. It makes the conversation with your mother feel less necessary, and by Tuesday it has slid down the list to somewhere below the car insurance.

The work version has the same shape and different furniture. There is a meeting at ten. There is one person in it who is going to push back on your number, and you know which one. So at twenty to ten you are in the stairwell with somebody else from the team, and the sentence you use is that you wanted to give them the background before you all go in, and the background you give is accurate, complete, and arranged.

You are not lying to anybody. Every fact in it is a fact. What you are doing is making sure that when the number gets questioned at ten past ten, one other person in the room has already heard why it is the right number, from you, in the absence of the person who is going to question it.

Then there is the version with no phone call and no meeting, which is the one people find hardest to see in themselves. Somebody is going to hear about this eventually, from one of you. You would prefer they heard it from you first. So you find a reason to bring it up, and the reason is a good one, and you would fail a lie detector on none of it.

By Wednesday, four people know about a sentence said by one person to one person on a Sunday afternoon. Two of them have views. One of them has repeated it to somebody you have not thought about. And your mother, who said the thing, has heard nothing at all and does not know that anything is happening.

Somewhere in that week a two-person problem became a four-person one. You could not point at the moment you did it, because there was no moment. There was a voice memo on a driveway, and it felt like the most natural thing in the world.

## What Does This Look Like Across an Ordinary Month?

Much smaller and much more distributed than the word for it suggests, which is why almost nobody catches it.

It looks like a two-line message that begins with the words *you will not believe what*, sent while the original conversation is technically still going.

It looks like reading a difficult email out loud to the person at the next desk before replying to it, and reading it in a particular voice.

It looks like the seating plan for the christening being discussed by three people, none of whom is the person whose seat is the problem.

It looks like asking a friend what they would have done, and describing the situation for four minutes before asking, and describing it in a way that makes only one answer available.

It looks like a phone call that opens with *am I being unreasonable here*, which is a real question when it is asked once and a rhetorical one by the third asking.

It looks like forwarding something with no comment on it, because no comment is required. The forwarding is the comment.

It looks like mentioning a version of an event to somebody who is going to hear the other version at the weekend, in enough detail that the other version will arrive sounding like a defense.

It looks like a colleague you have never worked with closely knowing, by November, a surprising amount about somebody in another team, all of it from you, none of it untrue.

Six of those in a month produces a month in which you have behaved reasonably at every individual point and have not once said a difficult thing to the person it concerned.

## What Is the Name for This, and What Is the Name Worth?

The word is **triangulation**, and it is genuinely useful, with two large caveats attached that almost nobody supplies.

Start with what it describes. A relationship between two people has a particular property: whatever is happening in it has to be held by those two, and there is nowhere for the tension to go. Add a third person and that stops being true. Now there can be a pair and an outsider, a majority and a minority, an audience and a performance. The claim at the center of the idea is that a two-person relationship under strain reliably reaches for a third to steady itself, and that once the third is in place the original strain can go on indefinitely without ever being addressed, because it is now being carried by an arrangement rather than by two people.

Read against your own week, that description does a lot of work. It explains why the conversation with your mother stopped feeling urgent. It explains why the same three names keep appearing in the same combinations. It explains why a family can run for decades with everybody knowing what the problem is and nobody ever raising it with the person concerned.

**Where the word came from, and what that is worth.** It belongs to the family systems tradition, which developed in the middle of the last century out of clinical work with families rather than out of laboratories. The triangle was described as the smallest stable unit of a relationship system, on the grounds that two people under pressure will draw in a third and that a three-person arrangement can absorb more strain than a two-person one can. I am naming the model rather than crediting one figure with it, partly because more than one tradition arrived at the same structure independently and partly because the names attached to it are already carrying other work elsewhere on this site. The version where the third person is a child is set out with its attributions on the pages about [being turned against one parent by the other](/patterns/did-one-parent-turn-me-against-the-other/) and about [a teenager whose crisis belongs to a marriage](/patterns/is-my-teenagers-crisis-about-my-marriage/).

**What the term captures accurately.** That the move is structural rather than emotional, so it can be made by somebody who is calm. That it happens fast and usually without a plan. That the relief it produces is real. And, most usefully, that the arrangement is stable, which is the part that explains why a situation everybody can see stays exactly where it is for years.

**What the term does not capture, and this is the half that gets dropped.** It names a shape and says nothing about a cause. Two people can produce the identical arrangement for entirely different reasons and need entirely different things. It also says nothing about who was right. A person with a completely legitimate grievance and a person with an unfair one will make the same move in the same week, and the word applies equally to both.

**And there is a third problem, which is what has happened to the word in public.** Almost everything written about triangulation now is written as an accusation. It describes something a manipulative person is doing to you, usually a parent or a partner, deliberately, as a strategy. That framing has one enormous advantage, which is that it never requires the reader to look at themselves, and one fatal flaw, which is that it does not describe the thing. From inside, this behavior has no strategy in it at all. There is a phone call, a feeling, and a voice memo, in that order, inside twenty minutes.

| How the word is usually used | What it actually describes |
|---|---|
| Something a manipulative person does to you | A structural move most people make, usually without deciding to, including you |
| A trait somebody has | An arrangement between three people that can form on a Sunday and dissolve by Friday |
| A clinical finding with evidence behind it | A model produced from watching families in consulting rooms, with almost no direct measurement attached |
| Proof that the other person was in the wrong | A description of a shape, which applies identically to fair grievances and unfair ones |
| Something that mainly happens in families with children | The most written-about version. The most common version is two adults and somebody from work |
| A thing you can be diagnosed with | Nothing a clinician can enter anywhere, and there is no accepted way to measure it |

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Four situations, and none of them is what most people arriving here are looking at.

If a direct conversation would put you or a child at physical risk, then working out whether your indirect route is a reality check or a coalition is the wrong question and the wrong week to be asking it. Recruiting support is the correct move and it needs proper help rather than a page.

If what is being carried between third parties concerns somebody's safety, or sits inside a legal or workplace process where you should be taking advice, then the honest answer is that you need somebody whose job this is, and that a self-observation exercise will slow you down.

If you are currently the third person, being used by two adults to carry what belongs between them, and you are still living inside that arrangement, that is a different situation from the one described here and it deserves proper attention.

And if there is any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a licensed professional now rather than later, and everything else here can wait indefinitely.

## What If You Genuinely Need a Reality Check?

This root goes first, at length, because a page that skips it will make careful people paranoid about something entirely reasonable.

You cannot see your own reaction from outside. That is a permanent condition rather than a weakness, and it means that when somebody says something to you and you feel a lurch, you have no way of telling from the inside whether the lurch is proportionate. The only instrument available is another person. You describe what happened, you watch their face, and their face gives you information you had no other route to.

This is one of the most useful things friendship does, and it is the reason people who have nobody to check with drift so badly. Somebody with one honest friend will find out within a day that they overreacted. Somebody with none can spend a fortnight building a case out of a remark that was about the parking.

The checking version has a particular feel to it and most people can recognize it in memory. There is a question in it that you do not know the answer to. You describe the other person's side, badly, but you describe it. You are slightly hoping they will tell you it is nothing, because that would be the cheaper outcome. And when they say something you were not expecting, it lands, and it changes what you do next.

That version is legitimate, common, and not what this page is about. Nothing here is asking you to stop doing it.

What is worth knowing is that the checking version and the coalition version begin identically, use the same words, and are told to the same friend. They diverge later, and the divergence is small enough to miss and consequential enough to be worth catching. The next section is the whole of the useful material here.

## What Actually Separates a Reality Check From Building a Side?

Five questions, none of them about what you felt while doing it, and all of them about behavior you can check.

**The disagreement question. What happened when somebody did not agree with you?**

This is the strongest single test and it is worth running before the other four. A reality check is a request for information, and information that contradicts you is still information, so it lands and it changes something. A coalition is a request for ratification, and a contradiction is a failed result. The reliable tell is what came next. If the answer is that you updated, you were checking. If the answer is that you went and found somebody else, and then possibly somebody after that, you had stopped checking before the first conversation ended.

Most people can answer this instantly about a specific recent situation, and the answer is frequently uncomfortable.

**The audience question. Could the person you told have gone either way?**

Choosing the audience is the most invisible part of the whole move, and it happens in under a second. Almost everybody, asked which friend they would never take a particular complaint to, can name them immediately and can say exactly why. That knowledge is doing work all the time and it almost never rises to the level of a decision. A person whose loyalty in this matter was already committed cannot function as a check, whatever they say, because their answer was available before you started talking.

**The recognition question. Would the other person recognize the account you gave?**

Not agree with it. Recognize it. Would they say that is roughly what happened, with two things missing, or would they say that is not what happened at all? This test is useful because it does not require you to have lied about anything. Every fact can be accurate and the account can still be arranged, because selection does the same work that invention would have to do. The specific question worth asking is whether you said out loud, to anybody, the strongest part of their case. Most people who recognize themselves on this page have never done that once.

**The request question. Did you ask something, or deliver something?**

A reality check ends in a question mark. A coalition ends in a verdict with a pause after it. The words can be identical: *am I being unreasonable here* is a genuine question the first time and a piece of stagecraft by the third. What separates them is whether an answer existed that would have altered your course. If no answer they could have given would have changed anything you did, then you were not asking.

**The aftermath question. Did the two-person conversation still need to happen?**

This is the one that matters most and the one nobody runs, because it can only be checked afterward. A reality check leaves the original problem exactly where it was, still needing to be dealt with, sometimes more clearly than before. A coalition dissolves it. The urgency drops, the situation moves down the list, and three weeks later the thing has still not been said and no longer feels like it needs saying.

| The question | Checking looks like | Building a side looks like |
|---|---|---|
| What happened when they disagreed? | You updated, and what you did next changed | You went and asked somebody else |
| Could they have gone either way? | You picked somebody whose answer you could not predict | You picked somebody whose answer was decided before you spoke |
| Would the other person recognize your account? | Roughly, with things missing | They would say that is not what happened |
| Did you ask or deliver? | There was an answer that would have changed your course | No answer available would have changed anything |
| Did the conversation still need to happen? | Yes, and possibly more clearly | The urgency dropped and it slid down the list |

Two notes on using this. Most real situations are a mixture, and the mixture is normal rather than damning. And the fifth row is worth more than the other four combined, because it is the only one that measures the actual consequence rather than the intention.

## Why Does Being Agreed With Feel Like the Matter Has Been Handled?

Because agreement closes an open question, and an open question is the uncomfortable part.

Look at what you were actually holding when the call ended. A sentence, a tone, and a private uncertainty about whether the whole thing was in your head. That uncertainty is more unpleasant than the sentence was. It has no shape, it cannot be acted on, and it keeps returning.

Then somebody says the tone was unbelievable, and in that second the uncertainty converts into a fact. Two people now hold it. It has a shape. It can be referred to. The relief is genuine and it is out of all proportion to anything that has actually changed, because nothing has actually changed except that a second person is now holding the same version.

The trouble is where the relief lands. It lands in the same place the direct conversation was going to land, on the same pressure, and it gets there in four minutes instead of four days. A pressure that has been discharged does not push anything. Whatever was going to make you raise it on Tuesday is gone by Sunday evening.

There is a second effect running underneath the first, and it is better evidenced than most of what this page rests on. When people who already lean the same way talk a position over together, the group's position tends to move further out rather than settling toward the middle. Isenberg's 1986 meta-analysis pulled together a large body of work on this and found the effect to be reliable across a wide range of topics and setups, which makes it one of the more replicated findings in small-group research. A later experiment ran the thing directly: Schkade and colleagues assembled groups of people who already agreed with each other on contested questions, had them deliberate, and found that afterward they held more extreme views than they had walked in with, and that the groups had also become more internally uniform.

The honest limits are worth stating. That work was done on political attitudes in arranged groups, and nobody has run it on a person recounting an argument with their mother to a sympathetic sister. The extension is a reasonable inference and it is an inference. What the work supports is the general shape: a position talked over with people who lean your way is more likely to harden than to settle, and the feeling of having thought it through carefully is exactly what that hardening feels like from inside.

Put the two together and you have the mechanism. The recruiting relieves the pressure that would have produced a conversation, and the agreement makes the position less revisable. You end the week calmer and further from resolution than you started it.

## Why Does Your Own Version Get Harder to Change Every Time You Tell It?

Because each telling is a rehearsal, and rehearsal does its work whether or not you are being dishonest.

The second telling is shorter than the first. The fourth has a structure. By the sixth there is a line in it that gets a reaction, and the line stays because it works, and two pieces of context that complicated it have quietly fallen out because they slowed the thing down. Nobody decided any of that. It is what happens to any account that gets told repeatedly to people who respond to it.

There is a laboratory finding that bears on this from an unexpected direction. Coman and Hirst and colleagues have studied what a conversation does to memory, and reported that when somebody recounts an event selectively, both the speaker and the listener afterward show reduced recall for the related material that went unmentioned. Talking about part of an event makes the unmentioned part of it harder to retrieve later, for everybody in the conversation.

Hold that loosely. The effects are modest, the work is done in laboratories on material with no personal stake attached, and applying it to a family argument is my extension rather than anybody's finding. Held loosely, it still suggests something worth sitting with: the version you tell your ally is not only the version they now hold. Over enough tellings it becomes closer to the version you have available.

Which is why the sixth telling feels more certain than the first, and why the certainty is not evidence of anything.

## What Happens to a Two-Person Problem Once Four People Know?

It stops being a problem and becomes an arrangement, and arrangements are much more durable than problems.

The oldest useful account of why sits in sociology rather than psychology. Simmel argued at the beginning of the last century that moving from two people to three changes the situation in kind rather than in degree. In a pair, each person faces the other with nothing between them and the whole thing ends if either walks away. Add one and majorities become possible for the first time, an outsider position exists, and the third party acquires several very different roles they can occupy: the impartial party who helps the other two settle it, the one who benefits from the two staying at odds, and the one who plays them off against each other. Caplow took the coalition part of that further in the 1960s, working out formally which pairings tend to form in a triad and showing that the strongest member is frequently the one left out, because the other two have more reason to combine.

That framework is theoretical and the experimental part of it was played out in artificial games with points and payoffs, which is a long way from a christening. Take it as a way of seeing the shape rather than as a measurement of anything.

Seen that way, the shape is clear enough. What used to require two people to sit with something now requires nobody to. The tension has somewhere to go. Every time it builds, it gets distributed rather than resolved, and the distribution works, which is precisely why the arrangement lasts. Families run like this for decades. Teams run like this until somebody leaves.

And the original two people acquire a strange property, which is that they can be perfectly civil. The conflict does not need to appear between them any more, because it is being handled elsewhere. From outside, and often from inside, the relationship looks fine. It is simply no longer a relationship in which anything difficult is ever said.

## Which Part of This Did You Actually Choose?

Almost none of the part you feel guilty about, and all of a part you have probably never looked at.

The recruiting happens too fast to be a decision. It fires before you could have said what you were feeling, and there is no moment in it where two options got laid out and the worse one was picked. Applying guilt there produces nothing, because guilt applied where you had no vote just adds a second thing to manage.

The chosen part sits later and it is easy to find once you know where to look. It is the hour after somebody agrees with you. The pressure has dropped. You feel steady. The thing you were going to say to your mother is still entirely available, and it now feels unnecessary rather than frightening, which is a completely different obstacle from the one you had an hour ago.

That hour is the only genuinely voluntary moment in the sequence, and almost everybody spends it the same way, which is by treating the drop in pressure as evidence that the matter has been dealt with.

There is a second chosen moment, smaller and easier. It is the point at which you notice you are about to tell a fourth person. The first telling is reflex. The third is a decision, and it is available to you, and it is the cheapest place in this entire pattern to change anything.

## How Good Is the Evidence Behind Any of This?

Weaker than the confidence of everything else you will read on this word, and the weakness is worth mapping precisely.

**The central idea has almost no direct measurement behind it.** The triangle came out of clinical observation. Somebody watched a great many families across a great many years, noticed a recurring shape, and described it. That is a legitimate way to generate an idea and a poor way to test one. There is no accepted instrument for measuring whether a given three-person arrangement is a triangle. There is no body of published work reporting triangulation scores, because that body of work does not exist. Related concepts from the same tradition have generated questionnaires; the triangle itself largely has not.

**The tradition it comes from sits outside the experimental literature and mostly predates it.** These ideas were built in the 1950s, 60s and 70s, largely by clinicians writing up what they saw, in books rather than in journals with control conditions attached. Plenty of durable ideas arrived that way. What it means in practice is that the concept never went through the filtering that a submitted experiment goes through, and that a century of confident restatement has accumulated on top of a foundation that was descriptive from the beginning.

**The word has drifted so far that studies and articles using it are frequently describing different things.** In the clinical tradition it names a structural arrangement that lowers tension between two people. In most of what circulates online it names a deliberate manipulation strategy performed by one person on others. In some research on adolescents it means something narrower again, specifically a child being drawn into parental conflict, and that version does have measures attached to it. Three different referents, one word. Any confident claim that connects the online version to a research finding is almost certainly connecting two different things.

**The neighbors I have used are in better shape and they are not the same claim.** Group polarization is well replicated, and it was measured on group discussions of attitudes rather than on personal accounts of conflicts. The conversational memory work is careful and its effects are modest and laboratory-bound. The sociology of triads is a framework rather than a finding, and its experimental portion used artificial games. In every case I am borrowing a mechanism from a nearby field and applying it here, and you should treat the application as an argument rather than as evidence.

**What survives all of that.** That two people under strain frequently pull in a third. That being agreed with is relieving. That relief tends to reduce urgency. That positions discussed with allies tend to harden. That the arrangement, once it forms, is stable. Those five claims are modest, they are checkable against your own last month, and they are considerably more than the word delivers on its own.

## Why Do Two People Make the Identical Move for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the move is the visible part, and the visible part carries almost no information.

Two people send an identical four-minute voice memo on an identical Sunday. One of them has an aunt who has been dangerous to disagree with for thirty years, and going through a sibling is an accurate reading of a real power difference. The other has an entirely reasonable family and simply cannot sit in an unfinished tension for more than about an hour without needing somebody to take it off them.

Those two are indistinguishable from outside. Every piece of advice that starts from the behavior gets given to both, and it will be wrong for at least one. The first person does not have a communication problem and does not need one solved. The second one has a tolerance problem, and telling them to go direct is telling them to do the one thing the whole move exists to avoid.

Five reasons produce this often enough to be worth separating. The first has already been dealt with above, because it is legitimate and it goes first. Here are the other four, and then a way of telling which is yours.

## What If the Direct Conversation Would Actually Cost You Something?

Then recruiting support is competent rather than avoidant, and nothing here is asking you to stop.

There are people with whom a direct conversation carries an asymmetric price. A manager who can end your income and has shown you what happens to people who push back. A family member whose reaction to being contradicted has been frightening before. A landlord. A person who controls something you need. In each of those, going direct hands somebody a stick, and going around them costs almost nothing.

Choosing the cheaper route there is an accurate reading of your situation. Any article telling you to speak openly regardless of consequence was written by somebody who did not think about who might be reading it.

The tell for this root is that the cost is nameable. You can say who, what, and what happened last time, and it is concrete rather than atmospheric. Vague dread about how badly a conversation would go is a real experience and it is a different thing from an identifiable consequence, and confusing the two is how this root gets claimed by people it does not belong to.

The question worth asking is narrower, and it is the only one that separates a competent response from a stuck one. Does the route stay where it is needed?

Because it generalizes. A person who cannot raise anything at work and routes around it there will very often find, on inspection, that they now route around a friend of eleven years who has never once reacted badly to anything. The skill was acquired somewhere it was necessary and it does not check the address before running. Keep the version doing accurate work. Look hard at the version that has spread.

## What If You Need Them to Be Wrong in Front of Somebody?

Then what you are after is a verdict, and a verdict is a different product from a resolution.

This root is uncomfortable to read about and it is more common than the others put together. Underneath it there is a proposition that almost never gets stated even privately: that the thing which would actually settle this is not an agreement between the two of you but a finding, in public, that you were right and they were not.

Notice what that requires. A resolution needs two people and no audience. A verdict needs an audience and does not particularly need the other person, which is why this root produces the version where the absent party is never approached at all. They are not the counterparty. They are the defendant, and defendants are not consulted.

It is worth naming without contempt, because the wish is not stupid and it usually has history behind it. Almost everybody carrying this has a real record of being the one who was told they were overreacting, of losing the argument to somebody more fluent, or of growing up in a house where being right was never enough on its own and you needed somebody else to say it. In that history, a witness is not a luxury.

The tells are specific. Being agreed with produces something closer to triumph than to relief. You find yourself checking whether the third person has repeated it. And if the other person were to apologize privately, with nobody else present, some part of you would find that unsatisfying in a way that is difficult to admit and easy to recognize.

If what you notice is that the need is to have the last word in the argument itself rather than a finding in front of others, [that is a related and separate mechanism](/patterns/needing-the-last-word/).

## What If You Cannot Sit With It Unresolved?

Then the third person is a way of putting something down, and the speed is the point.

An unfinished two-person tension has a physical quality. It sits somewhere around the chest and it does not respond to reasoning. Nothing about it is subtle and most people will do a great deal to end it, which is a fact about bodies rather than a fact about character.

Telling somebody ends it in minutes. The tension does not go anywhere useful. It gets distributed, and distribution feels almost identical to resolution for the first day or two, which is exactly long enough for the urgency to be gone.

The tell for this root is the timing. Look at the gap between the event and the first telling. People in this root are frequently under twenty minutes, sometimes under five, and the telling starts while the original conversation is still technically running. Compare that with the version where you thought about it for a day and then rang somebody, which is a different animal entirely.

The second tell is what happens if you try not to. If you deliberately hold it for a single evening and what shows up is not calm but something closer to agitation that keeps getting worse until you send the message, then the telling was doing a job, and the job was not communication.

This root responds to something very ordinary and slightly boring, which is practice at holding it for longer. An evening. Then a day. The capacity is trainable and it moves faster than people expect, and it is the only one of the five where the useful work is available immediately.

## What If Messages Always Went Through Somebody Where You Grew Up?

Then this is procedure rather than choice, and it has never once presented itself as a decision.

Some households run entirely on relay. Your father's view of something reaches you through your mother. Your mother finds out what you think from your brother. Nobody says a difficult thing to the person it concerns, ever, and the system works well enough that nobody in it has noticed it is a system.

A child in that house learns the procedure long before they learn there is another one. What gets installed is a default about routing rather than a belief about conflict, and defaults do not announce themselves. Twenty-two years later you are on a driveway with a voice memo and it feels like the obvious thing to do, because in the only model of family communication you ever had, it was.

The tells for this root are structural rather than emotional. There is no particular charge attached to the move. You do it with people you are not upset with, about things that are not serious, in situations where a direct word would have cost nothing. Somebody outside the family, hearing how a piece of news traveled, finds it genuinely strange, and you find their reaction genuinely strange.

The other tell is that you can usually draw the map. Most people from a household like this can say within thirty seconds which two people talked about which third, and how a message got from one end of the family to the other. Being able to draw it that quickly is the evidence.

Where the family arrangement itself is the larger subject, and separateness rather than conflict is the difficulty, [that has a page of its own](/patterns/enmeshment/).

## Which of the Five Is Yours, and How Would You Know?

Judge it by evidence you already have rather than by which description reads better.

| If this is true | The reason is probably | The tell that confirms it | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| You described their side badly but you described it, and what they said changed something | You were checking | You can name the thing they said that you had not thought of | Nothing. Carry on |
| You can name the specific consequence and it is concrete | A real power difference | It stops with people who have no power over you | Nothing inside that situation. Check whether it spread |
| A private apology would feel unsatisfying | You want a finding | You checked whether the third person repeated it | Examining what the audience was originally for |
| The gap between the event and the first telling is under twenty minutes | You cannot hold it | Trying to hold it for one evening produces agitation | Practice at holding, which is trainable and quick |
| You can draw the family map in thirty seconds | Inherited procedure | You do it with people you are not upset with, about nothing | Noticing it is a procedure, which takes a while |

Two things about the table. Most people are a blend, usually of the fourth row and one other, and the blend matters less than the dominant one. And the third row is the one people move past quickly, so if you skimmed it, that is worth a second look.

## Is Going Direct Always the Right Move?

No, and the advice to go direct is given far too freely by people who have not counted what it costs.

Direct conversations are expensive. They take a specific kind of energy, they can go badly in ways that cannot be undone, and they carry a genuine risk of making a workable situation unworkable. Plenty of two-person tensions are best left to dissolve on their own, and plenty do. A relationship in which every small friction gets raised and processed is not obviously better than one in which most of them are absorbed, and anybody who has lived with somebody committed to total directness knows this.

There are also situations where the third person is straightforwardly correct. A colleague who genuinely needs context before a meeting is being informed rather than positioned. A friend who is asked to sanity-check something before it gets raised is doing a real job. Somebody both people can talk to, whose account either of them would recognize, and whose involvement makes the two-person conversation more likely, is functioning as a bridge. That role exists and it is valuable and nothing on this page is written against it.

The claim here is narrower than go direct, and it is worth stating exactly. In most instances of this pattern, the third person was chosen before the direct option had been assessed at all. There was no comparison. The route was taken and then, afterward, a reason for it was available if anybody had asked. That is a different situation from having weighed a difficult conversation and decided against it, which is a legitimate decision people make all the time.

So the useful question is whether a decision was ever made, rather than whether to go direct.

## What Does This Cost, and Who Pays It?

Rarely you, in the short run, which is exactly why it lasts.

**The person you told pays first.** They now hold a version of somebody they may have to sit across from at a christening. They cannot check it, because checking would mean going to the person it is about. They cannot raise it, for the same reason. They cannot forget it. And they have acquired a position on a matter they had no stake in, on the basis of one account. If they later meet the absent person and find them perfectly reasonable, they now have a private problem about you that they will almost certainly never mention.

**The absent person pays without knowing.** They are being discussed in rooms they will never enter, on evidence they cannot answer, by people whose warmth toward them is quietly changing. They notice something eventually. What they notice is that a cousin has gone slightly cool, and they have no idea why, and there is no version of that conversation available to them.

**You pay last and in a different currency.** The account gets more certain and less accurate. The number of people who could tell you something you have not already decided shrinks. And you acquire a growing list of two-person conversations that were never had, each of which is now harder to start than it was, because starting one means explaining why it took until November.

**And the relationship you were protecting pays the largest bill of all.** Nothing difficult gets said in it, so nothing difficult gets repaired in it, so its capacity to survive anything difficult quietly declines. From outside it looks civil. It is civil. It has simply stopped being a place where either of you finds out anything about the other.

## Where Does This Hide When It Does Not Look Like Taking Sides?

In several places where it reads as diligence, or loyalty, or good management.

**In briefing.** Context given to somebody before a meeting, which is a normal professional act, delivered in a version where the other side's strongest point does not appear. What is missing there works as an omission with a direction, which is a different act from lying.

**In consulting.** Asking four people what they would do, where the first two answers were already what you were going to do. The consulting is real. The number is the tell.

**In protecting somebody.** Telling a third person so they are prepared, or so they are not blindsided, or because they would want to know. The stated reason is often true. It is worth noticing whether the person you are protecting had asked to be.

**In the pre-emptive account.** Getting your version to somebody before the other version reaches them. This one wears the clothes of ordinary conversation almost perfectly, because it usually is ordinary conversation with one piece of timing attached.

**In humor.** A story told well, at somebody's expense, in a room where they are not. Everybody laughs and nobody notices that a position has just been distributed to four people.

**In the sympathetic listener role.** Being the one everybody comes to. This is the version people are proudest of, and it is worth asking what proportion of your closeness with certain people is made of material about somebody who is not present. The page on [talking about people behind their backs](/patterns/talking-about-people/) covers the closeness-through-shared-judgment mechanism properly.

## Where Does This Stop and Something Else Begin?

Five neighboring patterns get folded into this one, and separating them matters, because the wrong name leads to the wrong work.

**Talking about people.** Most talk about absent people is neutral, does no structural work, and is simply how humans discuss the world. The mechanism by which a shared judgment produces fast closeness, and what that closeness costs, belongs to [the page on talking about people](/patterns/talking-about-people/). What this page owns is narrower: the third person recruited into a live two-person conflict, where the recruiting changes the shape of the conflict.

**Venting.** Discharging a feeling onto somebody, at volume, faster than they can absorb it, is a pacing problem rather than a structural one. You can do it to a stranger and change nothing about who is aligned with whom. [The unloading page](/patterns/unloading-on-people/) owns that, including what the listener actually experiences.

**Complaining about the same thing for years.** If the striking feature is repetition, the same account to the same listener across three years, then the subject is the loop rather than the recruitment, and [that page](/patterns/complaining-without-changing/) owns it. It also answers the specific question of why the direct version keeps getting postponed. This page is about the moment the third person is brought in and what that does to the structure, rather than about the years afterward.

**Being the third person yourself.** If what you recognize is having been the one messages traveled through as a child, carrying what belonged between two adults, that is the other side of this shape and it produces its own consequences. [That page](/patterns/did-one-parent-turn-me-against-the-other/) owns it, and [the version where a couple's conflict gets routed through a teenager](/patterns/is-my-teenagers-crisis-about-my-marriage/) is covered separately.

**Sending the message sideways.** If the dissatisfaction goes into timing, slowness, or a compliment with something in it, rather than into another person, that is a different route with the same destination and [it has its own page](/patterns/expressing-anger-sideways/). The two frequently run in the same person, and the giveaway for which one is dominant is whether there is an audience.

Two more worth naming. If the thing you keep noticing is that the same argument returns in the same shape regardless of who you are with, [that pattern is separate](/patterns/why-do-we-keep-having-the-same-fight/). And if the difficulty is that you avoid the direct conversation with everybody, with or without a third person involved, [the avoidance itself is the subject](/patterns/why-do-i-avoid-conflict/).

## What Does the Resolved Version Look Like on an Ordinary Tuesday?

Considerably smaller than people expect, and it does not involve becoming somebody who says everything to everybody.

Your mother says the sentence. You still feel the lurch, and the lurch does not get smaller, because it was never the problem. What is different is that somewhere in the next hour you notice the reach happening, and you notice it as a reach rather than as a plan.

You still tell your sister. That does not stop and there is no reason for it to. What changes is one small thing in the telling, which is that at some point you say the part that complicates it, the piece of context that makes your mother's sentence slightly more understandable, and you say it out loud rather than leaving it out. That single sentence is most of the work, and it takes about nine seconds, and it changes what your sister is able to give back to you.

You notice the drop in urgency when it arrives, and you notice it as information rather than as resolution. Sometimes you then have the conversation with your mother, badly, four days later, and it lasts a couple of minutes and is unremarkable. Most of the time you do not, and you decide not to rather than drifting into not.

The fourth telling mostly stops happening. Not through a rule. It stops because you have started noticing it as a decision, and once something is a decision it becomes possible to make it differently.

And there is one durable change that people report and do not expect: you find out things. Somebody tells you something about the situation you had not considered, because you asked in a way that made room for it, and the situation turns out to have a second half in it. That happens perhaps once every few months, and it is worth more than all of the rest.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

Three different speeds, and running them together is why people abandon this in the third week.

The noticing moves first and it moves at the wrong end. For the first few weeks you will catch yourself an hour after the voice memo has been sent, which feels like failure and is the normal first stage. Within a month or two most people catch it while it is happening. Catching it before it happens is a matter of months rather than weeks, and it never becomes completely reliable.

The habit takes longer, and it is worth expecting the reach to still be the first thing that fires under pressure. It will return during any week that is already hard, with the specific person it was originally built around, and immediately after any direct conversation that went badly. A return under those conditions is the return of the conditions.

The reason underneath moves on its own schedule and does not respond to effort applied at the behavior. A real power difference resolves when the situation resolves. An inherited routing default can sit quietly for years and then loosen across one unremarkable season, usually because you spent time somewhere that ran differently. The wish for a verdict changes when the history behind it is looked at, which is slow.

One thing works immediately and for everybody regardless of which reason is yours. Counting who you told, for two weeks, without changing anything. That is available today and it does not require knowing anything about yourself first.

## Where Does This Thinking Come From?

From a modest amount of published research and a much larger amount of watching people describe this in rooms, and the two are worth separating.

On the hardening of a position through sympathetic discussion, Isenberg published the meta-analysis of group polarization in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology in 1986, and Schkade and colleagues reported the like-minded deliberation experiment in 2007, in which groups of people who already agreed ended up further out and more uniform than they started. On what a conversation does to memory, Coman and Hirst and colleagues have published a line of work since the mid-2000s on the way selective recounting reduces later recall of the unmentioned material, for the speaker as well as the listener. On the structure of three-person situations, Simmel's account of the difference between a pair and a triad dates from 1908, and Caplow set out the coalition analysis in *Two Against One* in 1968.

The family systems triangle I have deliberately described without attaching a name to it. More than one clinical tradition arrived at the same structure independently in the same decades, the figures usually credited with it are already carrying other work on other pages here, and the model is better served by being stated accurately than by being attached to a single person. I have also left several relevant researchers unused. There is a substantial literature on third parties in workplace disputes and on negative ties inside organizations, and it would have made the citation list longer without making the argument any stronger.

The rest is mine and carries no research weight at all. Five years and more than five thousand hours of doing this work on myself before I built anything around it, and several hundred conversations since in which somebody has described the voice memo on the driveway, the stairwell at twenty to ten, and the specific quiet that arrives when a person who was always going to agree with you agrees with you. I hold no clinical qualification of any kind. None of the researchers named has any connection to this page or to the seminar, and where their published work and my own experience disagree, theirs is the one to go and check.

## What Is Worth Doing This Week, Before You Say Anything to Anybody?

Two weeks of counting, and no conversations at all.

Change nothing. Do not resolve to go direct, do not announce that you are working on this, and do not tell anybody what you are doing, because announcing it converts the whole thing into something you are being watched on and that is the fastest way to stop looking honestly.

Every time you tell somebody about a difficulty with somebody else, write down four things. Who you told. How long after the event. What you were asking them for, in your own words, honestly. And what you would have done if they had disagreed.

By the end of the fortnight you will have somewhere between five and twenty of them, and three things will be visible that were not available any other way. One name will appear far more often than the rest. The gap between the event and the first telling will be far more consistent than you expected. And the fourth column will be close to empty, which is the entire finding.

Then take the two most recent entries and ask the fifth question from earlier: did the two-person conversation still need to happen afterward, or did the urgency simply go?

If what landed hardest here was the timing, the counting will probably be enough on its own and you can stop there. If what landed hardest was something else, the recognition that a private apology would feel unsatisfying, or that you can draw your family's map in thirty seconds, or that you already know which friend you would never take this to and why, then the thing worth working on sits underneath the route rather than in it. [Our Sacred Valley seminar](https://florianrolke.com/the-soulsearching-experience/) works at that layer. First we find what the move is actually doing for you, because a behavior that reliable is never doing nothing, and then what stands in the way of the version where the thing gets said, and then a plan you are held accountable to afterward, back in your own week with the same aunt and the same seating plan. The reason it works on this particular pattern is the one thing you have never had access to: a room where we can be helpful to each other as well as honest with each other, which is a rarer combination than it sounds, and the exact opposite of a room full of people who were always going to agree with you. Everybody is screened before arriving, which keeps the group small enough that somebody who has been routing around one person for twenty-two years gets worked with properly rather than handed a worksheet. One limitation worth stating plainly: exact dates, the price, and the final cap on group size are still being settled, so anybody comparing programs on those numbers this week cannot yet compare ours. Ask every program you are weighing the same questions, this one included, and decide from what comes back.

The fortnight of counting costs nothing and is worth having whichever way it comes out.
