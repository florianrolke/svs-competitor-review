# Why do I suddenly go off someone I was attracted to?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Because disgust is faster than thought and it does not answer to argument. A detail arrived, your body issued a verdict, and the reasoning you have been offering since was assembled afterward to explain a decision already made. That is why knowing it is unfair changes nothing. What the revulsion means, though, varies enormously.

The escalator was stopped. That happens at that station, and everybody does the same thing, which is to walk up it, and it takes about eleven seconds.

They ran. Not properly, because nobody properly runs up a stationary escalator, but a short, effortful trot with a tote bag swinging out and hitting one leg on every second step, and a small backward glance at the top to see whether you were coming.

You had been looking forward to this evening for four days.

Something closed. You felt it happen. There was no thought in it and no sentence attached to it, just a drop somewhere behind your sternum, and a sudden, total certainty that you did not want to be touched by this person again.

You spent the rest of the evening being kind, because they had done absolutely nothing, and because the alternative was explaining. On the way home you tried out the sentence in your head. They ran up an escalator. That is the whole thing. That is the entirety of what happened.

## What Does This Actually Look Like, in the Second It Happens?

It is faster than the account of it, which is most of the problem.

There is no deliberation. Nothing gets weighed. There is a detail, and then there is a state you are already in, and the gap between them is small enough that people routinely describe it as instantaneous even though it cannot quite be. Somebody says a word slightly wrong. Somebody holds a fork like a shovel. Somebody uses a voice on a dog that they have never used on an adult. Somebody sends a message with a word in it that you now cannot unsee.

And then the whole architecture goes.

Not just the attraction, though the attraction is what you notice first. The plans go with it, and the memory of the good evenings gets reprocessed, and things that were charming on Tuesday are unbearable by Thursday, and the person you were describing to a friend last week as unexpectedly great has been replaced by somebody you are now looking for reasons to avoid.

What is strange, and what nearly everybody who has this reports independently, is the guilt arriving almost simultaneously. The reaction and the shame about the reaction are close to the same event. You are repelled and you are appalled at yourself for being repelled, and both are running while you are still standing in the same room being pleasant.

Then comes the week of trying to argue with it. This is the part people spend the most time on and get the least from. You list their qualities. You remind yourself that the trigger was nothing. You point out, correctly, that you also hold a fork oddly and that somebody has probably had this reaction to you. All of it is true. None of it does anything.

## What Is the Ick, and How Much Research Is Actually Underneath the Phrase?

The word is **the ick**, and it is worth having as long as you get an honest account of where it came from and how thin the ground under it is.

The term describes a sudden and physical aversion to somebody you were romantically or sexually interested in, triggered by a specific detail that is usually small, usually behavioral, and usually impossible to defend as a reason. Two things do the work in that definition. **Suddenness**, because a gradual cooling is a different experience with a different feel and probably a different cause. And **triviality of the trigger**, because the mismatch between the size of the detail and the size of the collapse is the thing that makes people search for this at two in the morning.

**Where the word came from, honestly.** Popular speech, then television, then social media, in that order. It circulated in British dating slang, arrived on a widely watched dating program in the late 2010s, and then spread through short-form video, where a hashtag of the same name accumulated an enormous quantity of content. None of that is research and none of it was trying to be. It is a good coinage, which is why it spread, and a good coinage is a description rather than a discovery.

**What the research actually consists of.** One study, and I would rather show you its shape than pretend otherwise, because the confident tone of most pages on this term is doing you no favors.

Collisson, Saunders and Yin published a paper in 2025 in *Personality and Individual Differences* taking the term itself as its subject, and it is, as far as I can establish, the only peer-reviewed study that does. It has two parts. A pilot analysis of eighty-six videos posted under the hashtag, used to work out what people actually mean by the word. Then a survey of single adults, correlating self-reported experience of the ick against several existing trait questionnaires.

Now the parts that matter and that nearly every write-up of it dropped.

The final sample was 125 people. The authors had run a power analysis in advance and determined they needed 134. They fell short of their own target and said so, which is to their credit and is exactly why you should read it carefully rather than confidently. The sample was around ninety percent heterosexual. The design was correlational, meaning it can report that two things move together and cannot say that either causes the other. And the items measuring the ick were built for that study, so there is no validated instrument here and no second study checking whether the first one measured a stable thing.

**What it reported.** More women than men said they were familiar with the experience and reported it more often. Higher scores on a general disgust sensitivity questionnaire went with both the likelihood and the frequency of it. So did a measure of perfectionism directed at other people, which is the finding I would put the most weight on, because holding high standards for other people and being repelled by small failures in them are close to the same claim in different words.

There was also a self-report narcissism measure in the analysis, and it correlated with the likelihood of experiencing the ick. That result then traveled a long way from where it started. Headlines converted a modest correlation, in one small underpowered sample, with no follow-up, into a statement about what kind of person you are. It does not support that and the authors did not claim it did. If you read something in the last year that told you getting the ick means something about your character, that is where it came from and that is how much it weighs.

**What the term genuinely captures.** Three things, and it captures them well.

It captures the **speed**, which is the part that distinguishes this from ordinary cooling and which every reader recognizes instantly. It captures the **mismatch**, where the trigger is trivial and the effect is total, and naming that mismatch is a relief to people who thought they were the only one. And it captures the **involuntariness**, the fact that this happens to you rather than being done by you, which is the single most important thing anybody can tell you about it.

**What the term does not capture.** It names a **what** and says nothing whatsoever about a **why**. Two people can have the identical experience, at the same stage, triggered by details of the same size, for completely unrelated reasons, and the word covers both of them without distinguishing them at all.

Two further gaps deserve naming. The word arrives carrying a verdict about the person who has the reaction, which is why saying it out loud feels like a confession. And almost every use of it points outward, at what somebody else did to cause it, which is the entire genre of content on this subject and which quietly removes the only part you can actually work with.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Four situations, and none of them is the ordinary version this page is written for.

If the aversion has stopped being about people and has spread into eating, touch generally, or how you feel about your own body, and it has held across weeks rather than across one bad stretch. That is a different subject from the one here and it deserves proper attention from somebody qualified.

If the revulsion is attached to something that happened to you rather than to a detail about them. If a specific gesture, smell or phrase reliably produces a physical reaction that seems to belong to a different scene, that belongs with a professional who can work with it properly, and it will not be improved by anything written on a page.

If you are frightened of the person, or being controlled by them. Nothing on this page applies, and none of it is any use to you.

And if there is any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a licensed professional now rather than later, and everything else here can wait indefinitely.

For everybody else, and that is nearly everybody reading this, the rest applies.

## Are You Shallow, or Is That the Wrong Question?

It is the wrong question, and it is the first one nearly everybody asks.

Shallowness would require a choice. It describes somebody applying a standard, deliberately, and finding a person short of it. Nothing like that happened. There was no standard in the room and no moment where you consulted one. The reaction arrived complete, through a channel that does not check with your values on the way past, and by the time you had any access to it there was nothing left to decide.

That distinction does something more useful than letting you off, since it is the only thing on this page that makes any further work possible. You cannot do anything useful with a reflex you are treating as a moral failure. People who are busy prosecuting themselves are not observing anything, and observation is the entire method here.

What is worth saying plainly, since the guilt is doing a lot of damage on its own: you did not do anything to them. Something happened in you. The distance between those two sentences is where every useful move on this subject lives.

There is one honest caveat. If the trigger reliably tracks something like class, weight, income, an accent or a disability, that is worth looking at squarely rather than filing under mystery, because a reaction with a consistent demographic shape has a history behind it that is worth knowing about. That is a different investigation from the one this page runs, and it is a serious one.

## Why Does Knowing It Is Unfair Change Nothing?

Because the argument and the reaction are running in different systems, and only one of them is listening.

Disgust has a set of properties that make it unusual among emotions, and they are well documented. It is fast. It attaches to something concrete and physical rather than to an idea. It produces a characteristic facial movement and a set of bodily sensations that turn up across very different cultures, which is part of why it feels less like a mood than like a fact about the world. And it is unusually resistant to being talked out of.

That last property has been examined directly and the results are worth knowing.

There is a line of research in which people are asked to make a judgment about something they find repellent, and are then walked systematically through every reason they gave for it, with each reason shown to be inapplicable. Harm is ruled out. Consequences are ruled out. Consent is established. Participants agree, one by one, that each of their reasons has failed. And then they keep the judgment. Haidt and colleagues named the state people end up in, where somebody knows they can no longer defend a verdict and holds it anyway, saying some version of *I cannot explain it, I just know it is wrong.*

That work was done on moral judgments rather than on attraction, and I want to be exact about the extension I am making. The finding is that reasoning frequently arrives after a verdict rather than producing it, and functions as a lawyer for a decision already taken. Applying that to a person you cannot look at anymore is my inference from a well-supported general observation, not a measured result about romantic aversion.

But it explains something that otherwise makes no sense. You have not failed to argue yourself out of this. You have been arguing with something that never made an argument, and that has no obligation to answer one.

This is also why the week of listing their good qualities backfires so reliably. Every time you rehearse the case, you return your attention to the detail in order to defeat it, and attention is what a disgust response feeds on. People who try hardest to reason themselves out of this frequently report that the trigger became more vivid, not less.

## Why Does One Small Detail Contaminate the Whole Person?

Because disgust spreads, and the spreading has been studied for decades under a name that predates psychology.

The **law of contagion** was catalogued by Frazer, an anthropologist writing in the 1890s, as one of the principles underlying magical thinking across many cultures. It runs: once in contact, always in contact. Two things that have touched are treated as permanently connected, and properties transfer between them in a way that survives any amount of cleaning, distance or time.

Psychologists later found the same rule running quietly in ordinary adults with no magical beliefs of any kind. People decline to wear a laundered sweater that was previously worn by somebody they dislike. People will not drink from a glass that briefly held something harmless but repellent, even after it has been sterilized. The knowledge that nothing physical remains does not touch the reaction at all, which is the same unpersuadability described above, showing up in a different setting.

I am naming this as a described model rather than attributing it, because it was built by a long line of work rather than by a single finding, and because the honest position is that this research was done on food, on physical contaminants, and on objects. It was not built on romantic attraction. Applying it to a person is my extension of it, and it is a plausible extension rather than a demonstrated one.

With that caveat, it accounts for the part of this that people find most disturbing, which is the spread. The detail contaminates the hand that produced it. The hand contaminates the arm, then the voice, then the smell of them, then the restaurant you both liked, then the memory of the good evening three weeks ago, which now plays back with the ick already installed in it. Nothing about them changed. The contamination moved outward from one point along the ordinary route it always takes.

It also accounts for why the reaction does not respect proportion. There is no mechanism in this system for weighing the size of the trigger against the size of the response, because the system was built to move fast and to over-apply rather than to weigh anything, on the reasonable logic that being wrong in one direction costs you an evening and being wrong in the other direction costs you considerably more.

## What Is the Difference Between a Revulsion and a Judgment?

This distinction is the spine of the page and it is worth slowing down for, because almost every difficulty described so far comes from collapsing the two.

A **judgment** is reached. It has inputs, it has a process, it can be shown its own reasoning, and it can be changed by better information. When you decide somebody is unreliable because they have canceled four times, that is a judgment. It is revisable. New evidence acts on it.

A **revulsion** arrives. There are no inputs you have access to, there was no process, and the reasoning you produce afterward is a reconstruction rather than a record. It cannot be changed by better information because information had nothing to do with making it.

The reader of this page has almost always been treating a revulsion as a judgment. That is the source of the exhausting part. You keep taking the reaction to court, presenting evidence, winning every round, and finding the verdict unchanged, and then concluding that something is wrong with you because you cannot be persuaded by your own perfectly good argument.

Nothing is wrong with your reasoning. You have been prosecuting a case in front of a system that does not hear cases.

Two consequences follow, and they are the practical core of the page.

**First, the revulsion is not a verdict on the person, and it is not nothing either.** Both of the popular positions are wrong. The position that says trust your gut, it is telling you something, treats an unreasoning reaction as a reliable instrument, which it plainly is not, as anybody who has had this about somebody they later loved can confirm. The position that says it is superficial and you should ignore it treats a system that fires fast for reasons as pure noise, which it also is not. The accurate position is narrower and less satisfying. It is a signal of unknown origin and unknown reliability, and the origin is what you are actually trying to find.

**Second, and this is the part worth carrying away: the revulsion is involuntary and the following week is not.** You did not choose the drop behind your sternum. You do choose what you do on Tuesday, what you tell them, whether you go quiet, whether you manufacture a reason, whether you let a person you were fond of work out from your behavior that something has changed and never be told what. That week is the only part of this with any moral content in it at all, and it is the part almost nobody examines, because everybody is busy examining the second that they had no control over.

## Why Do Two People Get This Identically for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the reaction is the surface, and five very different things underneath it produce the same surface.

This is the part every other page on this subject skips, and it is the reason those pages cannot help you. A list of common triggers is a list of surfaces. Two people can both go off somebody over the way they held a fork, and one of them has simply stopped fancying that person while the other is running a reflex that has ended four relationships at exactly the same stage. Same fork. Nothing else in common.

What follows are five roots. They are not types of person and you are not one of them permanently. They are five different jobs a sudden revulsion can be doing, and the first one is the most common by a wide margin and the least discussed, so it comes first deliberately.

## What If You Have Simply Stopped Fancying Them?

Most of the time, this. It needs saying before anything else, because a reader who has just gone off somebody in the ordinary way should not be handed a mechanism.

Attraction ends. It has always ended, it ended before anybody had a word for it, and it usually ends without announcing itself in advance. What is new is that there is now a term available, and a large volume of content encouraging you to treat an ordinary ending as a psychological event with a hidden cause.

Here is what that looks like from inside. The attraction had already been thinning, quietly, over a couple of weeks, in a way you had not registered because there was nothing to register. Then a detail arrived and gave the ending a place to attach. The detail did not cause anything. It was the nearest available hook, and it got the whole weight of a process that had been running underneath for a while.

The tells for this root are straightforward.

It is happening with this person and it has not happened with others at the same stage. Looking back, you can find small signs of the interest thinning before the trigger, once you stop treating the trigger as the start of the story. There is no urgency to it, no panic, nothing frantic. And crucially, the reaction is not accompanied by a strong pull toward them at the same time. You are simply not interested, and the revulsion is a fairly mild passenger on a departure that was already happening.

If that is you, there is nothing here to fix and no root to find. You stopped fancying somebody. That is not a pattern, not a defect, and not a subject. The only thing worth adjusting is the conclusion you drew about yourself, and that can change in the time it takes to read this paragraph.

The thing to be careful of is the reverse error, which is using this root as cover. If you have concluded five times that you simply went off somebody, and all five were at roughly the same stage, that is no longer this root, and the repetition is the evidence.

## What If the Detail Was Carrying Real Information?

Sometimes the reaction is compressed information rather than nonsense, and the detail is standing in for something real that you had noticed and not permitted yourself to say.

This root is why the trivial-trigger framing can be actively misleading. A detail is only trivial if you look at the detail. Frequently it is a sample.

The way somebody speaks to a waiter is a sample of how they speak to people whose position is weaker than theirs. The particular way somebody tells a story about an ex is a sample of how you will be described in a year. A joke made in a certain register about somebody who is not in the room is a sample of a whole attitude, delivered in four seconds. In every case the detail is small and what it is drawn from is not, and the fast system got there considerably before the deliberate one did.

There is a reason this arrives as revulsion rather than as a thought. You had already collected the information, in fragments, over several weeks, and had declined to add it up, usually because adding it up would have required doing something about it. The fast system had no such reluctance. It reached the conclusion, could not express it in sentences, and expressed it in the only language it has, which is a physical no.

The tells for this root are distinctive.

The trigger, when you look at it honestly rather than at the version you told your friend, has a content to it. It was a behavior rather than a texture or a sound, and the behavior involved another person, or a value, or a moment where you saw how they treat somebody with nothing to offer them. There is often a specific memory attached that you have been not-thinking-about for a fortnight. And there is a peculiar quality of relief mixed into the aversion, which is the feeling of a thing finally being allowed to be true.

Care is needed here, in both directions. This root is real, and it is also the most flattering interpretation available, which makes it the one people reach for when they want their reaction to have been wise. The honest test is whether you can state the underlying observation in a sentence that has nothing to do with the trigger. If you can, and the sentence holds up when you are calm, there was information in it. If every attempt to state it collapses back into the detail, this is probably a different root.

## What If It Arrives Exactly as They Get Closer?

This is the version that repeats across relationships, and its signature is timing rather than content.

The trigger changes every time. The stage does not. Somewhere around the point where a thing stops being provisional, the revulsion arrives, and it arrives attached to whatever happened to be in the room. Three months, or the first time they said something unguarded, or the week after they introduced you to somebody who matters to them, or the evening they were plainly, visibly pleased to see you.

What makes this root distinct from the others is what it accomplishes. A revulsion is the most complete exit available to a human being, because it requires no explanation, produces no negotiation, and cannot be argued with by them or by you. A doubt invites a conversation. A reason invites a counter-reason. Physical aversion invites nothing at all. It ends the thing in a way that leaves nothing to discuss, and it does so without you ever having to decide anything or say anything you would have to defend.

That is what makes it so effective and so hard to catch. It does not feel like an exit. It feels like a discovery about them, arriving from outside, at an unlucky moment.

The tells are the clearest of any root on this page.

It has happened before, with people who had very little in common, at approximately the same distance in. The trigger differs every time, which is why you have never spotted the pattern, since you have been comparing triggers. It correlates with their interest rather than with their behavior, so it fires hardest with the people who were most straightforwardly pleased with you. And there is frequently a period of intensity immediately before it, which is the part that confuses people most.

I want to be precise about what this page owns here and what it does not, because two other patterns sit right next to this one and are different.

If closeness produces withdrawal in you without any aversion attached, if the liking stays completely intact and what arrives is a need for distance and a set of reasons to be busy, that is the exposure pattern and [the page on fear of intimacy](/patterns/fear-of-intimacy/) works it properly. The distinguishing feature is that no revulsion turns up at all.

If what collapses is the charge rather than the person, specifically at the moment something hypothetical becomes real, that is a different mechanism again and [the page on micro-cheating](/patterns/micro-cheating/) covers it, because the thing being protected there is the rehearsal rather than the distance.

What this page owns is the **sensory** version. The body issues the refusal, it attaches to a specific perceptible detail, and it happens in under a second. If your version has no physical drop in it, you are probably on the wrong page, and one of the two above will fit better.

## What If You Learned the Contempt Somewhere?

Some of these reactions are inherited, and the inheritance is specific enough to be traceable.

The shape is this. A particular kind of vulnerability was mocked where you could hear it. Enthusiasm that was too visible. Trying too hard at something. Being pleased about a small achievement. Neediness of any kind. Softness in a man, or bigness in a woman, or an accent, or an interest somebody in your household found embarrassing. Somebody you were close to made a face, or a noise, or a joke, and it happened often enough that the flinch got learned without the reason for it ever being stated.

The flinch survives the memory. That is the whole difficulty. You keep the reaction and lose the source, and thirty years later somebody in front of you displays exactly that quality and your body produces the response it was taught, and it presents itself to you as taste.

This is the root where the trigger is most likely to be something that would not bother most people at all, and where the reaction has an unmistakable flavor of contempt rather than of simple aversion. That flavor is the tell. Ordinary revulsion has a recoil in it. This one has a sneer in it, and the sneer usually belongs to somebody else.

The tells for this root.

The trigger clusters around one quality across very different people. Naming that quality out loud produces an immediate reaction in you, often a flush of something before you have thought about it. There is frequently a person you can identify who found that same quality contemptible, and identifying them takes about four seconds once the question is asked directly. And the aversion is often sharpest at exactly the quality you were most careful to suppress in yourself.

That last one deserves a sentence of its own, because it comes up a great deal. The thing you cannot stand to watch somebody else do freely is quite often the thing you were required to stop doing. What arrives as disgust at their enthusiasm has an older feeling underneath it that is not about them at all.

## What If the Disgust Is Doing the Work of a Decision?

The last root is the one with the most useful tell, and it is a tell about timing.

The reaction arrived on a Wednesday. Something else happened on the Sunday.

On the Sunday they said something about your work that landed slightly wrong, or they were unavailable when you needed something, or you noticed that you had been the one organizing everything for six weeks, or somebody asked how serious it was and you heard your own answer. Whatever it was, you decided at the time that it was too small to make anything of, or that raising it would be disproportionate, or that you were being oversensitive. So it was set down.

By Wednesday you cannot look at them.

The delay is the whole signature. A reaction that arrives days after its actual cause, attached to a detail that has nothing to do with the cause, is frequently carrying out a decision that was refused conscious consideration. The disgust does the job the conclusion would not do. It gets you out of something, or reduces your investment in it, without you ever having to name the thing you were unhappy about and without any risk of a conversation in which you might be argued out of it.

This is also why the trigger feels so specifically unfair. It is unfair. It is a stand-in. The actual material was three days earlier and was never allowed to become a sentence.

The tells for this root.

There is a gap of a day or more between something happening and the reaction arriving. The trigger has no relationship to the real material. And the reconstruction test works: if you write out the seventy-two hours before the reaction, in order, including everything you decided not to make anything of, something usually appears in the list that you had classified as nothing and had spent a surprising amount of energy classifying.

People who find this root often report a mixture of embarrassment and relief. Embarrassment because the actual grievance is small and would have been easy to raise. Relief because a reaction that felt insane turns out to have had a completely comprehensible cause, three days upstream.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, and What Separates Them?

Timing and repetition separate them. The trigger separates almost nothing, which is why the lists of common icks are useless for this purpose no matter how accurate they feel.

| Root | The question that finds it | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| The attraction was already ending | Was interest thinning before the trigger, once you stop treating the trigger as the start? | Mild, unpanicked, no strong pull toward them running at the same time |
| The detail carried real information | Can you state the underlying observation in a sentence with no reference to the trigger? | The sentence holds up when calm, and there is relief mixed into the aversion |
| It functions as an exit from closeness | Has this fired before, with very different people, at roughly the same distance in? | Tracks their interest rather than their behavior. Often intense just before |
| Contempt learned somewhere | Does the trigger cluster around one quality across different people? | The aversion has a sneer in it. You can name who found that quality contemptible |
| Disgust doing a decision's work | What happened one to three days before the reaction that you decided was nothing? | A delay, and a trigger with no relationship to the real material |

Two things about using this table.

More than one can be running. The commonest combination is the exit root with the decision root layered on top, where a small grievance supplies the occasion for a reflex that was going to fire eventually anyway. If two rows fit, take the one with the repetition in it, because repetition is the more informative signal.

And the first row is genuinely the most likely one. If you have had this once, with one person, and it does not match any of the other four tells, the honest reading is that you stopped fancying somebody. Going looking for a deeper cause in that situation is a way of making an ordinary event into a problem you can work on, which feels productive and is not.

## Why Can You Not Explain It to a Friend Without Sounding Absurd?

Because language only carries the trigger, and the trigger is the least informative part.

Here is the transmission problem in full. What happened to you had three components: a perceptible detail, a physical event of considerable force, and a speed that made the two feel like one thing. Speech carries the first one perfectly and the other two not at all. So you say the detail, and your friend receives a detail, and the detail is a man trotting up an escalator.

Your friend then does the only thing available, which is to supply the missing force from their own imagination, and their imagination has nothing to work with. So they conclude that the detail was the whole content, and that you ended something over a triviality, and they say the reasonable thing, which is: that seems a bit harsh. And they are being kind and they are entirely wrong, and you cannot correct them without describing an internal event that has no vocabulary.

This is worth understanding for two reasons.

The first is that it explains the isolation, which is one of the worst parts of this and which almost nobody mentions. A great many people have this and almost nobody says it, because saying it costs you something and buys you nothing. The silence measures how badly this survives being spoken rather than how rarely it happens.

The second is that it explains why you have not been able to think your own way through it either. You have been running the same transmission failure internally, describing the trigger to yourself, receiving the same thin account your friend received, and concluding the same thing they did, which is that you are being ridiculous.

The move that helps is to stop narrating the trigger and start narrating the timing. The detail is not the story. When it happened relative to everything else is the story, and that version survives being said out loud.

## What Do You Do With the Guilt?

Take it seriously as a signal that something has gone wrong in your account of events, rather than as a verdict.

The guilt is running on a premise, and the premise is that you did something. Look at the premise directly. Something happened in you, without your involvement, faster than you could have intervened even if you had known it was coming. Nobody has ever been able to prevent one of these in advance, because the first thing you know about it is that it has already happened.

You did not do anything to them. That sentence is worth reading twice, because the guilt depends entirely on its opposite.

What you can do something about starts afterward, and it is worth being specific about what that consists of, because this is the part with actual content in it.

You can be honest with yourself about what happened, rather than manufacturing a reason. This is where most of the real damage gets done. People with this reaction very rarely say what occurred. They construct a different, more presentable explanation, and then they act on the constructed one, and frequently they tell the other person the constructed one. That is a choice, it is made over days rather than in a second, and it is the part that would be worth doing differently.

You can decline to punish them for a reaction they did not cause. Going cold, becoming unavailable, letting somebody work out from your behavior that something has changed while telling them nothing, is a course of action. It happens slowly. It is chosen.

You can end things cleanly if you are going to end them, without a story that leaves them examining themselves for a fault they do not have.

And you can decline to make them into a bad person to justify the reaction, which is the most common thing people do here and the most quietly corrosive. The mind is uncomfortable holding both they did nothing wrong and I cannot be near them, so it resolves the discomfort by finding faults. Six weeks later you have a whole case assembled, all of it retrospective, none of it what actually happened.

None of that requires knowing which root is yours. All of it is available today.

## What Does the Settled Version of This Look Like?

Not the absence of the reaction. Something more modest and considerably more useful.

The reaction still happens. Nobody has any evidence that it can be removed, and a page promising you that is selling something. What changes is everything around it.

The gap between the reaction and your interpretation of it gets wider. Instead of ick, therefore this person is wrong for me, there is a pause, and inside the pause a question: what else was happening this week. That pause is the entire skill, and it is buildable.

The guilt drops away, because it was resting on a false premise and false premises do not survive being looked at repeatedly.

The week afterward gets handled differently. You stop constructing reasons. You either say a true thing or you say nothing, and if you end it you end it cleanly, and the other person is not left auditing themselves.

And the pattern becomes visible to you, if there is one. People who have watched four of these go past with the timing written down usually know their root without anybody telling them, because the repetition does the work once it is recorded rather than remembered.

What does not happen: it does not become a reliable instrument. You will not learn to read it accurately. It fired once about somebody you later loved and it will do that again, and the correct relationship to it is interested rather than obedient.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

It depends entirely on which root is yours, and confusing them is why people conclude that nothing works.

If the attraction was simply ending, no time at all, because nothing needs to change. The only thing worth altering is the conclusion you drew about your own character, and that goes as soon as you stop treating an ordinary event as a symptom.

If the detail was carrying information, this resolves quickly too, but in a different way. What takes the time is your willingness to say the underlying observation out loud, and people frequently find that once the sentence is said, the revulsion loses most of its force within days, because it was doing the job of the sentence.

If it functions as an exit, allow months, and expect the movement to be indirect. What changes is your tolerance for staying in something that has started to ask something of you, and that gets built in small places rather than in the largest one available. People commonly notice a year or so later that something fired and then simply passed, and cannot identify when that became possible.

If it is inherited contempt, this is the slowest of the five and it moves in stages. First you can name the quality. Then you can catch the reaction in progress. Then, considerably later, the reaction starts arriving quieter. Nobody removes it entirely, and the useful target is recognizing it as somebody else's rather than as your own taste.

If disgust has been doing decisions for you, this one can move fast, because the fix is upstream of the reaction. What changes is that small grievances get named at the time. People who start doing that report the sudden aversions thinning out noticeably within a couple of months, which makes sense, since the reaction was carrying something that now gets carried by a sentence.

One thing works immediately in every version, and it is in the next section.

## What Is Worth Doing This Week, Before You Decide Anything?

Nothing involving them. Two pieces of writing, both private, both cheap, and neither one commits you to anything.

**The seventy-two hours.** Reconstruct, in order, the three days before the reaction arrived. Not the reaction. The three days before it. Where you were, who you saw, what was said, what you were worrying about, how you slept. Write it plainly and do not edit for relevance, because relevance is exactly what you cannot judge yet.

Then go back through it and mark every moment you decided was nothing. Every small thing you chose not to raise. Every time you thought, that is not worth mentioning. Those marks are the whole point of the exercise. In a great many cases the real material is sitting in that list, having been classified as unimportant with more energy than an unimportant thing requires.

**The register.** List every previous time this has happened to you, going back as far as you can. For each one: who it was, how long in, what the trigger was, what else was going on that week, and whether it faded or held.

Then read the columns rather than the rows. The triggers column will look like nonsense, because it is a list of unrelated details and that is exactly what it should look like. The how-long-in column is the one to look at. If the numbers cluster, you have found something that no amount of thinking about escalators would ever have shown you. If they scatter, you probably do not have a pattern, and that is real information too.

Both take about an hour and neither requires you to talk to anybody or decide anything.
