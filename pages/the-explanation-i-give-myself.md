The second glass is poured before the sentence finishes forming, and the sentence is a good one.

It has been a long week. You are not driving. One more is not going to do anything, and the actual rule, the one you set in January and told exactly one person about, was really about weeknights, and this has been the sort of week that stops counting as a normal week somewhere around Wednesday.

Every clause in that is true. That is the part worth sitting with.

You did not construct it. It was there, complete, before the glass was full, and it arrived with the finished quality of something remembered rather than something built. There was no moment of assembly you could point to. There was the pour, and then there was a reason, and the reason has been available every single time this has happened, which is roughly once a week since about March.

Then, a few days later, you are describing your week to somebody and a version of it comes out of your mouth. And it is noticeably worse out here. It has a slight quality of being recited. The person you are talking to says nothing at all, or says something mild, and you find yourself adding a second clause you had not planned on adding, and then a third, and you can hear that the third one is doing repair work on the first two.

That is the whole subject of this page. Not the glass. The thing that arrived after it.

## What Does the Explanation Actually Sound Like From the Inside?

It sounds like remembering, which is why it is almost impossible to catch.

There is no experience of deciding on it. There is no menu of possible reasons, no weighing, no moment where an alternative account is considered and set aside. The explanation is simply present, in the way a fact about your own week is present, and it feels retrieved rather than made. That phenomenology matters enormously, because the thing that would let you interrogate it is the sense of having produced it, and you do not have that sense.

It is also specific to the occasion. This is worth noticing. The reason for tonight contains no general policy about drinking or spending or scrolling. What arrives is a reason about tonight, tailored to tonight's conditions, and it will be replaced next Thursday by a differently shaped reason equally well tailored to next Thursday. Somebody keeping a list would notice that the reasons do not agree with each other. Nobody keeps the list, because each reason is only ever in play for the length of one evening.

And it is proportionate. It never overreaches. This is the detail that most convinces people the explanation is trustworthy: it does not claim the behavior was good, or clever, or something you should do more of. It claims something far more modest, which is that this particular instance does not count for the purposes of the rule. A wild claim would be examined. A modest one is waved through.

Then there is the small physical thing that happens underneath all of it, which most people can locate if they look. A brief tightening somewhere in the chest or the stomach at the moment of the act, lasting a few seconds at most, gone by the time the reason arrives. That sequence is the whole mechanism in miniature, and the fact that the discomfort ends exactly when the explanation lands tells you what the explanation was for.

## What Is Cognitive Dissonance, and Why Does Almost Everyone Use the Term Wrong?

The name for this is **cognitive dissonance**, and it is one of the most famous ideas in psychology and one of the most consistently misused.

**What people think it means.** In ordinary speech the phrase now means something like holding two contradictory ideas at the same time. It gets used that way in newspapers, in arguments, and as a polite insult aimed at somebody whose views you think are inconsistent. On that reading, a person who believes in privacy and uses a smartphone has cognitive dissonance.

That is not what the term names, and the error is not a small one. Holding contradictory ideas is ordinary, constant, and largely comfortable. Everybody does it about almost everything, and it produces no pressure whatsoever most of the time.

**What it actually names.** Two things, in sequence.

The first is a state: the genuine discomfort produced when your own conduct and your own belief fail to line up. Not two abstract ideas in tension, but a thing you did sitting against something you hold about yourself. The theory's original claim was that this state is aversive, that it functions like a drive, and that people are motivated to reduce it the way they are motivated to reduce hunger.

The second is the prediction that follows, and it is the part popular usage loses completely. Given two elements that clash, a person will move whichever one is cheaper to move. That is the engine. And after the fact, the price difference is not close. The behavior has happened, is fixed, is on the record, and undoing it would cost a real loss. The belief is private, unwitnessed, and adjustable at no cost at all.

So the belief moves. Nearly every time, in nearly everybody, and the movement is experienced from the inside as a reason arriving.

**Where the model comes from.** It was set out in 1957 and tested in a 1959 experiment that is still the standard illustration. Participants were made to perform a genuinely dull task, then paid either a small amount or a large amount to tell the next person waiting that it had been interesting. The people paid the large amount went on reporting the task as dull. The people paid the small amount later said they had rather enjoyed it.

The account offered was this: a large payment is a complete external reason for saying something untrue, so nothing needs to change internally. A small payment is not enough of a reason, which leaves an act with no adequate justification attached, so the justification gets manufactured where it is cheapest, which is in the belief. I said it was interesting for almost nothing, therefore some part of me must have found it interesting.

I am naming the model here rather than attaching it to a person, and the rest of this section is the reason why. The finding belongs to a line of work rather than to a single authority, and the interpretation has been in dispute for most of the time since.

**What the term genuinely captures.** Three things, and it captures all three better than any other available word.

It captures the **direction of travel**, which is the single most useful fact on this page. Beliefs move toward behavior far more readily than behavior moves toward beliefs. Almost every attempt people make to fix a pattern assumes the opposite.

It captures the **automaticity**. The repair is not chosen, not noticed, and not available to be declined. Nothing about it feels like an act.

And it captures the **discomfort as the trigger**. Whatever is finally settled about the mechanism, the ordinary experience of a brief unpleasant sensation that stops the moment an account is available is real, common, and reportable by almost anybody who looks for it.

**What the term does not capture.** It names the **what** and says nothing about the **why**. The term describes a repair process that is universal, which means it describes something that is running identically in two people whose situations have nothing in common. One of them has a rule that never suited them and is being told so. Another is defending a sentence about their own character. A third is covering a cost that is landing on somebody else. The word covers all three without distinguishing between them, and the distinguishing is the only part that leads anywhere.

There is a second gap. The term arrives with a faint accusation built into it, because in ordinary use it is something you point at in other people. That makes it nearly useless as a tool for looking at yourself, since the first thing it does is make you defensive, which triggers the very repair you were trying to observe.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

A few conditions, and they are about the behavior underneath rather than about the explaining.

If the thing being explained is damaging your body, your finances, or somebody who depends on you, and the explanation has been holding that line steady for months, the explaining has stopped being the interesting part. If you have tried to stop for a few days and could not. If somebody close to you has said directly that this is serious, and an explanation arrived to cover that conversation too. If the behavior involves anything you would be frightened to write down.

And if there is any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a licensed professional now rather than later, ahead of any page about mechanisms.

## How Good Is the Evidence Here, Honestly?

Better than most things in this field for the effect, and considerably worse than you have been told for the mechanism. The distinction is the whole answer and almost nothing written about this term makes it.

**The effect is robust.** Across a large number of experimental designs, in several countries, in young children, and in non-human primates, a reliable pattern appears: after a person has done something, their stated attitude shifts toward what they did. That much is about as well established as findings in this area get, and it survived the period when a great many famous results did not.

**The founding studies were tiny.** The 1959 experiment recruited seventy-one people, discarded eleven for procedural reasons, and analyzed sixty. Twenty per condition. All male undergraduates at a single American university. That was unremarkable practice at the time and is a fragile basis by current standards, and it matters because the interpretive weight placed on that one study over the following decades was enormous.

**The standard measurement tool has taken a direct hit.** For roughly fifty years the workhorse design was the free-choice paradigm: rate a set of items, choose between two you rated equally, rate them again. The chosen item goes up, the rejected one goes down, and the gap was read as preference change produced by the act of choosing. In 2010, Chen and Risen argued that the design cannot support that reading. Choosing between two items you rated identically is not choosing between two things you value identically, because a rating scale is coarse and your actual preference is finer than the scale can record. The choice reveals the finer preference. The second rating then picks up something that was already there. On their analysis, part of a fifty-year literature is an artifact of how the choices were assigned rather than evidence of anything changing.

**The evidence that looked strongest is exactly what the critique lands on.** In 2007 Egan, Santos and Bloom reported the same spread in four-year-old children and in capuchin monkeys, which was taken as strong evidence that the process is early, basic, and not a product of adult self-presentation. That study is among the clearest targets of the artifact argument, since children and monkeys have preferences finer than any rating a researcher can extract from them. The original authors ran a later version using choices the subjects could not have based on a known preference, which is the right response and does not close the question.

**Attempts to answer the critique have their own problems.** Work by Sharot, Velasquez and Dolan reported preference change following choices participants were not aware they had made, offered as a design the artifact objection cannot reach. That result has itself been questioned on related statistical grounds. This is what a live argument looks like from the inside, and presenting any single study in it as the last word would be misleading.

**The main rival account has never been eliminated.** In 1967 an alternative was proposed that requires no discomfort at all. On that account, people have poor access to their own attitudes and work them out the same way an observer would: by watching their own behavior and the circumstances around it, then inferring what they must think. Told the untrue thing for almost nothing, an outside observer would conclude the speaker probably meant some of it. So does the speaker. No drive, no aversive state, no repair. Decades of experiments were designed specifically to separate the two accounts, and the outcome was a partial truce rather than a verdict: dissonance appearing to fit better when a behavior sits far from your existing position, self-perception fitting better when it sits close. A truce is not a resolution, and this one has held mostly because the field moved on.

**There is a modern defense worth knowing about.** A later line of work reframes the whole thing around action: the point of resolving a contradiction is not comfort but the ability to keep acting effectively, since a person paralyzed by every inconsistency would do nothing. That account brings physiological and neural evidence that something measurable is happening, which the pure inference story has more trouble with. It is a serious position. It is also one position among several, and I am describing it rather than naming it, because it is a body of work rather than a single decisive result.

**Where that leaves you.** The thing this page describes happens, you can observe it in yourself this week, and the observation does not depend on which account is correct. What you should not do is repeat the confident version you will find almost everywhere else, in which a settled mechanism was proved in 1959 and everything since has been elaboration. That version is tidier than the literature and it is not what the literature says.

**And one more distortion, which is specific to this term.** Popular usage has drifted so far from the technical meaning that a study using the phrase and an internet post using the phrase are frequently describing different things entirely. The study means the repair. The post usually means an inconsistency somebody has spotted in an opponent. Anybody trying to check what is known about this by reading widely will find those two meanings interleaved with no warning that they have nothing to do with each other.

## Why Does the Belief Move Instead of the Behavior?

Because of when the repair runs, and this is the sentence to keep if you keep only one.

It runs afterward. The pressure does not exist before the act, because before the act there is no contradiction to resolve. It appears in the moment the behavior becomes a fact, and at that moment the two things in tension are in radically different conditions. One is finished. The other is not.

Consider what would actually be involved in moving the behavior instead. You would have to undo something that already happened, which is impossible, so in practice moving the behavior means accepting the contradiction and carrying it: I hold this rule, I broke it, both remain true, and the discomfort stays. Nothing about that is unavailable to a person. It is just expensive, and it has to be paid every time, and the alternative is free.

The belief is not free to move in some abstract sense either. What makes it cheap is that nobody witnesses it. No announcement is required. The rule as you now hold it and the rule as you held it last month are both stored in the same place, and one has simply been overwritten by the other, with no notification and no record of the previous version.

This is also why the argument you have with yourself the next morning goes nowhere. By morning there is no contradiction left to argue about. The repair completed hours ago and what you are examining is the repaired version, which is internally consistent and reads as reasonable, because consistency is the product the repair exists to deliver.

## Why Does It Sound Thinner When You Say It Out Loud?

Because inside your head it has never once had to survive an audience that was not already on its side.

An explanation held privately gets tested against nothing. It arrives, it fits, the discomfort stops, and that is the entire quality-control process. There is no version of you standing slightly back and asking whether the third clause is doing work the first two failed to do. The listener and the speaker are the same person and that person wanted the matter closed.

Said out loud, three things happen at once that never happen internally.

It has to be sequential. Inside, the reason exists all at once, as a kind of atmosphere. Out loud it has to be laid end to end in a specific order, and the order exposes which parts are load-carrying and which are decoration. People frequently hear themselves reach a clause and know, mid-sentence, that they are about to skip it.

It arrives in front of somebody who has the earlier version. If you told this person about the rule when you set it, they are holding both, and you can feel them holding both even if they say nothing at all. The gap becomes audible to you before they respond.

And it takes on the specific quality of being recited, which is the tell people report most often. That quality is information. It means the account was already finished before the conversation started, and a genuine reason produced in the moment does not sound like that.

None of this makes the explanation false. Nothing about it changed on the way out of your mouth. What changed is that its only support, an audience that agreed with every step, was removed, and it turns out that support was carrying more than you thought.

## Is It a Lie?

No, and getting this wrong will cost you the whole page.

The material is true. It genuinely was a long week. You genuinely were not driving. One of them genuinely does not matter very much on its own. If any of those were false the explanation would not work, because you would catch it, and the whole point of the repair is that it passes inspection by the only inspector present.

What is inaccurate is smaller and much harder to see. The explanation makes a claim about causation that it never states out loud. It presents itself as the reason the thing happened. It is actually a set of true facts that were available in the environment, selected afterward because they fit, and assembled into a shape that resolves a contradiction. The facts are real. The causal claim is retrofitted, and the retrofitting is invisible because nothing false was used to do it.

This distinction is the reason the standard advice does nothing. Being told to stop lying to yourself lands on somebody who is not lying, and the instruction bounces, correctly. You examine the explanation, find it accurate, and conclude that this particular framework does not apply to you. It applies exactly as much as it did before, because accuracy was never the issue.

There is a related trap worth naming, since the word tends to invite it. The repair is not a defect in you. Anybody without it would be intolerable to live inside, spending their days accumulating unresolved contradictions with no way to close any of them. What you are looking at is a normal function running normally. The question worth asking is what it has been quietly buying, not whether you should have it.

## Why Can You Never Catch the Standard Moving?

Because the movement is sized precisely so that it cannot be caught, and the sizing looks deliberate.

It is never a reversal. Nobody goes from I do not do this to I do this. The adjustment is closer to a notch, roughly the amount required to accommodate one instance and no more. Weeknights becomes weeknights that were normal. Nothing after eleven becomes nothing after eleven unless something is actually happening. Each edit is small enough to look like a clarification of what the rule always meant, and clarifications do not trigger any alarm, because refining a rule is what a thoughtful person does.

There is a second reason, and it is structural. The rule lives in the same place that does the repairing. When the repair runs, it edits the rule in the same pass, so at no point do you hold both versions simultaneously. You cannot compare a thing to itself. By the time you go looking, only the current version exists, and it feels like the version you always had.

The only way anyone ever sees it is by comparison against something outside their own head. A note written years ago. Somebody quoting you back to yourself. A photograph of a page. That is why people discover this in a specific and consistent way: they find something they wrote, read it, and have a sharp physical reaction to how far it is from where they now are, with no memory of any point at which they agreed to move.

Nothing about that is a failure of attention. There was no moment to pay attention to. The whole thing happened between the pours.

## Why Do Two People Produce the Same Sentence and Need Opposite Things?

Because the sentence is the surface, and five quite different situations produce it in a form you could not tell apart from a transcript.

This is where the page stops being about a mechanism and starts being about you specifically. The mechanism is universal, which means it explains nothing about your case. Everybody runs the repair. What differs is what the repair is doing, and there are at least five answers, one of which means you should keep the behavior and change the rule.

Read all five. Most people recognize themselves in one strongly and a second faintly, and the faint one is often the more useful of the two.

## What If This Is Just the Ordinary Repair Everybody Runs?

Then very little is wrong, and this deserves naming first and generously, because it is the most likely answer and the one nobody writes.

Every person alive does this, continuously, about a great many things. The capacity to reconcile what you did with what you hold is standard equipment and it is doing far more good than harm. It lets people act without being paralyzed by the gap between their conduct and their values, and that gap is permanent, universal, and not going anywhere. Somebody who felt the full contradiction every time would get very little done and would not enjoy any of it.

If you have arrived here because you noticed yourself producing an explanation and it disturbed you, the honest reading is that your attention is unusually good and nothing much is wrong. The ordinary condition is running this repair several times a day for decades and never once observing it. Noticing puts you ahead of most people.

**The tell for this root.** The behavior itself is genuinely minor, the cost is genuinely absorbed by you alone, and if you subtract the explaining, what is left does not amount to a problem. The distress is coming from having seen the machinery rather than from anything the machinery has done.

**What this root needs.** Substantially nothing. Possibly a written rule, out of curiosity rather than concern, on the one behavior you are least sure about. If it holds, you have your answer and can stop reading about this.

The reason to name this root first is that the other four are only worth investigating once this one has been ruled out, and a page that opens with the expensive versions will convince people they have a problem they do not have.

## What If the Rule Was Wrong for You in the First Place?

Then the belief moving is the correct outcome, and the repair has been telling you something accurate that you have been treating as a moral failure.

This root is the one almost nobody names, and it is the most useful thing on this page. A rule can be broken repeatedly for the excellent reason that it was never yours. Rules get adopted in a burst of resolve on a bad Sunday. They get absorbed from someone whose circumstances look nothing like yours. They get inherited from a house you moved out of fifteen years ago, along with a great deal else that was never examined on the way out. They get set by a version of you who had different work, different sleep, and a different body.

A rule with no owner behaves in a distinctive way: it keeps getting broken, the explanation that follows is unusually good, and the standard drifts steadily in one direction with nothing ever restoring it. None of that describes a person failing at discipline. What it describes is a rule being quietly voted down over a period of years by the only person who ever had a vote.

**The test, and it takes a minute.** Write the rule in one sentence. Then write why it exists in one sentence, without using the word should, and without naming any person other than yourself.

A rule that belongs to you produces a reason connected to something you actually want. I stop at two because three costs me the following morning and the following morning is when I do the work I care about. That reason will survive being said out loud to a stranger.

A rule that does not belong to you produces one of three things. A shrug. A phrase in somebody else's voice, which you will recognize immediately if you listen for whose voice it is. Or a general appeal to what a sensible person does, which is a rule with no argument attached to it.

**What this root needs.** A decision, made deliberately and while calm, about whether to keep the rule or retire it. Both are legitimate. What is costing you is the third option you have been running instead, where the rule stays nominally in force, gets broken weekly, and generates a small pulse of self-reproach each time that goes nowhere and accomplishes nothing.

Retiring a rule properly means saying what replaces it, since a vacancy tends to fill itself. And it means noticing that the relief you feel at the possibility of retiring it is itself evidence about whose rule it was.

## What If What You Are Defending Is a Sentence About Yourself?

Then the behavior was never the point, and this explains the thing that most puzzles people: why the explanation is so disproportionate to what happened.

Some rules are attached to a description you rely on. I am someone who does not do this. I am not the sort of person who lets that slide. I am reliable about exactly this one thing. The description does real work. It may be one of a small number of things you are confident about, and in some cases it is holding up an area where the evidence is otherwise mixed.

When the behavior contradicts the description, what takes the hit is the description, and the description is much more expensive than the evening. So the repair works far harder than the event warrants. This is why people find themselves producing an elaborate account of something that objectively barely happened, and why the intensity feels strange even at the time.

**The tell.** Disproportion, and a particular kind of it. The explanation is longer, more urgent, and more thorough than the act could possibly justify. There is often a second stage where you rehearse it, hours later, against an objection nobody made. And the effort scales with how central the description is rather than with how bad the behavior was, which is why small acts sometimes generate enormous repairs and larger ones sometimes generate none.

A second tell is the shape of the explanation. It tends to be about who you are rather than about what happened. Not I did this because the week was bad, but this is not the kind of thing I do, therefore this instance must be a special case, therefore here is the special case. The special case is generated to protect the sentence.

**What this root needs.** The uncomfortable question is what the description is holding up. A sentence about yourself that has to be defended this hard is usually load-carrying somewhere else, and finding out where is a different job from managing the behavior. [The related pattern where a single event gets converted into a verdict about your whole character](/patterns/why-did-i-do-something-so-out-of-character/) runs on the same machinery pointed the opposite way, and is worth reading if the sentence you are defending is one you half suspect is already false.

## What If Somebody Else Is Paying for This?

Then the repair has become expensive, and this needs saying plainly with no moralizing attached, because moralizing is the fastest way to make somebody stop reading a section they need.

When the cost of the behavior lands on you alone, the explanation only has to satisfy you, and it does. When some of the cost lands on another person, the explanation has to cover their experience too, and it cannot, because you do not have access to their experience. So it makes something up, and the manufactured part is always in the same place.

**The structural tell, and it is reliable.** An explanation covering a cost that falls on somebody else will contain a claim about that person's inner state. They did not mind. They would have said something. They were fine about it. It was not a big deal to them. Whichever version arrives, that clause is doing the heaviest work in the whole account and it is the only part with no evidence underneath it at all.

Sometimes the claim is correct. Often it is a guess made by somebody who needed a particular answer, and the guess is systematically biased in the direction that resolves the contradiction. Where the other person has reason not to say anything, because saying something has been costly before, the guess is even less reliable and more confident.

There is a further move that shows up at this root and nowhere else. The explanation begins to include an account of the other person that makes the cost more acceptable, and that account gets slightly worse over time. They are difficult about things like this anyway. They overreact. That drift is worth watching for, because it is a second standard moving, and it does its moving in the same quiet way as the first.

**What this root needs.** One question, asked directly, of the person paying. Not an apology and not a confession, both of which are about your discomfort. A question about their experience, asked in a way that makes the honest answer available, and then held without being explained at. What comes back either dissolves the whole thing or gives you something specific to work with, and either result is better than continuing to guess on your own behalf.

## What If the Explanation Now Arrives Before You Do the Thing?

Then the decision has already been removed, and this is the version worth catching, because it is the one that stops feeling like a pattern at all.

A repair run often enough stops waiting to be needed. Once the same account has closed the same contradiction thirty or forty times, it stops being produced in response to anything and becomes simply available, sitting in reach. And an account that is already available will present itself while the decision is notionally still open.

The sequence inverts. There is no longer an act followed by a reason. There is a reason, arriving first, in the form of a small, familiar, entirely reasonable thought, and then the act, which now feels less like a choice than like a conclusion. The moment does not present itself as a decision at all, because by the time you reach it, everything a decision would have to weigh has already been settled.

**The tell.** You cannot locate the moment you chose. People at this root describe the evening in a way that has no decision point in it. It was already happening. The account of it is smooth, the transitions are missing, and the question of when exactly you decided produces a genuine blank rather than an evasion.

A second tell is that the explanation has stopped varying. At the earlier roots, each occasion generates a tailored reason. Here there is one standard reason, applied identically, and it has stopped feeling like a reason and started feeling like a fact about your life.

**What this root needs.** Something outside the sequence, because nothing inside it is available to be interrupted. There is no moment left to catch, which is what makes advice about catching yourself in the moment useless at this root specifically. What is left is the written rule, set on a day when none of this is live, which is the subject of the last part of this page.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, Using Only What You Already Know?

You have the material for this already. It requires no new observation, only sorting what you can recall.

| Root | The question that finds it | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| The ordinary repair | If you subtract the explaining, is what remains actually a problem? | The behavior is minor, the cost is yours alone, and the distress came from noticing the machinery |
| The rule was never yours | State why the rule exists in one sentence, without the word should | A shrug, somebody else's voice, or an appeal to what sensible people do. Relief at the idea of retiring it |
| A sentence about yourself | Is the explanation bigger than the act could justify? | Rehearsing it hours later against an objection nobody made. The account is about who you are, not what happened |
| Somebody else is paying | Does the explanation contain a claim about another person's state of mind? | They did not mind. They would have said. That clause is carrying the most and has the least under it |
| The repair runs first | Can you name the moment you decided? | A genuine blank rather than an evasion, and one standard reason that has stopped varying |

Two notes on reading this. Most people land on one strongly and one faintly, and the faint one is frequently the more useful, because the strong one is often the version that is easiest to admit. And the fifth root can sit underneath any of the other four, since it describes how long a repair has been running rather than what it is for.

## Why Does Deciding to Be More Honest With Yourself Fail?

Because it aims at the wrong moment and asks for a thing that is not missing.

Honesty is not the deficit. The explanation is made of true material, which is the entire reason it works, so an instruction to be more honest arrives at a door that is already open and finds nothing to do. People run this instruction for a while, examine their explanations, find them accurate, and reasonably conclude the framework does not describe them.

The second problem is that a resolution to be more honest is made of exactly the same substance as every other resolution, which means the repair can process it like anything else. The first time honesty is inconvenient, an account will arrive explaining why this particular case does not require it, and the account will be good, because they always are.

The third problem is timing. Every instruction of this kind points at the moment of the behavior, and at that moment there is nothing available to work with. The pressure has not arrived yet, so there is nothing to notice, and by the time there is, the repair has already completed. Aiming at the one part of the sequence you have no access to is why so much effort here produces so little.

Notice what is being ruled out. Watching for your own rationalizations, which fails for the reasons above. Announcing intentions to somebody so they will check on you, which turns a private rule into a performance and produces explanations aimed at them instead of at you. And any general resolution to be more truthful, which is a rule with no number in it, and rules with no numbers are the most movable objects on this page.

## What Is the Only Part of This You Actually Choose?

Writing the rule down, in specific words, before the next occasion. That is the whole list.

Everything else described here happens to you. The pressure arrives on its own. The explanation arrives on its own, complete and unrequested. The standard adjusts without consultation. None of those are available to be decided about, and treating them as though they are is what makes people feel they are failing at something.

One thing is chosen, and it is chosen on an ordinary afternoon when nothing at all is happening, which is exactly why it never feels urgent enough to do.

**Why written and not remembered.** A remembered rule is stored in the same place that performs the repair, so it gets edited in the same pass, and you never hold both versions at once. A written rule has a version from before. It sits outside the system that would otherwise revise it, and it cannot be quietly reinterpreted by somebody who wants it to say something else this evening, because the original wording is right there. The comparison that is permanently unavailable inside your head takes about seven seconds on paper.

**What it has to contain.** A number, a time, a place, or a condition. Nothing evaluative.

Two, and the third is where I stop. Not: I will drink less.

Nothing after eleven. Not: I will be sensible about my phone.

No orders on days I have not eaten properly. Not: I will stop shopping when I am upset.

Evaluative words are the mechanism by which a standard moves without appearing to. Less, sensible, reasonable, moderate, and properly all mean whatever the evening requires them to mean, and they can be reinterpreted with no sensation of anything having changed. A number cannot. That is the entire reason for the number, and it is why the rule should feel slightly too rigid when you write it.

**What to do with it.** Put it where you will encounter it without looking for it. Then leave it alone. There is no tracking system here and nothing at all to record. Its whole function is to still say what it said.

**What happens when you break it.** You get a fact instead of a fog, and that is the point rather than a failure of the method. This rule, these conditions, this many times, with the original wording still visible. That is material an actual answer can be built from, and it is the first time you will have had any. A rule that was quietly rewritten leaves nothing behind at all.

Expect the first month to mostly produce information rather than change. The information is the deliverable.

## What Does the Settled Version Look Like on an Ordinary Thursday?

Quieter than people expect, and the explanations have not stopped.

The reason still arrives. That does not change, and anybody promising otherwise is selling something. What changes is what happens in the few seconds after it arrives. There is a small recognition, without drama attached, that this is the familiar one. Not a confrontation, not a moment of honesty, just the ordinary recognition of something you have met before.

The rule is on paper somewhere and it says what it said in March. Sometimes you have kept it and sometimes you have not, and both are ordinary. The difference is that you know which, and you know roughly how often, because the wording has not moved underneath you.

On one or two things, the rule got retired deliberately, after the one-minute test produced a shrug and somebody else's voice. Nothing has gone wrong as a result. The small weekly pulse of self-reproach attached to that rule is gone, and what it was costing only became clear once it stopped.

Where somebody else was paying, you asked. The answer was less bad than the guess in one case and considerably worse in another, and both were useful, and neither could have been arrived at from inside your own account.

And the standard sits still. That is the thing you notice a year out, reading something you wrote. It says what you remember it saying. There is no jolt.

## How Long Does This Take to Change, Honestly?

Longer than the reading of it suggests, and with a shape worth knowing in advance.

The explanations never stop, and building an attempt around expecting them to is the most common way this goes wrong. A single written rule, on a behavior that actually matters to you, tends to produce something visible within a fortnight, and what it produces is usually information rather than improvement.

The one-minute test on whether a rule is yours can be run this afternoon and is often the fastest useful thing here, because retiring a rule that was never yours removes a whole category of this at once.

A standard that has been drifting for years takes considerably longer to hold still, and the honest range is months. The fifth root, where the explanation runs ahead of the behavior, takes the longest, because there is no moment left to work with and the whole thing has to be rebuilt from outside the sequence.

What does not take long is finding out which root you are in. Most people can do that from what they already remember, in one sitting, and that is genuinely the hard part of the work rather than the easy part.

## What Is Worth Doing This Week, Before You Decide Anything?

Two things, both private, neither of which commits you to changing any behavior at all.

Read the second sentence back and listen for whose voice it is in. If it is yours and it connects to something you actually want, the rule is real and worth keeping. If it produces a shrug or a borrowed phrase, you have learned that you have been failing at somebody else's rule for a period of years, which is a different problem with a much shorter solution.

**One written rule, with a number in it.** On whichever behavior you would most like to know the truth about. Specific enough that no reading of it can stretch. Put it where you will meet it by accident.
