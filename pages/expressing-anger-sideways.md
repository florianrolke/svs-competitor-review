# Why do I express anger sideways instead of just saying what is bothering me?

<div class="answer-box">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>
Because the anger has found a route that never requires you to say you are angry. That route was available early, it worked, and it has not needed a decision since. Most of the time the sideways move fires before you have located the feeling, which is why being asked what is wrong produces a genuine blank rather than a refusal to answer.

The shed door has needed a new hinge since March.

You were asked in March. You said yes in March, and you meant it in March. It is now September. In between there have been nine weekends where you were in the garden anyway, four trips to a hardware store for other things, and one afternoon where you looked directly at the hinge and then went inside to answer an email that could have waited a week.

If anybody asks, you have a full and accurate account. You have been busy. The right screws were not in stock. It has been raining more than usual. Every one of those is true. Not one of them is the reason.

The reason is that in March, about two seconds before you said yes, something in you objected to the way the request was made, and there was no version of the moment in which that objection could be said out loud. So it went into the schedule instead. It has been sitting in the schedule for six months, doing exactly the job it was hired to do, which is to register an objection while remaining permanently deniable.

Once you can see it in the shed door you start finding it everywhere.

There is a message you left for three days, and you had your phone in your hand at least forty times during those three days. There is a compliment you paid somebody at a birthday dinner, in front of four people, which had a small clean hook set into the end of it, and you heard the hook about half a second after they did, and their face changed and yours did not. There is a pharmacy pickup you forgot, and a work errand you forgot, and both of them were things you had said yes to in a tone that made the yes sound easy.

There is the reply you sent to a work email, three lines long, technically an agreement, with every warm word removed from it and nothing quotable added. There is the calendar invite you accepted and then arrived to eleven minutes late for.

And there is the moment that started you looking. Somebody who knows you well said, without much heat, that you had been off with them all week. And you went through the week in your head and found nothing. You had answered every question. You had not raised your voice once. You had done the thing they asked. From where you were standing you had been reasonable all week, and the accusation felt like an accusation, so you said you did not know what they were talking about, and you were not lying.

## What Does This Actually Look Like Across an Ordinary Week?

Much smaller and much more distributed than the phrase suggests, which is why it survives inspection.

It looks like eleven minutes late to something you did not want to attend, with a genuine traffic explanation attached. It looks like a one word answer where a three word answer would have been normal. It looks like agreeing to a plan in the exact number of words required to agree to it.

It looks like the thumbs up you sent instead of a sentence, and the fact that you knew, while sending it, that it would land as a small cold object.

It looks like a task on a shared task list that you completed to the letter of the request and no further, and then waited to see whether anybody would notice that the obvious extra step had been left undone.

It looks like helping with the washing up while making the washing up look expensive.

It looks like a wet towel left on the bed of a person who has asked you not to leave wet towels on the bed, on a day when you were annoyed about something entirely unrelated, and you could not have told anybody at the time that the two facts were connected.

It looks like a story told at dinner, funny, well shaped, at somebody's expense, where the person at whose expense it is told laughs along and something in the room gets slightly colder for the rest of the evening.

Twelve of those in a week produces a week that looks completely normal from the inside, and a week in which somebody living with you comes away with the accurate impression that they have done something wrong and no ability to say what.

## What Is the Name for This, and How Much of It Is Real?

The name is **passive aggressive behavior**, and it is worth a great deal less than the confidence with which people use it.

Start with what it accurately names, because it does name something. The behavior is the expression of dissatisfaction through action, timing, or tone rather than through a statement. The dissatisfaction reaches the other person. It arrives with nothing attached that could be quoted, challenged, or discussed. Those two properties together are the entire phenomenon, and they are what make it durable, because a message that lands and cannot be raised is a message that never has to be defended.

**Where the phrase came from, and what happened to it.** It began life as clinical shorthand. The words were coined in the 1940s, in a military setting, to describe people who followed an instruction and then carried it out in a way that made the compliance worthless. For several decades afterward it existed as a formal clinical category, with a written description attached to it. Then it was taken out of the main list of such categories, because it did not survive examination. The objections at the time were straightforward. It described a single behavior rather than a broad and stable pattern. It could not be separated cleanly from a reasonable response to a genuinely constraining situation. And the people applying it did not reliably agree with one another about who had it.

I am deliberately not naming what was removed or where it was removed from. Naming it would invite you to go and look up a description that no longer describes anything, and this page has no business handing you a category to apply to yourself. The fact that matters is simpler and it is the one almost nobody tells you: what survives is a plain description of a behavior. Every competing article on this phrase treats it as a settled clinical thing with a fixed technical meaning. It has not been one for a long time.

**Better vocabulary, in three pieces.**

The first is the distinction between **indirect and direct expression of dissatisfaction**. Direct means the dissatisfaction is stated, which makes it available to be answered, argued with, or settled. Indirect means it is carried in behavior, timing, or tone, which means it registers without ever becoming a topic. Both are expression. Only one of them opens a conversation. That distinction does the same descriptive work as the popular phrase and carries none of its character judgment, which makes it far more usable when the person you are describing is yourself.

The second is the observation that sits underneath everything else here. The move reliably communicates, and it reliably prevents any conversation about what it communicated. Those are usually treated as two separate facts. They are one fact, and they are the reason the behavior is so stable. A move that failed to communicate would be abandoned as useless. A move that communicated and then produced a conversation would be answered, and either resolved or overruled. This move does neither. It delivers the message and removes every handle somebody could grip to open it. Nothing about that requires cunning. It requires only that a route be available which happens to have both properties, and this one does.

The third is the **demand-withdraw pattern**, and it is worth naming because you have almost certainly never seen your half of it described as half of something. One person presses for a discussion, a decision, or a change. The other moves away from it, goes quiet, gets busy, agrees in order to end the exchange, or leaves the room. The pressing reads as pressure, so the withdrawal grows. The withdrawal reads as indifference, so the pressing grows. Neither person built the loop and both of them are making a sensible response to the move in front of them.

If your anger comes out sideways, you are usually the withdrawing half. That is worth sitting with for a moment, because the single most useful finding attached to this pattern removes almost all of the character reading people apply to themselves afterward. Eldridge and colleagues reported in 2007, comparing couples at different levels of distress, that the pattern was more frequent and considerably more rigid where distress was higher, and that the roles tracked who wanted the change under discussion rather than who had which temperament. Change the topic to something you want changed and the roles frequently swap over with it. You are not a withdrawer. You are the person who does not want the change currently being asked for, and the withdrawing is what that looks like from outside.

Where all three of these names stop is the same place. They describe the shape of the mechanism with real precision. Not one of them tells you why the route was built, why it is still running, or what would have to be true for it to stop. The vocabulary is the what. Nobody has ever found a root by looking one up.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Four situations, and none of them are unusual.

If the anger frightens you, or frightens somebody who lives with you, then the size of it is the thing to attend to first, and decoding a pattern is the wrong tool for that job. It is work for somebody qualified, and it is ordinary work that people do all the time.

If the intensity does not match anything you can point at, so that a two second delay produces a response that would be proportionate to a betrayal, then something older is being reactivated and the mechanism described here will not reach it.

If somebody raising their voice puts your body into a state that takes hours to come down from, that is worth taking to a licensed professional rather than working on alone, whatever else is also true.

And if there is any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a professional now rather than later, and everything else on this page can wait indefinitely.

## What If Saying It Directly Would Actually Cost You Something?

This root goes first, because a page that skips it will do damage to the people who need it most.

There are situations where the indirect route is the only route, and taking it is an accurate reading of your circumstances rather than a defect in your character. A manager who can end your income and who has already shown you what happens to people who push back. A landlord. An immigration process. A family member whose reaction to being contradicted has been dangerous in the past and might be again. A workplace where the person who raises the issue becomes the issue, which is a real and common structure and not a paranoid reading of one.

In every one of those, direct expression of dissatisfaction carries a genuine and asymmetric cost, and the indirect version carries almost none. Choosing the cheaper one is competent. If you are reading this from inside a situation like that, nothing on this page is asking you to hand somebody a stick to hit you with, and any article that tells you to communicate openly regardless of consequences is written by somebody who has not thought about who is reading.

The question worth asking is narrower, and it is the only one that separates a competent response from a stuck one. Does the behavior stay inside the situation that requires it?

Because the route generalizes. It does not stay where it was built. A person who cannot object at work and objects sideways there instead will very often find, on inspection, that they now do it at home with somebody who has no power over them at all, and in a friendship where the worst possible outcome of speaking plainly is a slightly awkward twenty minutes. The skill was acquired in a place that demanded it and it does not check the address before running.

So: keep the version that is doing accurate work. Look hard at the version that has spread. Those are two different behaviors wearing the same clothes, and they need opposite things from you.

## Why Does the Sideways Version Arrive Before You Know You Are Angry?

Because in the most common version of this, the behavior is the first evidence.

The sequence people assume is: something happens, you feel angry, you decide not to say it, you express it sideways instead. That sequence describes a minority of cases and it is the one every piece of advice is written for. The far more common sequence runs like this. Something happens. Something in you registers it, below the level where you could report on it. The next thirty behaviors you produce are shaded by that registration. And you find out that you were angry, at some point later, by looking at what you did.

This is why the most reasonable question in the world produces nothing. Somebody asks what is wrong. You go looking. There is a physical sense that something is off, located somewhere around your chest and shoulders, with no sentence attached to it. You say nothing, or you say you are fine, and both of those are honest reports on the contents of your attention at that moment. The other person hears a refusal to say, because from their side there is obviously something and you are obviously declining to name it. There is nothing available for you to name.

Then, twenty minutes later, or in the car, or the next morning while filling the kettle, the sentence arrives fully written. It is specific. It has a name and a date in it. It is exactly the thing you would have said if you had had it, and it is now four hours too late to say without reopening something that has closed.

There is a body of research relevant to this that is worth knowing about, on **emotion differentiation**, which is the capacity to tell your unpleasant feelings apart from one another rather than experiencing them as one undifferentiated bad. Kashdan and colleagues reviewed this in 2015. People who make finer distinctions among negative states regulate them more effectively, and related work has linked poor differentiation to being more likely to act aggressively after a provocation, which is the closest published thing to what this page describes. The finding is correlational, the effect sizes are moderate, and the way differentiation is measured is genuinely contested, so hold it loosely. Held loosely, it still says something useful: the problem is not that you are hiding the feeling. The problem is upstream of hiding, in whether the feeling has arrived in a form you could report.

## Why Does Nobody Ever Get to Have the Conversation?

Because every route into it has been closed by the same move that opened the subject.

Consider what the other person actually has to work with. They have a task that was agreed to and has not happened. They have a compliment that was technically a compliment. They have an eleven minute delay with traffic behind it. They have a three line email that agreed with them. Each of those, taken alone, is nothing. Raised alone, each one makes the raiser sound unreasonable, and they know it, which is why they do not raise them.

So they wait until they have several, and then they say something general, because the general version is the only one the evidence supports. You have been off with me all week. And the general version is the one you can most easily and most honestly deny, because you have no access to the channel it was carried in.

That exchange has one reliable outcome. They come away holding a real observation they could not evidence. You come away having been accused of something you cannot find. Neither of you is lying and neither of you has learned anything. And the thing that was originally bothering you, back in March, when the request was made in a way you did not like, is still sitting exactly where it was, untouched by the entire conversation.

Run that four times a year for a decade and you get two people who are certain about each other and wrong in both directions.

## Are You the Withdrawing Half of a Loop You Have Never Seen Drawn?

Almost certainly, and seeing the whole shape usually changes what you do about your half.

Here is the loop running at full speed. Somebody wants a thing settled. They raise it. You do not have a formed position, so you produce the shortest survivable response, which sounds like agreement or sounds like nothing. That reads to them as being managed rather than answered, so they raise it again with more weight behind it. The additional weight reads to you as pressure, which makes producing a formed position even less likely, so the next response is shorter. And now they are pressing hard on somebody who is producing almost nothing, which looks, from where they stand, like contempt.

At which point they say something about how you never engage, and you experience that as unfair, because you have been sitting there answering questions for forty minutes.

The reason the loop matters more than either half is that most people work exclusively on their own move. They resolve to engage more, or to stay in the room longer, or to give a real answer next time. That resolution is applied at the point of maximum pressure, which is the point at which you have least access to anything, and it fails, and the failure is then read as further evidence about your character.

The finding that helps is the one about roles tracking topics. If the roles swap when the subject swaps, then what you are looking at is a position in a structure rather than a permanent feature of you. Read that as a redirection rather than as a comfort. The useful work is on what happens when you do not want the change being asked for, which is a much smaller and much more tractable problem than becoming a different kind of person.

## How Good Is the Evidence Behind Any of This?

Weaker than the tone of most writing on the subject, and you should know exactly where the weakness is.

Start with the label. There is no widely agreed, well validated way of measuring this behavior in adults. The few self report instruments that exist are short, built largely from the old clinical description rather than from observation of what people actually do, and rarely tested across different populations. The formal clinical category was abandoned, for the reasons given above. So the phrase has an unusual property: it is enormously popular and it is not anchored to a measurement anybody agrees on.

Then there is how the phrase is used in ordinary speech, which is the second problem and possibly the larger one. It gets applied to a short reply, a delayed answer, a decision somebody disagreed with, a tone somebody did not like, and to essentially any behavior the speaker found annoying and wanted a technical sounding word for. A term that covers all of that has stopped picking out anything in particular. This matters for research as well as for arguments, because studies that use the phrase are frequently not measuring the same behavior as each other.

The surrounding research is in better shape than the label is, and it is where the useful material sits.

On indirect expression as a developmental achievement, Bjorkqvist, Lagerspetz and Kaukiainen reported in 1992 that indirect forms of aggression appear later in childhood than direct ones and depend on more developed social and verbal skill, since going indirect requires predicting what will land and how it will be read. The proposed logic is that the indirect route carries a lower risk of retaliation, which is exactly the property that makes it worth acquiring. The headline finding that paper is remembered for, a large sex difference in indirect aggression, has held up considerably less well. Archer's 2004 meta analysis of aggression in real world settings found the sex difference in indirect forms to be small, inconsistent, and heavily dependent on how it was measured and at what age. The developmental observation survives that correction. The sex difference story mostly does not, and it is repeated far more often than it is checked.

On where anger actually goes, Kassinove and colleagues surveyed self reported anger episodes across two countries in 1997 and found what the earlier work had found: episodes overwhelmingly involve somebody the person knows well and often likes, they are usually triggered by another person's behavior rather than by circumstances, and most of them are expressed in some form rather than kept entirely private. All of that is self report and all of it is retrospective, which are real limitations. The direction is consistent enough across studies to rely on.

On demand-withdraw, the pattern has been described in couples-communication work since the 1980s and the 2007 finding cited above is one of the clearer statements of it. The honest caveat is about direction. This work is largely observational and cross sectional, and it cannot settle whether the pattern produces the distress or the distress produces the pattern. Almost certainly both, in a loop, which is easy to say and hard to demonstrate.

What survives all of that is modest and it is enough to work with. Indirect expression is a real, learned, low risk route for dissatisfaction. It communicates. It forecloses discussion. It is more common where speaking directly is expensive. That is about as much as anyone can say with confidence, and it is considerably more than the popular phrase delivers.

## Why Do Two People Run the Identical Move for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the move is the visible part and it is the least informative part.

Two people leave the shed door for six months. One of them grew up in a house where a raised voice preceded something frightening, and learned before the age of eight that objections travel better through actions than through sentences. The other one has no such history at all, and simply has never in their life caught a feeling early enough to say it, so the schedule carries what the mouth never got to.

Those two are indistinguishable from outside. Every piece of advice that starts from the behavior will be given to both of them, and it will be wrong for at least one. The first person needs the old rule about what happens when you object to be examined and updated, which is slow work and worth doing. The second person does not have a rule to update. They have a timing problem, and the fix is a skill rather than an insight.

Five roots produce this behavior often enough to be worth separating. The one about power came first and it stays first. Here are the other four, and then a way of telling which is yours.

## What If Anger Was Never Safe to Show Where You Grew Up?

Then the indirect route was the only route, and you have been running it for so long that it no longer feels like a route.

This is the version people expect to find, and it is real, though it is not the most common one. A child in a house where anger arrived loudly and unpredictably learns two things at once: that anger is dangerous when it is shown, and that it does not stop existing when it is not. What is left is a set of channels that carry the charge without triggering the danger. Slowness. Forgetting. Compliance with something missing from it. A joke that lands harder than a joke.

Those channels are not chosen and they are not a strategy. They are what remains available after the direct channel has been closed off, and a child will find them without being taught, because the pressure has to go somewhere and every other exit has a cost attached to it.

The tell for this root is that the closure is total rather than selective. You do not object directly to anybody. Not to a friend who would take it well, not to a waiter who brought the wrong dish, not to a person you have known for twenty years and who has never once reacted badly to anything. When people with this root try to identify a single person they could say a hard thing to plainly, they frequently cannot produce a name.

The second tell is the physical one. Getting close to a direct statement produces something in the body that is out of all proportion to the situation. A conversation that could not possibly go worse than mild awkwardness produces a heart rate that belongs to something else entirely. That gap between the actual stakes and the physical response is the most reliable evidence that what you are running is old rather than current.

## What If You Genuinely Do Not Know You Are Angry Until Afterward?

Then the sideways expression is the first news you get rather than a substitute for saying it.

This is the most common root and it is almost never described, because it does not fit the shape of the popular story. In the popular story there is a feeling, and a person concealing it. In this root there is a registration that has not yet become a feeling you could report, and behavior that is already responding to it.

People with this root recognize themselves in one particular detail. They are frequently told what they are feeling by somebody else, and the report turns out to be right, and being right about it is infuriating in a way that is hard to explain. Somebody says you have been in a mood since Thursday. You say you have not. By Saturday you can see that you have, and that it started on Thursday, and that the cause was a thing that took nine seconds.

The other detail is the arrival time. The formed sentence shows up on a delay that is remarkably consistent for each person. For some it is twenty minutes. For others it is the following morning. Almost everybody who looks for it discovers that their delay is stable enough to predict, which is the first genuinely useful piece of information in this whole pattern, because a predictable delay can be planned around.

What this root does not need is communication training. You already know how to say a thing. The gap is entirely in access, and no amount of practice at phrasing will help with a feeling that has not arrived yet.

## What If Being Asked Directly Feels Like Losing?

Then what is being measured is whether they work it out, and telling them ends the measurement.

This root is uncomfortable to read about and it is extremely common. Underneath it there is a proposition, usually never stated even privately, that goes something like: if they cared enough, they would notice. Being asked to spell it out converts a test into a request, and the whole value of the test was that it did not require you to ask for anything.

It is worth naming without contempt, because the proposition is not stupid and it did not come from nowhere. Almost everybody who runs this has a real history of asking and not being heard, or of asking and being made to feel expensive for asking. Once that has happened enough times, a system that gets the result without requiring the ask is a rational upgrade. It fails for a reason nobody explains at the time: it hands the entire outcome to somebody else's perceptiveness, which is a variable you have no control over and which has very little to do with how much they care.

The tell is the specific quality of the disappointment. When somebody eventually asks what is wrong, you do not feel relieved. You feel something closer to defeat, and if you look at it honestly, the defeat is about the asking rather than about the subject. That reaction is the whole root, visible in about one second.

## What If the Behavior Simply Works?

Then it will continue, and no amount of insight will touch it until the accounting is on the table.

This is the version nobody admits to and it deserves to be named plainly and without any moral weight attached. The indirect route works. It registers displeasure with high reliability. It is almost never challenged, because the evidence for any single instance is too thin to raise. It costs nothing socially in the moment. It very often produces the change you wanted, because people adjust to a cold week without ever discussing why the week was cold. And it does all of that while keeping you, in your own account of yourself, as somebody who does not make a fuss.

A behavior with that profile requires no further explanation. Call it a solution that returns more than it costs rather than a failure of character, and solutions like that do not need early harm behind them to persist. Plenty of people running this have entirely unremarkable histories and simply found something efficient.

The reason it is worth examining anyway is that the accounting is incomplete rather than favorable. What the ledger does not show is on the other side of the table. Somebody near you is running a slow background process about whether they have done something wrong. They cannot resolve it, because the thing that would resolve it never gets said. Over years that produces a particular kind of tiredness in a person, and the tiredness gets attributed to the relationship rather than to the pattern, which is how the bill for this eventually arrives in a completely different currency from the one it was run up in.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, and How Would You Know?

Judge it by evidence you already have rather than by which description sounds most flattering.

| If this is true | The root is probably | The tell that confirms it | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| The person genuinely can harm you, and the behavior stops with people who cannot | Real power difference | You can name the specific consequence, and it is concrete rather than vague | Nothing, inside that situation. Check whether it has spread |
| You cannot name one person you could say a hard thing to plainly | Anger was unsafe early | Physical response far out of proportion to the actual stakes | The old rule examined, which is slow and worth doing |
| You find out you were angry by looking at what you did | No early access to the feeling | Your delay is consistent and predictable once you look for it | A timing skill, not communication advice |
| Being asked what is wrong produces defeat rather than relief | Wanting it worked out without telling | The disappointment attaches to the asking, not the subject | Examining what the test was originally built to find out |
| You can describe exactly what the behavior gets you | It works | You feel slightly defensive reading this row | The other side of the ledger, in writing |

Two things about the table. Most people are a blend, usually of the third row and one other, and the blend matters less than the dominant one. And the last row is the one people skip, so if you moved past it quickly, that is worth a second look.

## Where Does This Hide When It Does Not Look Like Anger?

In the places where slowness and precision are both socially acceptable, which is most places.

**In work that is technically complete.** The task was done. It was done to the letter of the request and no further. The obvious adjacent step, the one anybody doing it willingly would have taken, was left, and you knew you were leaving it.

**In documentation.** A written record produced for a situation that has never before required one. A summary email after a conversation that did not need summarizing. A politeness so precise that its precision is the message.

**In generosity with a hook.** A gift chosen with real attention that contains one detail which functions as a comment. A card with a sentence in it that reads two ways. A favor done in a manner that makes its cost visible.

**In accuracy.** Being right about something small at the exact moment when being right is least helpful. Correcting a date. Noting an inconsistency. The content is unimpeachable and the timing is doing all the work.

**In enthusiasm that has been withdrawn.** Nothing is refused. Nothing is missing. The temperature has simply dropped a few degrees across everything, uniformly enough that no single interaction is odd.

**In illness and tiredness that arrive on schedule.** A headache before a particular type of evening. Exhaustion that lifts an hour after the thing is canceled. This one is worth stating carefully, because tiredness is usually just tiredness, and the pattern only counts if it has a target.

## Is This the Same as Going Quiet, Saying You Are Fine, or Going Grey Rock?

No, and the differences are worth having, because these four get treated as one thing and they need different work.

Going quiet in the middle of an argument is an involuntary loss of access under live pressure. The words stop being available while the conversation is running, and they come back afterward. That is a different mechanism and it has [its own page](/patterns/why-do-i-shut-down-in-the-middle-of-an-argument/). What this page describes happens outside the argument, frequently days later, in a channel nobody is watching, and it does not require pressure to be present at all.

Saying you are fine is a two word answer with its own page as well, and if what brought you here was the question of why that phrase arrives before you have checked how you are, [that one](/patterns/saying-im-fine/) is the more useful page. What belongs here is the narrower version where the words are technically an answer and the delivery is carrying a message the words are not.

The silent treatment is aimed. It works by making an absence loud, and it is meant to be noticed and understood as deliberate. What this page describes works the opposite way. It is meant to be readable and unprovable, which is exactly why the person on the receiving end ends up sounding paranoid when they try to describe it.

Going flat and uninteresting around a specific person is a protective move aimed at yourself rather than at them. It withholds material so there is less to be used. That is a defensive posture rather than a delivered message.

And resentment is the accumulated version, an old and unexpressed injury that has hardened into a fixed position and now leaks onto whoever is nearest. What this page describes is live and current and correctly addressed. The anger is aimed at the right person about a recent thing. It is only the route that is indirect. The two are close relatives and [the resentment page](/patterns/resentment/) covers the accumulated case properly.

## Why Does "Just Communicate" Fail Every Time You Try It?

Because it solves a problem you do not have.

The advice assumes a person holding a formed grievance who is deciding whether to voice it. Everything that follows from that assumption is reasonable. Choose a good moment. Use a structure that does not put the other person on the defensive. Describe the behavior rather than the character. All of that is sound and all of it requires content.

You do not have content at the moment it would matter. What you have is a physical registration and thirty behaviors already responding to it. Handing a sentence structure to somebody in that state is like handing somebody a pen when the problem is that they cannot read what they are being asked to sign.

There is a second failure that is quieter and more discouraging. On the occasions when you do have content, and you use the structure, and you say the sentence, the result is frequently underwhelming. The other person hears a formal, slightly stiff sentence arriving four days after the event it refers to, and they respond to the strangeness of the delivery rather than to the substance. You conclude that speaking plainly does not work for you and go back to the route that does. Both of those conclusions are wrong and both are completely reasonable given the evidence you have.

The thing the advice never accounts for is the gap. All of the useful work sits between the registration and the formed sentence, and communication advice starts after the sentence exists.

## What Is the Difference Between the Move and the Twenty Minutes Afterward?

One of them was available to you and one of them was not, and everything that changes here changes in the second one.

The sideways move happens before you have located the feeling. There was no fork in the road, no moment where you weighed two options and chose the worse one. Treating it as a choice produces guilt, and guilt applied at a point where you had no vote is worse than useless, because it adds a second thing to be quiet about.

The twenty minutes afterward is different in kind. At some point, and the point is more predictable than you think, the sentence arrives. You know what the thing was. You know who it is about. You know precisely what you would say. And in that window there is a real choice, made by you, with full information, and it is the only genuinely voluntary moment in the entire sequence.

Almost everybody spends that window doing one of two things. Rehearsing a version they will not deliver, which is a way of feeling like the matter has been handled without any of it leaving your head. Or deciding it has passed and is no longer worth raising, which it always looks like at that distance, because the thing was small and the moment has closed and going back to it now would be strange.

Going back to it now is strange. That is the actual cost and there is no way around it. Raising something four hours late means saying that you have been thinking about a small thing for four hours, which is a slightly embarrassing thing to admit about yourself. That embarrassment is the entire price of admission, and almost every person who does this pays it and reports afterward that the conversation itself was unremarkable.

The strangeness also drops fast. The first time you return to something after the fact it feels enormously awkward. By the fourth or fifth time it is a normal feature of how you talk to that person, and by then the delay has usually started shortening on its own, because the sentence is now being written for something rather than into a drawer.

## What Does the Other Person Actually Experience?

Something they can feel completely and describe not at all, which is a specific and corrosive combination.

They know the temperature has changed. They knew within about an hour. What they do not have is a single event they could point at that would survive being said out loud, because every individual instance has an ordinary explanation sitting on top of it and they know how they would sound raising it.

So they hold the observation. And holding an observation you cannot check does something particular to a person over time. They start monitoring. They read your replies for length. They notice the gap between the two texts. They construct theories and then talk themselves out of them, and then something happens that supports the theory again.

Eventually they either say the general version, which you can honestly deny, or they stop mentioning it and adjust around it silently. The second outcome is the more common one and it is the more expensive one. Adjusting silently around somebody for years is how people end up living carefully with a person they love, and the carefulness is almost never traced back to its cause, because its cause was never a nameable event.

If somebody has told you that you are hard to read, or that they never know where they stand with you, and you have found that unfair because you consider yourself extremely easy to get along with, this is the mechanism they are describing badly.

## Are You Sure That Is What You Are Looking At?

Several things get called this that are not this, and the phrase is applied so loosely that it is worth checking before you accept it about yourself.

You might simply be slow at a task you agreed to and find genuinely unpleasant, with no dissatisfaction attached to anything. Plenty of tasks are avoided because they are boring rather than because they carry a message. The test is the target: an objection has an address, and a chore that everybody in the house avoids does not.

You might be conflict avoidant without the indirect expression, which is a different pattern. Somebody who lets things go and then genuinely lets them go is not doing what this page describes, however uncomfortable the letting go feels.

You might be dealing with somebody who uses the phrase as a general purpose complaint. It gets applied to short replies, to reasonable delays, to a decision somebody disliked, and to almost any behavior that irritated the speaker. Being called this is not evidence of doing it, and one of the costs of a phrase that vague is that it can be used to make an ordinary boundary sound like something wrong with you.

And you might be tired, or unwell, or carrying something entirely unrelated that has flattened everything. Tiredness lands on whoever is nearest and it is indiscriminate. Anger has an address. If everybody in your life got a slightly worse version of you this week, look at your sleep before you look at your character.

## What Actually Moves This?

Noticing earlier, in that order, and nothing else first.

**Move the recognition, before you change any behavior.** For two weeks, do not attempt to say anything to anybody. Simply catch the delay. When the sentence arrives, in the car or at the sink or the next morning, write down two things: what it was about, and how long after the event it showed up. That is the entire exercise. Almost everybody discovers that their delay is far more consistent than they expected, and consistency is what makes a thing workable.

**Then work the twenty minutes.** Once you know your delay, you know when to expect the sentence. Use it once. Go back to the person and say the small late thing, with the lateness included in the sentence rather than hidden. Something in the shape of: this is four hours late and it is small, and on Tuesday when you said the thing about the shed I got annoyed and did not say so. Nothing about that requires a technique. It requires accepting that you will sound slightly odd for about eight seconds.

**Then look at the ratio, not the incidents.** Take one week and list the things you agreed to and did not do, and beside each one, whether you wanted to do it. Nobody argues with a single forgotten errand. The column is where the pattern lives, and it is not available any other way, because each item individually has a real explanation and the explanations are all true.

**Then look at what the route is buying.** For each person you do this with, write down what actually changes after a cold week. Frequently something does. That is the part of the ledger that has never been examined, and examining it does more than any resolution, because a resolution argues with a behavior while the ledger explains it.

**And leave the root for last.** Whichever of the five is yours, it does not move by being identified. Identifying it tells you what kind of work is in front of you and it is not itself the work. The recognition skill, on the other hand, improves for everybody regardless of root, which is why it goes first.

## What Does the Resolved Version Look Like on an Ordinary Tuesday?

Smaller than people imagine, and it does not involve becoming a person who says everything.

Somebody makes a request in a way you do not like. You still do not have a sentence in the moment. What is different is that you notice a small flat feeling within about an hour instead of within three days, and you find it slightly interesting rather than alarming. That evening you say a version of it, badly, and the conversation lasts ninety seconds and is boring.

The shed door gets fixed in a week, or it gets renegotiated out loud, and either of those is fine. What has gone is the third option, where it sits for six months carrying a message nobody can read.

The compliments stop having hooks in them, mostly, and when one does you hear it in the same second rather than half a second late, and about a third of the time you can say so.

Somebody occasionally still tells you that you have been off with them. The difference is that you can now usually find it when you go looking, and finding it takes a minute rather than a week. That is the whole change and it is worth more than it sounds, because the alternative was a week of them not knowing and you not knowing either.

What does not change is the preference. You would still rather not have the direct conversation. That preference is allowed to stay. The difference between the current version and the easier one sits in whether the preference gets to run a household for six months at a time.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

Three different speeds, and running them together is why people give up in the third week.

The noticing moves fast. Two weeks of catching the delay and writing it down usually shortens it noticeably, and most people get from days to the same evening within a month or two. It shortens further with attention, and for many people it lands somewhere around the same hour, which is close enough to be usable.

The habit takes longer. The indirect route has been the default for a long time and it will keep being the first thing that fires. Expect months rather than weeks, and expect the route to still be there under exhaustion, in the first weeks after any conversation that went badly, and with the specific person it was originally built for. A return under those conditions is the return of the conditions.

The root moves on its own schedule and does not respond to effort applied at the behavior. An old rule about what happens when you object can sit quietly for years and then loosen over a single unremarkable season. A power difference resolves when the situation resolves and not before. The version that runs because it works changes when the ledger changes, which is fast for some people and never for others.

One thing that requires no waiting at all: the two weeks of catching the delay works on the first attempt, for everybody, regardless of which root is underneath. It is the cheapest useful thing here and it is the only one that does not depend on knowing anything about yourself first.

## Where Does This Thinking Come From?

From a small amount of published research and a much larger amount of watching people describe this behavior in rooms, and it is worth separating the two.

The research side is thin and I would rather say so than dress it up. Bjorkqvist, Lagerspetz and Kaukiainen published the developmental work on indirect aggression in 1992 in Aggressive Behavior, and it is the source of the claim that indirect forms appear later and require more social skill than direct ones. Archer published the meta analysis in Review of General Psychology in 2004 that substantially weakened the sex difference finding that paper is usually remembered for. Kassinove and colleagues published the two country survey of self reported anger episodes in 1997, which is where the observation about anger overwhelmingly involving people we know well comes from. Eldridge and colleagues published the demand-withdraw comparison across distress levels in the Journal of Family Psychology in 2007. Kashdan and colleagues reviewed emotion differentiation in Current Directions in Psychological Science in 2015.

There are researchers whose work is relevant here whose names I have left off deliberately. The demand-withdraw pattern has more cited originators than the paper I have used, and the measurement of anger expression styles has a canonical figure behind it. Both are cited on other pages of this site in different contexts, and repeating a name across a set of pages makes a body of writing feel assembled rather than thought about. Where the finding matters more than the attribution, I have named the finding.

The rest of this is mine and carries no research weight whatsoever. Five years and more than five thousand hours of doing this work on myself before I built anything around it, and the several hundred conversations since in which somebody has described the shed door, the hook in the compliment, and the errand that evaporated, without ever having heard anybody else describe them. I hold no clinical qualification of any kind. None of the researchers named has any connection to this page or to the seminar, and where their published work and my own experience disagree, theirs is the one to go and check.

## What Is Worth Doing This Week, Before You Say Anything to Anybody?

Two weeks of catching the delay, and nothing else at all.

Do not attempt a conversation. Do not resolve to communicate better. Do not tell anybody you are working on this, because announcing it converts the whole thing into something you are being watched on and that is the fastest way to stop looking honestly.

Just catch the arrival. Every time a sentence shows up late, in the car, at the sink, in the eleven minutes before you fall asleep, write down what it was about and how long after the event it arrived. Two lines. You will have somewhere between four and fifteen of them by the end of the fortnight.

Then read the column. The delay will be more consistent than you expected, and the targets will be less various than you expected, and one name will appear more often than the rest. All three of those are information you have never had, because the behavior is specifically designed to prevent you from having it.

If what landed hardest here was the timing, the two weeks will probably be enough on its own and you can stop there. If what landed hardest was something else, the recognition that you cannot name one person you could speak plainly to, or that being asked what is wrong feels like defeat rather than relief, or that you can describe exactly what a cold week gets you and would rather not have written it down, then the thing worth working on is underneath the route rather than in it. [Our Sacred Valley seminar](https://florianrolke.com/the-soulsearching-experience/) works at that layer. The goal is not to feed your emotions. The goal is to find what the behavior has been doing for you, then what is standing in the way of the direct version, then a plan you are held accountable to afterward, back in the same kitchen with the same shed door and the same person. Everybody is screened before arriving, which keeps the group small enough that somebody whose anger has been arriving four days late for twenty years gets worked with properly rather than handed a worksheet. One limitation worth stating plainly: exact dates, the price, and the final cap on group size are still being settled, so anybody comparing programs on those numbers this week cannot yet compare ours. Ask every program you are weighing the same questions, this one included, and decide from what comes back.

The fortnight of lines costs you nothing and it is worth having either way.
