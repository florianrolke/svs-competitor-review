# Why do I come on too strong at the start of relationships?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Early intensity is common and, on its own, a poor signal of anything. Being told you came on too strong reports on what reached the other person rather than on how much you felt. The feeling was not chosen. What you did when they went quiet was, and that is where this whole pattern actually turns.

It was thirteen days.

You could count them if you had to, and at some point you probably did. Thirteen days, four evenings, one long afternoon that went from a bookshop to a bench to somewhere neither of you had planned to be. And on the thirteenth day you were looking at somewhere to go next summer, not seriously, or not seriously in a way you would have admitted, but with enough attention that you knew what the flights cost and which week would be better.

You did not tell them that part. You told them a slightly smaller part of it.

And you found the hardback. That was the same week. They had mentioned a writer once, in passing, in the middle of a story that was actually about something else, and you had held onto it the way you hold onto everything, and then there it was in a secondhand place on a shelf you were not even looking at properly. Fifteen pounds. You did not think about it for a second. You wrote something inside the cover, and then you looked at what you had written for a long time, and then you gave it to them anyway.

They said thank you. They said it warmly. And then something in the evening went slightly flat in a way you could not have explained to anyone, and you spent the walk home going through the sentence you had written, word by word, trying to find the one that had done it.

Then the replies got shorter.

Not cold. Nobody was cold. A message you had spent a while on came back as a line, and then the line came back a bit later than the one before it, and you read the gap the way other people read weather. And what you did next is the only part of this whole account that you actually chose, so it is worth saying it plainly rather than skating past it: you sent more. A longer one. Then, when that did not land, a lighter one to take the pressure off, which you also spent a while on. Then a small plan for the weekend that you presented as casual and had actually been thinking about for two days.

And somewhere in there they said the word. Intense. Or too much, or a lot, or one of the softer versions that means the same thing and is harder to argue with.

So you went and looked it up, the way anybody does now, and what you found was a great deal of writing about a person who does this on purpose. A person running a strategy. A person who overwhelms somebody early because a person who feels overwhelmed is easier to keep. Every article assumed you were reading it because that person had happened to you, and every article was written to help you escape them.

None of it fit. You were not running anything. You were not steering toward an outcome. You were a person who liked somebody a great deal, thirteen days in, and who does not know any way to show that other than to show all of it.

And so you closed the tab and you have had nowhere to put this since.

## What Is Love Bombing, and Where Did the Word Actually Come From?

The word you have been handed for this is **love bombing**, and its history matters more than usual, because most of what the word carries was picked up somewhere other than where it is now being used.

It came out of writing about coercive groups in the 1970s. In that setting it named a specific recruitment tactic with a specific structure: surround a newcomer with overwhelming, unearned warmth, get them dependent on it, and then make it conditional on staying. The warmth was a means to something from the first minute. That was the entire point of noticing it and giving it a name.

Applying it to one individual and their new partner came much later, mostly through popular writing and then, very fast, through social media. Nobody really argued that move anywhere. It happened, and the word brought its original weight with it, which is why being called love bombing feels less like a description of your Tuesday and more like a verdict on your character.

Used precisely, the term still means what it meant. It describes attention, affection, gifts and certainty delivered early and heavily **in order to** produce dependence. The purpose is the whole content of the word. Strip the purpose out and you have described an enthusiastic first two weeks.

That precision has almost entirely gone. In ordinary use now the word attaches to a shape: fast, warm, large, early. And since the shape is all anybody outside can see, the shape is what gets named.

**Here is the boundary this page draws, and it is worth drawing before anything else.** If you look for it honestly and find that you chose the intensity for what it would do to them, and knew that while you were doing it, then what you are describing is a different matter from the one this page treats, and a page about a pattern is the wrong place to bring it. That is one sentence and it does not have a softer version. Everything after this is written for the far larger group of people whose early intensity is sincere, uncalculated, and expensive anyway.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Four situations, and none of them is the ordinary version described above.

**When the answer to the calculation question was yes.** Named already, and worth naming twice. If you can find in yourself a decision to use somebody's feelings to hold them in place, reading about patterns will not touch it and may well give you a more sophisticated vocabulary for not touching it.

**When a pull-back has produced anything you would not want described in a courtroom.** Turning up somewhere uninvited, repeated contact after being asked to stop, anything involving their accounts, their movements or their other relationships. That is a different order of thing than a long message, it has consequences that do not care about your intentions, and it needs help now rather than later.

**When the end of something early takes you somewhere frightening.** If the collapse after a short relationship goes past grief into a place where your own safety is a live question, stop reading pages and speak to somebody licensed today. That is an emergency rather than a pattern, and it is treatable.

**When somebody has told you, more than once, that they were afraid of you.** Not annoyed, not overwhelmed. Afraid. Two people saying that independently is data of a kind no page can help you interpret, and it deserves a professional rather than an article.

## Is Anything Actually Wrong With You for Feeling This Much?

Almost certainly not, and the evidence points the other way fairly clearly.

Strong early attraction is an intense state in most people who experience it. It produces repetitive thinking about one person, mood that tracks small signals, a physical drop when a reply is slow, and a picture of somebody assembled at a speed that has no business being that confident. It does all of this in people whose lives are in order, whose work is fine, and who are perfectly sensible about everything else in the same week.

What follows from that is a point people find genuinely counterintuitive, so it is worth stating flatly. The size of the feeling is a poor signal on its own. It does not tell you the relationship is unusually good. It does not tell you this one is different. And it does not tell you that the other person is anywhere near the same place, which is the part this page is actually about.

There is a study worth knowing here, because it puts a number on something that usually gets left as an assumption. Zsok, Haucke and Barelds went to actual dating events and asked people, on the spot, whether they had just experienced love at first sight, rather than doing what most of this literature does and asking established couples to remember. What they found was that reports of it were strongly predicted by how physically attractive the other person had been rated. People reporting it did not report more passion, intimacy or commitment than anybody else in the room. And the reports were overwhelmingly one-sided.

That last part is the one to sit with. One person having a large experience of a moment does not make the moment a shared event. It is entirely ordinary for the size of the thing on your side to have no counterpart at all on theirs, without anybody lying, misleading, or doing anything wrong.

The honest limits of that work: it is cross-sectional, the samples were mostly students and event attendees, and the number of people actually reporting the experience was small. It settles nothing about your own thirteen days. What it does do is take a claim you may have been treating as obvious, that a feeling this size must be about something real between two people, and show that it does not follow.

## How Much Evidence Is Actually Behind the Word You Have Been Handed?

Less than the confidence of the writing around it would suggest, and this matters because you have probably been reading that writing as though it were settled.

The first published attempt to measure love bombing appeared in 2017, from Strutzenberg and colleagues. It built a short self-report questionnaire and scored it against other self-report questionnaires in a student sample. It was published in an undergraduate student research journal. That takes nothing away from the people who did it, who did more than anybody had done before. What it describes is where the foundations sit.

A small number of studies have appeared since, mostly recent, mostly short self-report scales, mostly in samples of young adults. The whole measurement literature on this term is a few years old and thin.

And then there is the problem that no scale can get around, which is the one that matters most for you.

**A questionnaire cannot see intent.** Every item on every one of these scales asks about behavior: did they contact you constantly, did they give extravagant gifts, did they talk about the future early. A person who did all three sincerely and a person who did all three strategically answer identically. The scale cannot separate them, because the difference lives entirely in a place the scale has no access to.

Which means the term as measured and the term as defined have quietly come apart. The definition is about purpose. The measurement is about behavior. Nothing currently connects the two.

The checklists circulating on social media are a further step down from that. They have no validation whatever. Nobody has shown that scoring high on one predicts anything at all, the items are broad enough that a normal enthusiastic beginning clears most of them, and they were written to help somebody worried about a partner, which is a legitimate job and a completely different one from telling you what you are.

| What gets claimed | What is actually behind it |
|---|---|
| Love bombing is a recognized pattern with clear signs | A term borrowed from 1970s writing about coercive groups, applied to couples decades later without much argument |
| There are validated warning signs you can check yourself against | The popular checklists have no validation of any kind; nobody has tested what a high score predicts |
| Research has established who does this and why | A handful of small, recent, self-report studies, the first in a student research journal, correlating questionnaires with other questionnaires |
| An expert could tell whether you were doing it | Intent is the defining feature, and no questionnaire and no observer can see intent |
| The behavior itself is the problem | The same behaviors are produced by sincere intensity and by calculation, at rates nobody has measured |

That table is the single most useful thing on this page for anybody who has been accused of this and cannot answer. The accusation is being made with an authority the underlying evidence does not have.

## Why Can Nobody Tell From the Outside Which One You Are?

Because there is genuinely nothing to see.

The person running a deliberate strategy and the person who simply feels a great deal and has one way of showing it produce the same messages, at the same hours, in the same numbers. They both remember the offhand mention and turn up with the thing. They both talk about the future early. They both respond to a cooling with warmth rather than distance. If you filmed both of them for two weeks and showed the footage to anybody, they would not be separable.

This is not a failure of perception on the other person's part, and it is worth resisting the pull to treat it as one. They are working with everything that is available, and everything that is available is the same in both cases.

It also explains why the accusation is unanswerable in the specific way it is. Being told you love bombed somebody is a claim about your interior, made from your exterior. The only evidence you have to the contrary is your own report of your own intentions, and that is precisely the evidence a person worried about being manipulated has the least reason to weigh. Insisting harder is itself an escalation, which is the original problem in a new costume.

So the arguing goes nowhere, every time, and both people leave more sure of what they came in with.

The way out of that loop is to stop trying to establish what you meant, which cannot be established, and to look instead at something that both of you could actually have observed at the time. There is exactly one such thing and the next section is about it.

## What Is the One Difference That Is Actually Checkable?

Whether what you were sending was coming back at roughly its own size.

This is the spine of the whole page, so it is worth being slow about it.

Ordinary getting-to-know-somebody has a rough symmetry to it that almost nobody is taught and almost everybody feels. Personal disclosure tends to be returned at similar depth. Warmth tends to be answered with warmth of about the same weight. A long message tends to get a long message. An offer of a weekend tends to be met with an idea about the weekend. This is a described regularity rather than one laboratory's finding, and it is one of the sturdier things known about how conversation between two people runs. It is treated properly on the [page about changing the subject](/patterns/changing-the-subject/), which carries the specific research on disclosure being returned at similar depth, and on the [page about keeping people at a distance](/patterns/keeping-people-at-a-distance/), which carries what happens when one person does all the disclosing.

What follows for you is simple to state and quite hard to look at.

**An escalation the other person is matching is a shared event.** Two people are speeding up together. Both of them are bringing things. Both of them are the one who mentioned the trip. Neither is being handed anything they did not reach for.

**An escalation the other person is not matching is one person's event happening in a room containing two people.** And that is checkable, in the moment, without knowing anything about your own motives, without a diagnosis, and without waiting to find out how it ends.

| What you did | Matched | Unmatched |
|---|---|---|
| Sent a long message | A long one comes back, roughly as personal | A line comes back, warm, and later than the last one |
| Talked about next summer | They add a place they want to go | They say that sounds nice and change the subject |
| Gave something they had mentioned once | They tell you something they had been holding onto too | They thank you warmly and the evening goes slightly flat |
| Said something large about how you feel | They say something of about the same size | They say something true and much smaller, and look away |
| Suggested seeing each other twice in a week | They suggest the second one | You suggest both, and the second one moves |
| Went quiet for a day to see | They surface first | Nothing arrives, and eventually you surface |

Read that table as information rather than as a verdict. The right-hand column does not mean they dislike you and it does not mean you have done anything wrong. Many people are slower. Many people were in a bad week. What the right-hand column means is that at that moment, the thing you were experiencing was not being experienced by two people, and everything you sent after that point was going into a room where nobody had asked for it.

This is also the honest reason the intensity is not the thing worth fixing. The intensity was fine on day one, when nobody could tell yet. It became expensive the moment the column changed and the sending did not.

## Why Does Their Going Quiet Make You Send More Rather Than Less?

Because more is the only thing you know how to send, and a drop in warmth reads as a call for exactly that.

Think about what the alternatives would even be. When somebody you like slows down, the available responses are: send more, send the same, send less, or send nothing and sit in it. Three of those four require tolerating a stretch of not knowing, and one of them requires nothing at all except doing the thing that has always worked. If more has been your way of signaling that something matters since you were young, then more stops being one option among four and becomes the option, with the other three left as theory.

There is a plain mechanism here and it is worth having, because it makes the moment feel less like a personal defect.

When a behavior that has reliably produced a result stops producing it, the behavior does not immediately fade. It briefly intensifies first. More of it, harder, faster, before it starts to drop off. This is well documented in the study of behavior generally and it is the single most recognizable thing on this page for most readers: the four days after somebody cools are the four days you send the most.

The honest version of that evidence, because it is regularly overstated: Lerman and Iwata went through clinical cases in which a reinforcer had been withdrawn and found that this temporary increase showed up in a minority of them, not in all of them, and that it was rarer still when the withdrawal was paired with other procedures. So the burst is real, it is common, and it is nothing like the universal law that popular accounts of habit turn it into. That work was also done in applied clinical settings on severe behavior decades ago, so carrying it across to two adults and a phone is an analogy rather than a finding. It is offered here as a shape that makes the four days legible, and not as proof of anything about you.

What the shape does explain is the timing, which people find genuinely disorienting. The heaviest sending arrives at the exact point the other person has the least capacity to receive it. That is the structure of the thing rather than bad luck. Your peak output is triggered by their withdrawal, so it is guaranteed to arrive at the worst possible moment, every time, for as long as more is the only lever.

## Which Part of This Did You Actually Choose?

One part, and the page is careful about this because everything turns on it.

**The feeling was not chosen.** Nobody selects the size of an early attraction and nobody has ever successfully talked themselves out of one. It arrives, it is loud, and it is loud in most people. You are not responsible for it in any sense that would make sense to hold against you.

**The picture you built was mostly not chosen either.** Thin information gets filled in with your own material at speed, automatically, and it happens to everyone. That is a whole pattern on its own and it belongs on the [page about falling for the idea of a person](/patterns/falling-for-the-idea-of-a-person/), which owns it properly.

**What you did in the days after they slowed down was chosen.** Not easily, not calmly, and against a strong pull, but chosen. Each individual message was a thing you decided to send. The hardback was a thing you decided to hand over after standing there reading what you had written in it. The weekend plan presented as casual was constructed over two days.

This is the good news on the page, and it is not a small piece of good news. If the whole thing were the feeling, there would be nothing to work with, because the feeling is not available for editing. The part that has consequences is the part that runs through a decision, even a fast and pressured one, and decisions can be worked on.

It also means the useful question changes shape. Not why do I feel so much, which has no answer and does not need one. The question is what the sending is doing for you, in the hour you do it. And that has five common answers.

## Why Do Five People Come On Too Strong for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the behavior is the last thing to happen, and by the time it shows up it has lost all the information about where it came from.

Two people can send the identical message on the identical evening after the identical silence. One of them is simply experiencing an ordinary large feeling and has no smaller way to express it. The other is trying to make an uncertain thing certain before it can be taken away. From outside, and from the other person's phone, they are the same event.

What follows is five roots. They are not types of person and nobody is only one of them. Read them for which one produces a flinch of recognition rather than which one sounds best.

## What If This Is Ordinary Early Intensity and Nothing Else?

Start here, because this is the largest group by a wide margin and most pages on this subject never mention it at all.

Some people simply feel early attraction more strongly than average, express it more openly than average, and have arrived, at thirteen days, at a place other people arrive at around week eight. Nothing underneath it is doing any work. There is no wound, no strategy, no deficit. The dial sits high, it has always sat high, and in the right situation it is one of the better things about them.

You would recognize yourself here if the intensity is not confined to romance. If you also get this way about a new job, a new city, a piece of music, a person you have just become friends with. If your enthusiasm has been described as a lot by people who were not talking about a relationship. If, when it has gone well, it has gone very well, and the other person met you at that speed and both of you look back on those weeks fondly.

For this root, the honest account is that nothing is wrong with the intensity and something is missing in the reading. The gap is that you have never had a way to check whether the other person is somewhere near the same place, so the size that is delightful when it is matched keeps getting deployed when it is not. That is a skill gap in one narrow spot rather than a fault in your character, and it is the cheapest of the five to close.

What would change if this is yours: nothing about how much you feel, and quite a lot about the four days after somebody goes quiet.

## What If More Is the Only Thing You Know How to Send?

The second root is about range rather than about size.

Some people have a rich vocabulary for showing care and some have one word said louder. If you grew up somewhere that warmth was expressed through provision, effort, or sheer quantity, then the ways of signaling that something matters to you are: do more, give more, say more, be there more. There is no smaller register available, because nobody ever demonstrated one.

That produces something specific and slightly odd, which is that a pull-back reads as a call for more. If the only dial you have is amount, then every situation gets solved by turning it. They seem distant: turn it up. They seem busy: turn it up so they know it is fine. They said something that worried you: turn it up. The dial does not have a direction that means less.

You would recognize yourself here if you find small gestures somehow embarrassing, or fake. If a short warm message feels like it does not count. If you have noticed that you would rather do a large thing for somebody than say a medium thing to them, and that being asked what you want feels harder than being asked to provide something.

There is a general version of this that is not about the beginning of relationships at all, and it is held on the [page about giving more than was asked](/patterns/giving-more-than-was-asked/), which treats the oversized gift across the whole of a life. What belongs here is the specific early case, where the size cannot be proportionate to anything because there is nothing yet for it to be proportionate to. On day thirteen, everything is an overshoot by definition. The overshoot page will serve you better on the dinners and the weddings. This one holds the opening stretch.

## What If You Are Trying to Get It Settled Before It Can Be Lost?

The third root is about speed rather than about size, and speed is doing a different job here.

For some people, the unbearable part of the beginning is that it is undecided. Somebody could still change their mind. Nothing has been established, nothing is owed, and every day contains the live possibility that this ends. Escalating fast is an attempt to convert an uncertain thing into a certain one, quickly, before it can be taken away.

The trip next summer has very little to do with the trip. What it puts on the record is a future. The large declaration has less to do with a feeling that cannot be held in than with getting an answer. Every early escalation of this kind is a request for a verdict dressed as an offer.

You would recognize yourself here if the relief when they respond well lasts a surprisingly short time before the next uncertainty opens. If you have noticed that you push hardest for definition precisely when things are going well, which makes no sense until you see that going well is when there is most to lose. If the question underneath those two weeks was less do I like this person and more is this safe yet.

This is the root that overlaps with what gets described as anxious attachment, and it is treated properly on [its own page](/patterns/anxious-attachment-style/), which covers where it comes from and what earned security looks like. It is deliberately not the frame for this whole page, because four of these five roots have nothing to do with it, and leading with the label would put most readers in the wrong room. If this section is the one that landed, that page is where to go next.

## What If the Giving Was About You Rather Than About Them?

The fourth root is the least comfortable to read and the most common to deny, so take it slowly.

Sometimes the offering is a bid for a verdict on yourself. At the deepest level, the hardback had less to do with their pleasure in receiving a book than with being the sort of person who notices, who remembers, who turns up with the thing. The message written at length was partly about being read as someone worth having. The plan was partly about being the person who makes things happen.

The tell is the drop. When an offering is genuinely about the other person, a flat reception is mildly disappointing and passes in an hour. When the offering was carrying a verdict, a flat reception is disproportionately painful, it lasts, and it produces a specific kind of replaying in which you go through your own performance rather than through their reaction.

You would recognize yourself here if you have ever handed something over and then watched the other person's face with an intensity that had nothing to do with generosity. If the thank you being warm but ordinary produced a small collapse. If you have found yourself wanting to explain the gift, which is a way of asking for a better verdict on it.

The uncomfortable part is that this root makes the other person into an audience without either of you deciding that. And it is worth saying that this has nothing to do with being selfish or dishonest. It is extremely ordinary and it usually installs itself in a household where affection followed achievement closely enough that the two got welded together. But the effect on the other person is that they are being asked to grade something while they thought they were being given a present.

## What If You Read Something Real That They Did Not Read Back?

The fifth root is not a fault at all, and it needs to be on the list because leaving it off would be dishonest.

Sometimes there really was something there. You read a connection accurately, in the sense that something genuinely unusual was happening in those four evenings, and the other person did not read it the same way. That is ordinary mismatch. It happens constantly, to people who are not doing anything wrong on either side, and no amount of self-examination will convert it into a fault.

The Zsok finding above is the relevant one. Reports of a large early experience were overwhelmingly one-sided. That is the base rate. Two people having a comparable experience of the same two weeks is the unusual outcome, and expecting it is the error rather than having the experience.

You would recognize yourself here if the pattern does not repeat. If you can point to several beginnings that were perfectly measured, and one that was not, and the one that was not involved somebody who was genuinely unusual to you. If your friends' account of it is that you were unlucky rather than that you always do this.

If this is yours, then most of this page is not about you and you can put it down. The one thing worth keeping is the checkable difference, which would have told you in about day six what took until day nineteen to find out.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, and What Separates Them?

The separation lives outside the behavior, which is identical across all five. What tells them apart is everything that happens around it.

| Root | Where it shows up outside romance | What a flat reception produces | What the pull-back is asking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary early intensity | Everywhere: new work, new music, new friends | Mild disappointment that passes the same evening | Nothing; you are just still going |
| More is the only thing you send | Any relationship where you want to signal care | Confusion about what else you could have done | A bigger version of the same signal |
| Wanting it settled | Any undecided thing: jobs, plans, invitations | A fresh round of uncertainty within hours | An answer, urgently |
| The giving is about you | Work, friendships, family, anywhere you present | A disproportionate and lasting drop, with replaying | A verdict on you |
| A real read they did not share | Nowhere; this one does not repeat | Genuine grief rather than alarm | Nothing; the reading was already made |

Two practical tests, both of which you can run tonight without anybody's cooperation.

**The outside-romance test.** Ask whether the same shape appears where there is no romance at all. If you also overshoot with new friends, new colleagues and new interests, roots one, two and four are more likely and root five is nearly ruled out. If it appears only with people you are attracted to, root three moves up.

**The flat-reception test.** Recall the last time you gave something and got a polite, warm, entirely adequate thank you. Not a bad reception. An ordinary one. Then find the sentence that arrived in the first moment afterward. *That did not land* points at root two. *I have done something wrong* points at root three. *They do not think much of me* points at root four. *Well, that was nice anyway* points at root one. Nothing much at all, with a delayed sadness later, points at root five.

Neither test is diagnostic and both are better evidence than a checklist, because both use something you actually watched happen rather than something you believe about yourself.

## What Does This Actually Cost the Other Person?

This section exists because a page that treated your sincerity as the end of the matter would be doing you a disservice, and because the cost is real and specific rather than vague.

What an unmatched escalation removes is room to answer.

Consider the position somebody is in on the receiving end of an offering larger than the relationship. They can match it, which requires saying something they do not yet feel. They can decline it, which means wounding somebody who has done nothing but be warm to them. Or they can go quiet, which is what most people do, because it is the only option that does not require an active untruth or an active injury.

So the going quiet you read as coldness is very often the only door left open, and it was your offering that closed the others. That is worth holding without either flinching from it or turning it into a case against yourself. Both of those are ways of stopping the thought early.

There is a second cost that is easier to miss. A very fast, very warm opening makes the other person's own pace look like a deficiency. If somebody needs six weeks to know what they think, and they are meeting somebody who knew on day four, they are placed in the position of being the cold one, the withholding one, the one who is not sure. Many people find that so uncomfortable that they go along further than they meant to, and the going along is what produces the abrupt ending later, which then arrives as a shock to you and as a relief to them.

None of this makes you a manipulator. The word for what happened is that the size of what you sent had effects you did not intend and could not see. That is a real thing to have done. It is also a thing that can be true about somebody who meant every word.

## What Does This Cost You, If Anything?

Two things, and the second is the one people underestimate.

The first is obvious. Beginnings end more often and end more abruptly. A pattern of intense beginnings that stop suddenly is expensive in a straightforward way, and you have probably already priced it.

The second is quieter. Every time this happens, the conclusion available afterward is that you are too much. And that conclusion is not neutral: it is the one that makes the next beginning worse, because a person who suspects they are too much either performs a smaller version of themselves and feels false, or gives up on regulating anything and goes bigger. Both of those are worse than what you were doing before, and both are consequences of a bad explanation rather than of the behavior.

There is a third cost that only appears over a long stretch, which is that you never find out what a beginning at somebody else's pace is like. The four days after a pull-back are always filled, so the information those four days would have carried is never collected. What would they have done if nothing had arrived? You do not know. You have never let one run.

## Why Does Deciding to Slow Down Never Work?

Because it aims at the wrong thing, and because it is not really a decision that gets made at the moment it would need to be made.

Deciding, on a calm afternoon, to be more measured next time is a decision made by a person who is not currently feeling anything. The person who has to carry it out is somebody sitting at nine in the evening looking at a reply that came back shorter than the last one. Those two are not the same person and the first one has very little authority over the second.

Worse, holding back only removes the behavior and leaves everything that produced it exactly where it was. So the pressure finds a different exit. People who successfully stop sending messages often start rereading old ones instead, or checking whether the account was active, or drafting things and deleting them, or going conspicuously cool in a way that is itself a large communication. The surface moved. Nothing underneath it did. And then the pattern comes back looking like a personal failure of willpower, which it was never a failure of.

This is also why this page does not tell you to mirror their pace, or to wait until they come to you, or to be less available. Advice of that shape asks you to perform a smaller version of yourself and calls the performance progress. It works for about five weeks and then breaks on an evening that is worse than usual, and the break tends to be larger than anything that would have happened without the rule.

What does move is smaller and stranger than a rule. It is finding out what the sending is doing for you in the hour that you do it. Once that is actually visible, and not merely agreed with, the pull in the evening changes on its own, because a behavior that has become legible stops being the only thing available.

## What Does the Settled Version Actually Look Like?

Not calmer. That is the surprise, and it is the part worth saying clearly, because most people expect resolution to arrive as a lowered dial.

Somebody who has worked this out still feels the whole thing. Thirteen days in, still looking at somewhere to go next summer, still finding the book, still writing the long one. The size is unchanged and there is no reason to change it.

What is different is four days long.

They send the message and it comes back shorter, and they notice the gap, and they feel exactly what they always felt, and then nothing happens for a while. Not as discipline. Not as a rule they are following, and not as a technique from an article. It is closer to having a second thing available where previously there was only one. The information sits there, and instead of being answered with more, it gets read.

And what they read is the column. Is this coming back at about its own size, or not. If it is, they carry on being enthusiastic, which is a fine way to be. If it is not, they have learned something real about the situation in day six rather than day nineteen, and the something real is not about their own worth, which is the part that used to make it unreadable.

The ordinary version of this, on an ordinary evening, is very unglamorous. A reply comes in shorter than the last one. There is a drop, physically, in the chest, which does not go away. And the phone goes face down on the arm of a chair for an hour, and the evening continues, and at some point later there is a shrug, and nobody is performing it. That is the whole of it.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

The recognition can arrive in one evening. Most people who read a page like this one get the checkable difference immediately and can apply it retroactively to three or four past beginnings before they have finished reading.

Noticing the impulse before acting on it takes a few weeks, and it requires something live to practice on, which is the awkward part. There is no way to practice this in an empty week. It needs an actual slow reply from an actual person you actually like, and those arrive on their own schedule.

Sitting through a gap without filling it becomes unremarkable somewhere in the first few months, on the assumption that you get several chances at it. The first time is genuinely difficult and will feel like something is being lost. It is worth knowing in advance that this feeling is not evidence.

The underlying question, which is what the sending was buying you, is the long one. That is a matter of a year rather than a month, and it is the part that determines whether the four days stay changed or come back, because a habit removed without its cause tends to return with a new surface.

None of this is a schedule. Two people who recognize themselves in everything above can be working on entirely different things and take entirely different amounts of time, which is the honest reason no page can tell you.

## What Is Worth Doing Before Your Next Message?

One thing, and it is not aimed at anybody else.

Do not apologize to anyone this week. Do not send a message explaining that you have realized something about yourself, because that is another large offering and it lands exactly the way the first one did. Do not make a rule about how often you will text. Do not tell the person you are currently seeing that you are working on this.

Instead, take the last time somebody slowed down on you, and write out the sequence. Not what you felt and not what you meant. What you sent, in order, and how long the gaps were.

Something like: *day one, they replied short. Day one, I sent a long one. Day two, no answer, I sent a light one. Day two, evening, I sent the plan for Saturday. Day four, I found the book.*

Then look at it and do two things.

First, count how many of those items were sent after the evidence had already changed. Most people find that the honest number is all of them after the first. That is a description of the shape rather than a moral finding, and it sits on paper in your own handwriting, which is considerably harder to argue with than a memory.

Second, and this is the part that actually does the work, go through the list and next to each item write what you were hoping would come back. Not what you were expressing. What you were hoping to receive. Reassurance. A verdict. Certainty. Their attention back. Evidence that you had not ruined it. Be specific and be unkind to yourself only as far as accuracy requires.

The column on the right is the answer to the question this page has been circling. What you were hoping to receive is what the sending was for, and it is the thing that will still be there after any rule about texting has broken.

Keep the paper. Look at it again the next time somebody goes quiet, before you send anything.
