# Why have I arranged my whole week around avoiding one person?

The 7:41 was the train you always took. It got you in at a reasonable hour, it had a seat, and for about nine years you never thought about it once.

Then there was a stretch in the spring when you started taking the 7:12 instead. You told yourself it was the quiet. Fewer people, a table seat, a chance to read. That was true, as far as it went, and it was also the only version you ever said out loud, including to yourself.

It has now been eight months. Twenty-nine minutes of your morning, five days a week, for eight months, because of one person who gets on at the same station and who you have not spoken to since a conversation that lasted under four minutes.

Once you see it in the train, you start seeing it everywhere else. There is a coffee place on the corner you genuinely liked, and you have not been in since March, and you have a slightly better one now that involves a nine minute walk instead of a two minute one. There is a supermarket you drive past to get to a different branch of the same supermarket. There is a Tuesday thing you stopped going to, and when a friend asked why, you said work had been mad, which was also true, and also not the reason.

And there is the two seconds. Every room you have entered this year, you have read the whole thing before your coat was off. Not consciously. It completes before you know it has started. You have never in your adult life walked into a room and looked for a friend first.

None of that ever felt like a problem, because none of it ever felt like anything at all. Every single one of those moves cost about ninety seconds and produced a small clean feeling of relief. Nothing in the design of a day flags a ninety second cost. So the moves accumulated quietly for years, and the first time any of it became visible was the moment you tried to explain to somebody why you were not going to a wedding.

**If the person you are avoiding has hurt you, threatened you, or made you afraid for your physical safety, then your avoidance is doing exactly the right job, nothing on this page asks you to reduce it by a single street, and the right next step is a licensed professional or a domestic violence service, both of which build safety plans as ordinary specialist work.** That is a real, specific thing that exists and it is far better than anything you can construct alone. Everything from this point forward is written for the other situation, where the person is somebody you would simply rather not see, and where the arrangement has grown far larger than the reason.

## What Does One Week of This Actually Look Like?

Much smaller and much more numerous than people expect, which is the whole difficulty.

It looks like leaving eleven minutes earlier on Monday. It looks like a glance along a row of parked cars on Wednesday before you commit to walking in. It looks like reading the top three names on an invitation before you read the date. It looks like a route home that adds four hundred meters and passes nothing you need.

It looks like an aisle you did not enter, because you saw a coat at the far end of it and your body turned you around before any thought had formed, and you finished the shop in a different order and never once framed it as an event.

It looks like a message thread with a mutual friend where you ask a general question in a casual tone that is actually a reconnaissance question, and you get the answer you wanted, and you feel briefly lighter, and neither of you notices what just happened.

It looks like a Saturday where you said no to something you would have enjoyed, and the reason you gave yourself was tiredness, and the reason was not tiredness.

And underneath all of it, running constantly and costing something you never bill for, there is the low background calculation. Where are they likely to be right now. Is this the kind of place. Is it worth the risk of the shortcut. That process runs whether or not you are anywhere near them, and it has been running so long that it now reads as your normal mental weather rather than as a thing you are doing.

Twelve moves in a week, each one under two minutes, produces a week that feels completely ordinary rather than a week dominated by anything, and that is precisely why nobody catches it. There is no bad day to point at. There is no moment where you noticeably failed. There is only a map that has been redrawn so gradually that you never saw an edit.

## What Is the Name for This, and Where Does the Name Stop?

There are three pieces of researched vocabulary here, and together they describe the machinery accurately. Not one of them describes you.

**Avoidance as a maintaining process.** In research on fear, avoidance is treated as the thing that keeps the problem alive rather than as one of its symptoms. The reasoning is simple and unpleasant. Removing yourself from a situation reliably reduces discomfort within seconds, and anything that reliably reduces discomfort within seconds gets learned very fast and repeated automatically. So the behavior that makes today easier is the same behavior that guarantees tomorrow will need it again. The technical framing is that avoidance is a maintainer, and the practical translation is that the eight months are not evidence of a large problem. They are evidence of a small move that nothing ever interrupted.

**The difference between escape and avoidance.** This distinction is old, plain, and much more useful than it looks. Escape ends an encounter that has already begun. You are in the room, you have seen them, and you leave through the side door. Avoidance prevents the encounter from starting, so it produces no event whatsoever. There is nothing to remember, nothing to describe, and nothing to feel bad about afterward. That difference is the reason this pattern grows so large without ever being examined. Escape leaves a story. Avoidance leaves a slightly different Tuesday, and nobody has ever audited a slightly different Tuesday.

**Protection from extinction.** This is the piece that explains the strangest part, which is that ten years of nothing bad happening has taught you nothing at all. The general finding is that a fear expectation gets revised when the predicted bad thing fails to happen while you are present to notice. A protective move removes that opportunity. Lovibond and colleagues demonstrated this cleanly in 2009: people who were given a response that prevented an unpleasant outcome went on expecting that outcome long after people without such a response had stopped expecting it, even though nothing bad had happened to either group. The move preserved the belief by preventing the test. Your route has been doing that for you, every working day, for eight months.

There is a fourth term that sits nearby, **safety behaviors**, which names the small protective moves people carry into situations they do go to: sitting near the door, keeping the keys in your hand, checking who else is coming before saying yes. That vocabulary belongs mostly to a different behavior and it is handled properly on the page about [planning your exit before you arrive](/patterns/needing-an-escape-route/). The distinction worth holding onto is that safety behaviors get you into a room with an insurance policy running. What this page describes is the version where you never go, which is cheaper, more effective, and therefore much more permanent.

Where all four names stop is the same place. They describe the shape of a mechanism with real precision, and they say nothing at all about why this person, or why you. Two people can be running an identical architecture around an identical kind of person, and the thing that would actually move it is completely different in each case. The vocabulary is the what. Nobody has ever found a root by looking it up.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Four situations, and the first one has already been stated at the top of this page because it matters more than anything else here.

If the person has hurt you, threatened you, or put you in fear, this is a safety matter before it is a behavioral pattern, and the people to talk to are a domestic violence service, a licensed therapist, or a lawyer, depending on what the situation involves. Safety planning is specialist work with an established method behind it, and the distance you are keeping is a correct reading of your circumstances rather than a pattern to be reduced.

If the range of places you will go has been narrowing month over month, and the narrowing has spread well beyond anything to do with one person, then what you are looking at is bigger than the frame of this page and it deserves attention on its own terms rather than a week of counting.

If being on a particular street produces a physical response, with your body behaving as though a real threat were present rather than as though a mildly unwelcome meeting might occur, that is worth taking to somebody qualified rather than working on alone. Something is being reactivated and the mechanism described here does not cover it.

And if there is any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a licensed professional now rather than later, and everything else on this page can wait.

## Why Does Every Single Move Look Reasonable on Its Own?

Because on its own, every single move genuinely is reasonable, and this is the part that most descriptions of avoidance get wrong by making the reader sound irrational.

Consider the earlier train in isolation. It is quieter. You get a seat. You arrive with a few minutes in hand. If somebody asked you to defend that choice you could do it comfortably and you would not be lying. The same is true of the further supermarket, which has a better bakery. The same is true of the longer walk to coffee, which is pleasant in summer. The same is true of the declined invitation, on a week when you actually were tired.

Each one survives inspection because each one was chosen against a real alternative and has a real benefit attached. What none of them survives is addition, and addition is the one operation nobody performs, because performing it requires holding twelve unrelated small choices in your head at once and noticing that they all point in the same direction.

There is a second reason the moves resist inspection. They are made below the level where you would call something a decision. A decision has a moment attached to it, a small hesitation, a sense of having weighed something. These do not. The body turns you into the next aisle before any thought arrives. The finger declines the invitation while your attention is on something else. You cannot argue with a choice you did not experience making, and you cannot decide against a move that happened before you were consulted.

That is the real asymmetry in this pattern. The behavior is automatic and free. Any change to it would have to be deliberate and effortful. So the behavior wins every single day, several times a day, for years, without a contest ever taking place.

## What Is the Difference Between Escaping and Avoiding?

It sounds like a distinction for a textbook, and it turns out to be the most practically useful sentence on this page.

| | Escape | Avoidance |
|---|---|---|
| When it happens | After the encounter starts | Before the encounter can start |
| What it produces | An event you remember | No event at all |
| How visible it is | Other people notice | Nobody notices, including you |
| What it costs | One awkward exit | A permanently smaller map |
| What it teaches you | That you can get out | Nothing, because nothing happened |
| How long it lasts | Ends when the evening ends | Runs indefinitely with no review |

Escape has a witness. You left the party early, somebody saw you go, and you thought about it in the car. That experience is expensive enough and memorable enough that most people ration it. They only escape a few times a year, and they know exactly which times.

Avoidance has no witness and no memory. Nothing happened. You simply took a different train and had a perfectly good morning. Because it costs nothing observable, there is no natural limit on how often it runs, and no point at which it triggers a review.

This is why the two behaviors have such different life cycles. People who escape a lot usually know they have a problem, because they have a folder of embarrassing exits. People who avoid a lot usually think they are fine, right up until somebody asks a specific question about a specific shop and the answer arrives faster than they wanted it to.

The other consequence of this distinction is that the escape version gets talked about and the avoidance version does not. Almost everything written about being uncomfortable around somebody assumes you are in the room with them. Very little is written for the person who solved the whole thing years ago by not being in rooms.

## Why Does the Belief Never Update?

Because the thing that would update it has been reliably prevented, by you, several hundred times.

There is a prediction sitting underneath the route, and if you say it out loud it usually sounds smaller than the structure built on top of it. Something like: if I see them I will not know what to say and it will be excruciating and I will think about it for three days. That prediction has never been contradicted. It has also never been confirmed. It has simply been protected, which is a completely different state and a much more durable one.

A prediction that gets tested and fails becomes weaker. A prediction that gets tested and holds becomes stronger. A prediction that never gets tested stays exactly where it was on the day it was formed, indefinitely, and this is the position yours has been in since the spring.

This produces one of the more disorienting features of the pattern. The passage of time does nothing. Ordinarily, a decade takes the edge off almost anything. Here, ten years of successful avoidance leaves the expectation as sharp as it was in year one, because every one of those years was spent producing precisely the absence of evidence. People describe this with real bewilderment: the encounter feels no less impossible now than it did before, despite everything having moved on, despite the original matter being long dead, despite both of them having entirely different lives.

The second thing that happens is generalization. The rule starts specific and does not stay specific. It begins as one station, then the station and the street behind it, then the district, then a category of event, then a whole part of a city. Nobody sits down and extends it. It extends by itself, because a rule that has never been contradicted has no reason to hold a boundary.

Which is how a four minute conversation ends up costing thirty hours a year, a coffee place, a wedding, and a part of town.

## How Good Is the Evidence Behind Any of This?

Weaker than the confident version, strong enough to act on, and the honest account is worth more to you than a tidy one.

The classic story about avoidance comes from a two-step model set out in the late 1940s. Step one, a neutral thing gets paired with something unpleasant and starts producing fear. Step two, the behavior that removes the fear gets reinforced by the relief and so repeats. It is elegant, it fits the eight months of train timetable almost perfectly, and it is widely known within the field to be incomplete. I am describing it without attaching a name to it because attributing a corrected model to its originator is how a page ends up sounding more settled than the evidence is.

The clearest problem with the two-step account is that it predicts avoidance should fade once the unpleasant thing stops appearing, and in practice avoidance is spectacularly persistent, in laboratories and in lives. A second problem is that fear and avoidance come apart much more often than the model allows. People avoid things they are not currently frightened of, and stay frightened of things they walk toward. A model built on fear driving the behavior struggles with both.

What replaced the mechanism story, rather than the observation, is a body of work on how extinction actually operates. Bouton's research on context is the clearest statement of the correction: extinction does not erase the original learning, it lays down a second, competing piece of learning, and that second piece is tied to the context where it was acquired. Which is why a fear that seems to have gone will come back in a different place, after a gap, or under stress. The practical implication for you is unwelcome and worth knowing. One good encounter in one setting does not delete anything. It adds a competing memory, and competing memories can lose.

There is a live disagreement in this literature that popular writing tends to flatten. The strong claim, that any protective move must impair recovery, has been directly challenged, and there are careful studies showing that judicious protective moves do not necessarily impair anything and can make a person willing to attempt something they would otherwise refuse. That disagreement has not been settled. Anyone telling you the science is unanimous here is telling you about their confidence rather than about the field.

Then there is the size of the gap between the evidence and your morning. Almost everything solid in this area comes from laboratory work: mild aversive stimuli, sessions measured in minutes, volunteers, and a controlled setting. Nobody has run an eight month train study, nobody could, and nobody would get it past an ethics board. The direction of the finding transfers well and has for decades. The magnitude, the timescale, and the specific prediction about your particular street after your particular decade are simply not in the evidence, and a page claiming otherwise would be selling you a certainty it does not have.

What survives all of that is modest and useful. Avoidance reduces discomfort now. It prevents the belief from being tested. Untested beliefs persist. That much is about as well established as anything in this area gets, and it is enough to justify counting your week. It is not enough to justify anybody telling you what to do about a specific person.

## Why Do Two People Redraw the Identical Map for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the map is the fruit and the reason is somewhere else entirely, and two people can produce a byte-for-byte identical week from two completely unrelated causes.

Put two people side by side. Both take the earlier train. Both gave up a cafe. Both scan a room in two seconds. Their behavior is indistinguishable and any advice written for one of them will be useless for the other, which is why generic advice about avoidance has such a poor record.

|---|---|---|
| Genuine danger or genuine harm | Protecting you, accurately, from a real risk | You can name a specific thing they did, and it is recent enough or serious enough to still be live |
| An unfinished conversation | Protecting you from a sentence you never said | Your dread is about the first ten seconds of talking, not about the person |
| Shame about something you did | Protecting you from your own reflection in their eyes | The discomfort is aimed inward, and it survives even imagining them being pleasant |
| They became a symbol | Protecting a story about who you are now | You avoid a whole category, not just an individual |
| Accumulated habit | Nothing at all, any more | You cannot name a current reason and the last real one is years old |

The order matters. Check the first one honestly and stop there if it applies. Work down only if it does not.

## What If the Person Actually Harmed You?

Then the whole frame of this page steps back, and it should.

If somebody hurt you, threatened you, stalked you, or made you afraid, the route you built is an accurate response to your actual circumstances rather than a pattern to be examined, produced by a system doing precisely what it exists to do. The eight months of earlier trains are eight months of you protecting yourself competently, and the correct reading of that is competence.

Nothing in the rest of this page applies to that situation. There is no version of this where you should be practicing encounters, testing predictions, or reducing your distance to see what happens. Somebody who tells you to face this is giving you advice built for an entirely different problem and it can do real harm.

What is worth having is support that matches the situation rather than a self-directed exercise. Domestic violence services, licensed therapists working in this area, and legal advocates do safety planning as routine specialist work, and it is more thorough than what a person can build alone: where you go, who knows what, what is documented, what happens in an emergency, what your options actually are. If money or access is the barrier, most countries have free confidential helplines that exist for exactly this call.

One more thing, said plainly. People in this situation frequently feel ridiculous about the size of their arrangement, and compare themselves unfavorably to somebody who seems to be coping better. That comparison is not information. The size of your route reflects the seriousness of what happened and how efficiently your system learned from it, and neither of those is a flaw in you.

## What If There Is a Sentence You Never Said?

This is the most common root by a wide margin, and it is also the most solvable, which is a rare combination.

The structure of it is distinctive. When you imagine running into them, the dread is very specifically about the opening. The first ten seconds. The moment where you both have to decide what register this is in and neither of you knows. What comes after that, in your imagination, is usually vague or fine. It is the entry that is unbearable.

That is a strong indication that the problem is a conversational one rather than a personal one. There is something unresolved that has never been put into words: an apology neither of you made, a version of events you never corrected, a thing they said that you never answered, a friendship that ended without anybody announcing it. The words never got said, so the situation never got closed, so any encounter reopens a file that has no ending.

People in this root often describe having rehearsed the conversation many times. That rehearsal is worth understanding for what it is doing. It is not preparation, and it never converts into the conversation itself, because the rehearsed version always ends well and the real one has no script. Running it again produces a small feeling of progress and changes nothing at all, which is a mechanism covered properly on the page about [rehearsing conversations that never happen](/patterns/rehearsing-conversations/).

Notice what this page will avoid telling you to do. Nobody here is going to recommend having the conversation. Closure conversations are frequently a bad idea, they are often more about the person initiating them, and quite a lot of them make things worse. What is worth knowing is simply that this root exists and that your dread is attached to a sentence rather than to a human being, because that changes what you are actually dealing with and it is much smaller than the arrangement suggests.

## What If You Are Avoiding Your Own Reflection?

This one reverses the usual reading, and people find it hard to see in themselves because everything about it points the wrong way.

Here the discomfort is being caused by the version of you that exists in their memory rather than by them. You did something, or failed to do something, and they were there. While you never see them, that version of you is filed away and mostly quiet. Their physical presence makes it briefly real again, in a way that no amount of private remembering does.

The tell is that the feeling survives all the reassurance in the world. Imagine them being warm to you. Imagine them having forgotten completely. Imagine them being pleased to see you. For most roots, that thought experiment brings relief. For this one it changes nothing, because the reminder was the problem all along rather than their opinion.

This root is often carrying more weight than the person deserves. Somebody who witnessed you at a bad time becomes the custodian of that time, and avoiding them functions as a way of keeping a chapter closed. That works, in the narrow sense that the chapter stays closed. It also means the chapter never gets revised by anything, because you have removed the only person who could have shown you that they remember it differently, less severely, or not at all.

What actually helps here has nothing to do with them and requires no contact whatsoever. The work is on the thing you did, on your own account of it, and on whatever it is you have never let yourself have any relief about. That is slower and less dramatic than a conversation, and it is the only version that holds, because a conversation with them would only ever produce their opinion, and their opinion was never the thing keeping this in place.

## What If They Stopped Being a Person and Became a Reminder?

Some people are not being avoided as people at all. They are being avoided as evidence.

This root has a distinctive shape. The person themselves is fairly neutral. You do not dislike them. You would probably describe them as fine. What you cannot tolerate is what their face does to the distance you have built between an earlier version of your life and the current one. Seeing them collapses that distance instantly and in public, which is why a two second encounter can feel out of all proportion to anything that has ever passed between you.

Everybody in this root recognizes it fast once it is described. The old crowd from a job you were unhappy in. A neighborhood you moved away from for reasons you have never quite said out loud. Somebody from a marriage, an industry, a version of yourself you spent six years leaving. The avoidance usually covers a category rather than an individual, which is one way to tell it apart from the others: you are not managing one person, you are managing an era.

What makes this one expensive is that eras contain a lot of good things too, and the whole era gets excluded together. People in this root routinely lose access to friendships they valued, places they liked, and a chunk of their own history, all as collateral on a rule that was aimed at something quite specific.

The useful move is separation rather than exposure. Work out precisely what the reminder is threatening. Very often it is a story that is doing more work than it can bear, along the lines of that period being entirely a waste, or being evidence about what you are. A story that fragile has to be defended by geography, and stories that need geography to survive tend not to be true.

## What If the Reason Expired Years Ago and the Route Did Not?

This is the quietest root and by a distance the easiest to change, which makes it worth checking before the harder ones.

The route was built for a reason. That reason had a life span. The life span ended. The route did not notice, because routes have no mechanism for noticing anything, and nothing in an ordinary week ever prompts a review.

The test takes about thirty seconds and is unusually clean. Try to name what you are currently protecting yourself from, in the present tense, about this month. Not what happened in 2018. What would actually be bad about seeing them on Thursday. A great many people discover at this point that they cannot answer, or that the answer they produce out loud sounds thin even to them. Some discover the person has moved to a different country. Some discover they are not entirely sure the person still lives there at all.

What keeps an expired route alive is that it costs almost nothing to maintain and nothing ever forces the question. The earlier train is not unpleasant. The further supermarket is fine. A behavior with a very low running cost and no review date can outlive its purpose by a decade without anybody being at fault.

The good news attached to this root is real. When the reason has genuinely expired, the route often comes apart in about a fortnight, with no courage required and no conversation involved. People try the old train once, nothing happens, and then find the whole apparatus was being held up by momentum rather than by anything current. This is the one root where a small experiment is genuinely all it takes, and where the biggest obstacle is usually embarrassment at how long it ran.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, and How Would You Know?

Four questions, and you can answer all of them without contacting anybody.

**Can you name a specific thing they did, and is it still live?** If yes, and it involved harm or fear, you are in the first root and the rest of this page does not apply to you. Go and get proper support instead.

**When you imagine the encounter, where exactly does the dread sit?** If it is concentrated in the opening seconds and thins out afterward, you are probably looking at an unfinished conversation. If it is spread evenly through the whole imagined scene and gets worse when you imagine them being kind, look at the shame root instead.

**Does imagining them being warm to you help?** For the unfinished conversation and the expired habit, it helps a lot. For shame, it changes nothing or makes it worse. For the symbol root, it is irrelevant, because their warmth was never the issue and you know it.

**Is it one person or a whole category?** If your route excludes a district, a crowd, or an entire period of your life, you are in the symbol root, whatever you were about to say about the individual.

And a fifth, worth asking last because it catches the thing people most want to avoid noticing. **When did you last review this?** If the honest answer is never, and if you cannot date the most recent piece of evidence, the habit root is in play regardless of whichever other one is also true. Roots combine. Most people have one dominant and one quietly running underneath.

## What Does a Year of This Cost, Counted Properly?

This is the addition, and it is the part of the page that does the actual work, because almost nobody has ever seen these numbers in one place.

| What gets changed | Typical cost per instance | Rough annual total |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier or later commute | 8 to 30 minutes a day | 30 to 120 hours |
| A shop or venue given up | A few minutes plus a worse option | Small in time, permanent in quality |
| Longer walking or driving routes | 3 to 10 minutes, several times a week | 15 to 40 hours |
| Invitations declined | One evening each | 4 to 15 evenings |
| Checking and scanning | Seconds, dozens of times a week | Uncountable, and constant |
| Reconnaissance conversations | Two minutes and a small dishonesty | Slow damage to a friendship |

The hours matter and they are the least of it. Thirty to a hundred and twenty hours a year is real, and if you told somebody they were spending three working weeks annually on anything else they would want a very good reason for it.

The second cost is the places, and it does not convert into hours. A cafe you liked is not replaceable by a cafe you tolerate. A shorter walk home past a river beats a longer one past a retail park by a margin no spreadsheet records. This category never appears in anybody's accounting because each individual substitution is only slightly worse, and slightly worse, applied across a hundred small choices, is how a life gets duller without anything identifiable going wrong.

The third cost is the invitations, and this one compounds. Declining four things a year for eight years is thirty two absences, and social life is a network rather than a series of events. People stop asking, not out of any resentment, but because asking is a habit that needs occasional reinforcement. Most people who notice this notice it as a mystery, several years late, and never connect it to the reason.

The fourth cost is the one that has no unit at all. The background process. The low continuous calculation about where they might be, which runs during hours you are nowhere near them, on holidays, in other cities. That process has been running long enough that you experience it as your baseline rather than as an activity, which means you have no idea what it costs and no way to compare yourself to yourself without it.

And then there is what all of this does to your account of yourself. Eight months is a fact about a timetable. Eight years produces a quiet belief that you are somebody who cannot handle a chance meeting, and that belief goes on to make its own decisions about entirely unrelated things.

## Why Does Deciding to Stop Avoiding Them Fail So Reliably?

Because the decision is being made in the wrong place by the wrong system.

You cannot instruct an automatic process by resolving harder. The turn into the next aisle happens before deliberation is available. The finger declines the invitation while your attention is elsewhere. The scan completes as you cross the threshold. Whatever you decided on Sunday evening was decided by a part of you that does not get consulted at the moment of action, which is why Monday goes exactly as every previous Monday did.

There is a worse failure mode than simply not doing it. Somebody resolves to go back to the cafe, goes, and does the whole thing braced. They sit facing the door. They scan every few minutes. They stay eleven minutes and leave with a sense of having discharged an obligation. Nothing was learned, because the protective apparatus came along and did its job. Afterward the experience gets filed as not really counting, and the person concludes the problem is deeper than they thought, when what actually happened is that they tested nothing.

The third failure is the advice you have already been given, which is some version of just talk to them. That advice addresses a conclusion you reached years ago. You are not confused about whether an encounter would probably be survivable. Being told the obvious thing one more time produces one more piece of evidence that you are deficient, and precisely no movement.

And the fourth reason it fails is that the plan was aimed at the wrong target. Trying to stop avoiding somebody puts all the weight on the single hardest, most exposed, least controllable moment in the whole pattern, which is a chance encounter you cannot schedule. There is a far smaller move available that nobody suggests, because it does not look like courage.

## What Is the Audit, and Why Is It the Only Move Here You Actually Choose?

Everything else in this pattern happens without you. The audit is the first thing in the whole sequence that requires a decision, which is exactly why it is the one to make.

It works like this. Open a note. Write one name at the top. For seven days, every time you change anything because of that name, add a line. Route, timing, venue, invitation, aisle, glance, question asked of a mutual friend. Next to each line, put the minutes it cost and what you gave up. That is the entire exercise.

Two rules, and they matter more than they look.

**Change nothing while you are counting.** Take the earlier train. Skip the aisle. Decline the thing. The week is a measurement and measurements get ruined by interference. Also, trying to change and count at once gives you a week of failed resolutions instead of a number, and the number is the whole point.

**Do not draft any conversation.** No opening lines, no scripts, no imagining what you would say. That is the pattern's favorite decoy, it feels productive, and it produces nothing. You are counting, not preparing.

At the end of seven days you will have between eight and thirty lines and a total in minutes. Nearly everybody reports the same reaction, which is that no individual line surprises them and the column does. That gap between the parts and the total is the pattern's entire method of concealment, and one page of handwriting dismantles it.

What the audit gives you that nothing else does is a decision point. Until the total exists, there is no moment at which anything gets weighed, because a ninety second cost never triggers a weighing. Once the total exists, you are looking at a real number attached to a real name, and you get to decide what you think about it. You might decide it is worth it. That is a legitimate outcome and considerably better than the current position, which is paying without knowing the price.

If you want a second step after the counting, make it the smallest possible one, and make it about a place rather than a person. Pick the single item on your list you resent most, usually a shop or a route rather than an event, and take it back. Not to prove anything, not to bump into anyone, and with no bracing. Just use the thing you liked. If they turn up, they turn up, and you will handle it about as well as anybody handles anything unexpected.

## Where Else Has the Map Been Redrawn Without You Noticing?

The route around a person is rarely the only route, and the others tend to be built by the same method.

There is the version aimed at a whole category rather than an individual, where a type of gathering, a kind of conversation, or an entire professional setting has been quietly excluded, and each exclusion had its own reasonable explanation at the time.

There is the version aimed at a topic, where certain subjects have been routed around inside conversations for so long that the people closest to you have learned not to raise them, which is described more fully on the page about [what gets left out of every conversation](/patterns/keeping-people-at-a-distance/).

There is the version aimed at a channel rather than a place. Certain calls do not get answered, certain apps do not get opened, certain threads stay muted. The engineering is identical and the medium is different.

And there is the version aimed at nothing in particular any more, where the original target is long gone and the general habit of narrowing has stayed on, so new places get pre-emptively excluded on the basis of a rule whose original subject you would struggle to name.

Recognizing the pattern in one of these is often how people find it in the others. The method is always the same: a cheap move, immediate relief, no review, and a boundary that quietly expands because nothing ever pushes back on it.

## What Does the Easier Version Look Like on an Ordinary Thursday?

Much less dramatic than the imagined version, and that is the useful part.

The scan still happens. You walk into a room and you still know in two seconds who is in it, because that process is fast, old, and not going anywhere. What changes is that the result stops issuing instructions. You register the information and carry on taking your coat off.

The commute goes back to the time it should take. Not as an act of bravery, and usually without any particular moment attached to it. People describe realizing about three weeks later that they have been getting the normal train again and had not noticed switching back.

Two or three places come back onto the map, and they are usually the ones you missed most, which is why they come back first. The cafe. The shorter walk. The Tuesday thing, or at least some of them.

Invitations get answered on the merits. You still say no to plenty of things, and the no is now about whether you want to go rather than about a name three lines down the guest list.

And the encounter, when it eventually happens, is almost universally reported as anticlimactic. Some version of about forty seconds of mild awkwardness, both of you slightly too polite, and then it is over and you are walking to your car thinking about something else. That outcome is so consistent that it is worth stating in advance, with the honest caveat attached: it does not always go that way, some encounters are genuinely unpleasant, and one bad one does not mean the arrangement was correct all along.

What does not change is the preference. You would still rather not run into them. That is allowed. The difference between the current version and the easier one is not the preference. The difference is whether the preference gets to redraw a map.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

Three different speeds, and confusing them is the usual reason people give up in week two.

The counting takes seven days and works immediately. There is no skill involved and nothing to practice. The number exists at the end of the week whether or not anything else has happened.

The routes move next, and they move faster than people expect, in a few weeks rather than a few months, particularly where the habit root is in play. A route with no current reason behind it tends to collapse quickly once somebody actually looks at it. Where a live reason still exists, expect it to move partially: some items come back, others stay excluded, and that is a reasonable settlement rather than a failure.

The root moves on its own schedule and it depends which one you have. An unfinished conversation can sit quietly for years and then resolve in a single unremarkable afternoon. Shame about your own conduct moves slowly and does not respond to reassurance at all. A symbol loosens as the story it threatens gets less load-bearing, which happens over a year or two rather than a month. A live safety situation is not on this list, because it is not the same kind of problem.

Expect the route back under specific conditions. Exhaustion, because everything effortful gets deferred when you are depleted. Any period where you are already avoiding several other things. And the first few weeks after a near miss, which reliably reinstates a rule that had almost dissolved. A return under those conditions is the return of the conditions rather than the loss of everything you built.

One thing that does not require any waiting: the audit works on the first attempt, for everybody, regardless of root. It is the cheapest useful thing on this page.

## What Is Worth Doing This Week, Before You Change Any Route?

One name and seven days of lines, and nothing else at all.

Write the name at the top of a note. You already have it. It arrived within about two seconds of you opening this page and it has been sitting there ever since, which is itself worth noticing.

Then count for a week. Every route, every time, every venue, every invitation, every glance along a row of cars. Minutes beside each one. Change nothing while you count and do not draft a single sentence you might one day say to them.

At the end of the week, add the column. That total is the thing you have never had, and it is the only piece of information in this whole pattern that has been systematically kept from you by the design of the behavior itself.

If what landed hardest here was the arithmetic, the counting will probably be enough on its own and you can stop there. If what landed hardest was something else, the realization that you can do this with everybody except this one particular person, or that the reason expired years ago and you kept going anyway, or that what you cannot be near is your own reflection rather than them, then the thing worth working on sits underneath the route rather than in it.
