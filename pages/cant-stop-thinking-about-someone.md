The conversation lasted about seven minutes. It happened by the bike rack at the end of a training day in March, while you were both waiting for the rain to ease, and it covered the coffee in the building, a road that has been closed for two years, and one sentence about their brother that you were not expecting.

You have been inside those seven minutes since.

Not continuously. It comes and goes, and there are whole afternoons where it does not surface. But it is there when you wake, it is there in the shower, and it is there in the ninety seconds between finishing one thing and starting another. And what it does, mostly, is rebuild. The order of the sentences. What their face did when you said the thing about the road. Whether the pause before they said *you would probably like it, actually* was a pause of the ordinary kind or a pause that meant something.

That sentence has now been examined more thoroughly than anything else anyone has said to you this year. You have tested it against a reading where it meant nothing. You have tested it against a reading where it meant a great deal. Neither reading can be ruled out, which is exactly why it is still running.

Meanwhile you know their schedule. You know they are in on Tuesdays and Thursdays, that they park at the far end of the lot, and that they take the second floor stairs rather than the elevator. You did not set out to learn any of this. It assembled itself, the way information about a person you are pursuing tends to.

And on the Thursday when they were warm to somebody at the far side of the room, and laughed at something you could not hear, there was a drop in your chest that arrived long before any thought about whether you had the standing to feel it.

You know this is disproportionate. You have known it for weeks. You could describe the whole thing to a friend in three sentences and hear how it sounds. The knowing sits there, complete and accurate, and it has never once altered anything.

That gap between knowing and being able to use the knowledge is the actual subject of this page.

## What Is the Name for This, and What Is the Name Actually Worth?

The word is **limerence**, and it is worth having, with the story of where it came from attached, because the story changes how much weight the word can carry.

Limerence describes an involuntary state of intense romantic preoccupation with one specific person. Its features are recognizable: intrusive thinking about them that arrives without being started, a strong and often aching wish for them to feel the same way, a mood that rises and falls with whatever you read into their behavior, a tendency to build them into something more coherent than any real person is, and a sensitivity to any hint of reciprocation that borders on the forensic.

**Where the word came from.** A researcher named Tennov coined it in the mid 1970s. She had interviewed and surveyed several hundred people about being in love, found that a particular pattern kept appearing in their accounts, and concluded that English had no adequate word for it. So she made one. She said plainly that the word had no root and no earlier meaning, that she built it out of nothing on purpose, precisely so that it would arrive without assumptions attached. The work was published in 1979 as a book for general readers rather than as a paper in a journal.

**What the term genuinely captures, and this part is not in doubt.** It captures the involuntariness with precision. It captures that the thinking is about one person rather than about romance generally. It captures the mood dependency, which is the part people find hardest to describe and most humiliating to admit. And it captures something that almost nothing else in the popular vocabulary does: that the state can be enormous and detailed while the actual acquaintance is almost nonexistent, and that these two facts do not contradict each other.

Naming that is a real service. People arrive at this word after months of having no way to describe what is happening to them, and the relief of finding it is not a small thing.

**What the term does not capture, and this is the half the ranking pages leave out.** It names a *what*, and it says nothing at all about a *why*. It describes an experience rather than a condition, it is not a diagnosis, and it is not something a clinician can enter anywhere. Nobody has limerence in the way somebody has an illness. Two people can be in identical states by every feature listed above and be in completely different situations, needing completely different things.

Both of those are true at once. The word is genuinely useful *and* it carries no clinical standing. Most of what you will read insists on one of those and drops the other.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Four situations, and none of them describes the ordinary version this page is written for.

If the one-sidedness has stopped registering as a fact. If you have come to believe that they are signaling to you deliberately, arranging their movements around you, or communicating with you through things only you would notice, that is a different situation from the one described here and it deserves proper attention rather than a week of self-observation.

If you have followed them, gone looking for where they live, or contacted them after being asked to stop. The line here is behavioral rather than emotional, and it is not blurry.

If your work, your sleep, or your existing relationships have visibly narrowed around this across months, with people who care about you saying so out loud, then the narrowing is the thing to examine rather than the fixation, and the narrowing is usually pointing at something larger.

And if there is any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a licensed professional now rather than later, and everything else on this page can wait indefinitely.

For everybody else, and that is nearly everybody reading this, the rest applies.

## Is Anything Actually Wrong With You?

Probably not, and this deserves saying early rather than at the end where nobody reaches it.

Intense preoccupation with somebody barely known is one of the most common experiences human beings have. It shows up across cultures, across centuries, and across every kind of life circumstance. It shows up in people with full social lives, good marriages, and demanding jobs. Most of the great complaints in poetry are about this exact thing, written by people who were not unwell.

What has changed recently is that the internet has attached a clinical vocabulary to it. You can now read, on pages ranking above this one, that limerence is a condition, that it comes in stages, that it correlates with childhood deficits, and that it takes a defined period to recover from. Very little of that is supported. What it does reliably is convert an ordinary and extremely absorbing human experience into evidence that something is broken in you, which is a heavy thing to hand somebody who is already not sleeping well.

So the honest position is this. You very likely arrived here to find out what is wrong with you. There is a reasonable chance that the most damaging thing in this whole situation is that expectation, which has kept you from mentioning any of it to a single person, which has in turn made it feel far stranger than it is.

That does not mean nothing here ever costs anything. One of the five roots below does cost something, and it is worth being able to recognize. But it announces itself in a particular way, and it is not what most people arriving at this page are looking at.

## How Good Is the Evidence Behind the Word Limerence?

Thin, older than it looks, and stated everywhere with a confidence that nothing supports. This is the section where honesty costs something, so it is worth being specific about what is missing.

**There is no accepted measurement instrument.** This matters more than it sounds. A term that cannot be measured cannot be studied comparatively, cannot be tracked over time, and cannot be tested against anything. A handful of measures have been proposed since the 1980s. None was validated at scale, none was adopted by other researchers, and you will not find limerence scores reported across a body of published work, because that body of work does not exist.

**There is essentially no experimental literature.** The original material was interviews and questionnaires. Nobody has manipulated anything, compared groups under controlled conditions, or followed a sample through time in any substantial way under this name. What exists is description, plus a large amount of writing that describes the description.

**The origin sits outside peer review.** The concept entered the world through a 1979 trade book. That is not disqualifying, and plenty of useful ideas arrived that way. What it does mean is that the concept never went through the filtering a journal submission applies, and that the confident restatements you read online are downstream of a book rather than of a literature.

**The stages, timelines and percentages have no source at all.** This is the most straightforwardly checkable claim on this page, and I would encourage you to check it. Follow any of the confident numbers you find. The three-stage model. The percentage of waking hours. The eighteen-month to three-year duration. Every trail either stops at another article repeating it, or stops at informal reports gathered from interviews in the 1970s, quoted with a precision that the original work never claimed.

**And the brain imaging is being asked to carry far more than it can.** Popular writing about this regularly reaches for scans of people newly in love as though they settled the question. The best known of those studies ran on groups in the teens and low twenties. That is a very small sample for imaging work, the results tell you which regions were more active rather than why anything happens, and the reflex of describing a state as *lighting up the reward system* explains nothing while sounding as though it explains everything.

None of this means the experience is imaginary. It means the word is a good description with very little machinery behind it, and that anyone telling you what stage you are in or how long you have left is making it up.

## If the Label Is Thin, What Is Better Evidenced?

Three things, and all three are more useful than the label because they explain rather than name.

**First, romantic love behaves like a motivation rather than like an emotion.** This is the most important shift available to you, and it does more work than anything else on this page. Emotions come and go on their own timescale and do not organize behavior for weeks at a stretch. Goals do. A goal that has not been met stays active, keeps returning attention to itself, and reasserts as soon as attention is free. Bode and Kushnick set this argument out in a 2021 review, drawing together what is known about the psychology, behavior and biology of romantic love, and concluding that it is best understood as a suite of mechanisms with goal-directed features rather than as a feeling that happens to be strong.

Read your own experience against that and it reorganizes immediately. The thinking that arrives before you are awake. The way it resumes the instant a task ends. The fact that distraction postpones it rather than reducing it. None of that behaves like a mood. All of it behaves like an unfinished pursuit, which is what it is.

This also disposes of the deficiency story, which is the frame almost every article uses. The story says the thinking is a symptom of something lacking in you, and that fixing the lack will end the thinking. A goal-directed account explains the same thinking without needing any lack at all. The system is doing what it does when something is wanted and not yet had.

**Second, intrusive thinking is an ordinary and measured feature of early attraction.** Surveys of people currently and recently in love find that the great majority report thinking about the person involuntarily, frequently, and at times they would rather not be. This is close to standard equipment. If you take one thing from this page, it might be that the involuntary thinking itself is not the unusual part of your situation, and that treating it as a malfunction has probably been costing you more than the thinking has.

**Third, the field itself is far less settled than the articles imply.** Berscheid, who spent a career on close relationships, argued that the study of love has been held back by having no agreed taxonomy, with the same word used for states that have almost nothing in common. That is a leading researcher saying the foundations are unfinished. Any page that tells you confidently what category your experience falls into is claiming a precision that the people who do this work do not claim for themselves.

## Why Does the Least Available Person Produce the Most Thought?

Because uncertainty keeps the question open, and an open question keeps calling attention back.

This is the part that feels backward, and it is the single most useful mechanism on the page. The intuitive model is that thinking about somebody tracks how much they like you, so a person who responds warmly should occupy you more. What actually happens is close to the opposite. Clear information, in either direction, closes the loop. A clear yes converts the pursuit into a situation. A clear no converts it into a disappointment. Both end the rebuilding, because there is nothing left to resolve.

Ambiguity supplies no closing information, so the loop stays open indefinitely, and every ambiguous data point gets processed twice: once for the reading where it meant something, once for the reading where it meant nothing.

There is an experiment on this that is worth knowing about and worth being careful with. In 2011, researchers showed young women profiles of men and told them the men had already rated them. One group was told the men had liked them most. One group was told the men had rated them average. A third group was told the ratings could be either. The women in the uncertain condition thought about the men more than either of the other groups, and reported being more attracted to them.

I am naming that finding without naming the researchers, deliberately. All three authors are already cited elsewhere on this site for entirely unrelated work, and stacking a fourth finding onto the same three names would make a thin evidence base look like a broad one. The study is easy to find under its own terms, and the finding itself is well known.

One more thing about it matters a great deal. That experiment was small. The sample was around fifty female undergraduates in a single laboratory, the manipulation was artificial, and it has not been through the kind of large preregistered replication that the last decade has taught us to want before treating a surprising social result as established. Treat it as a good illustration of a mechanism you can check against your own experience, rather than as a proven law.

And you can check it. Think about the last time somebody was unambiguously, uncomplicatedly interested in you. Most people find that the thinking about that person was mild in a way that felt almost disappointing. Then think about the person who was warm on a Tuesday and distracted on a Thursday and gave no reason for either. The difference sits in how much unresolved material each one left you holding, rather than in the people themselves.

Which leads to the most expensive mistake in the whole pattern, and the one most worth interrupting. Intensity gets read as significance. The reasoning runs: I have never thought about anyone this much, therefore this must be more important than anything before it. But if intensity tracks unresolved uncertainty rather than fit, then the strength of the preoccupation is telling you about the information situation and nothing about the person. The least suitable person in the building can produce the most thought, purely by being the least legible.

## Why Is the Fantasy So Detailed When the Real Conversations Are So Thin?

Because you built the detail, and you built it out of what you wanted.

Count what you actually have. Seven minutes by a bike rack. Some observed behavior across a few weeks. Whatever a shared workplace or class or team has shown you about how they treat other people. It fits on a note card.

Now count what you have constructed. You know how they would react to the thing that happened at your mother's last Christmas. You know what their kitchen looks like on a Sunday morning. You have a fairly settled view of what they would be like in an argument, in a crisis, and on a long drive. You know how it would feel to be spoken to by them in a particular way that has never happened.

None of that came from them, because none of it was ever available. It came from you, assembled in the gaps, and the material used to fill the gaps was whatever you most want to be true.

This explains something that otherwise makes no sense. A real conversation with them is frequently a small disappointment. It is shorter than the imagined version, less charged, and they say something ordinary that does not fit the picture. And then, within about twenty minutes, the picture is repaired. The ordinary remark is reinterpreted as reserve, or tiredness, or something meaningful being held back. The construction has more authority than the evidence, because the construction is what you need.

Which is why the useful question is neither *what are they like* nor *do they like me*. The question is what specifically you have built into them. People find this uncomfortable to answer and find the answer immediately clarifying. Write down, in plain nouns, the five things you have imagined about this person that you have no evidence for. Ease. Being properly looked at. A house that is calm. Someone who finds you interesting without you having to be interesting. Not being alone on Sunday.

Then look at that list and ask which of them is about a person at all.

There is a well-conducted piece of research that speaks to this from an unexpected direction. Joel, Eastwick and colleagues applied machine learning to speed-dating data, using everything participants had reported about themselves and about what they wanted, to try to predict who would desire whom. The models could predict how much a given person tended to like others, and how much others tended to like them. What they could not predict, at all, was the specific pairing. Compatibility between two particular people turned out to be essentially unpredictable from either person's account of themselves.

That is a well-powered finding with a clear negative result, and it lands directly on the reasoning most people use here. The conviction that this specific person is uniquely right, built from limited contact and a great deal of construction, is exactly the kind of judgment that does not survive measurement.

## Which Part of This Is Involuntary, and Which Part Is Chosen?

This distinction is the entire workable surface, and almost everything written about this subject blurs it.

**The thinking is involuntary.** You did not decide to wake at ten to five with it already running. You did not choose to reconstruct the seven minutes for the fortieth time while brushing your teeth. Nobody has ever successfully stopped thinking about somebody by deciding to. Instructions to stop are not merely useless, they generally make the thought more frequent, and anyone giving you that instruction has not thought carefully about how minds work.

**The acts around it are chosen.** Every one of these is a decision, made by you, usually in under a second, usually without registering as a decision at all.

- Taking the long way round to your desk so it passes theirs.
- Checking whether the dot beside their name is green.
- Arriving eleven minutes earlier than you need to on the days they are in.
- Reading their name on the sign-in sheet and knowing what time they got there.
- Working out which shared thing you could plausibly bring up on Thursday.
- Steering a conversation with a colleague toward them, to hear their name said by somebody else.
- Rereading the message thread, all four exchanges of it, from the beginning.
- Choosing the seat that faces the door.

None of these is dramatic. Each takes seconds. And each one deposits fresh material into the system, which the thinking then processes for the next several hours. The green dot carries no information about how they feel. What it is, reliably, is a data point that will be examined for two hours as though it did.

This is why the honest version of any advice here is narrow. The thinking cannot be addressed directly and should not be attacked. The acts can be looked at, counted, and changed one at a time, and changing even two of them measurably reduces the amount of material available to rebuild with.

It is also why the standard prescriptions fail, which the section below deals with properly.

## Why Do Two People Think About a Near Stranger for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the preoccupation is the outcome, and what produces it sits underneath, out of view.

Two people describe the identical thing in identical words. Both are thinking about someone they barely know. Both have rebuilt one conversation past the point of usefulness. Both know it is disproportionate and both are unable to use that knowledge.

For the first, this is ordinary attraction running hot in the ordinary way, and it will settle by itself within a few months whether or not anything is done about it.

For the second, it arrived four months after a marriage ended, it is the only thing in the week that reliably produces anticipation, and it is holding a place that something else used to hold.

Same experience. Same words. Entirely different situations, entirely different next steps, and no questionnaire can separate them, because the difference lies in what the preoccupation is doing rather than in what it looks like.

Five versions follow. The first is by far the most common, and it goes first for that reason.

## What If This Is Ordinary Attraction Running Hot?

This is the most likely answer for most people reading this, and it goes first and at length because most readers have arrived carrying a verdict they did not earn.

Early attraction is a genuinely intense state, and it is intense in almost everybody. It produces repetitive thinking, mood swings tied to small signals, idealization, and a physical response to indifference. It does all of this in people whose lives are otherwise entirely in order. The reason this surprises people is that adults rarely talk about it accurately, so each person meets the full force of it believing they are the only one who has ever experienced it at this volume.

In this version, the state is doing what early attraction does. There is nothing underneath it. It needs no explanation and nothing about your life would improve if it stopped.

The tells for this root are specific and reassuringly boring.

Nothing has narrowed. Your work is what it was. You still see your friends and you are actually present when you do. When real information arrives, in either direction, the state responds to it: a genuinely uninterested response deflates it rather than intensifying it, and a genuinely interested response converts it into an ordinary situation with ordinary logistics. It has a natural end and you can imagine the end without dread. And crucially, it moves. The version six weeks from now is measurably milder than the version six weeks ago.

If that is you, there is nothing to work on, and the useful thing this page can do is remove a verdict you were handed by other people's writing. This is one of the most absorbing experiences available to a person, and having it is not a defect.

One honest note attached to this root. Being in it does not make you immune to the fourth version further down. Circumstances change, and a preoccupation that was pure attraction in April can start doing a different job by September without any announcement.

## What If the Uncertainty Itself Is Doing the Work?

This is the version where the pattern of response, rather than the person, is running the whole thing, and it is the most commonly misread root here.

The signature is unpredictability. They are warm and then absent. They initiate and then go quiet for eleven days. There is a stretch where something seems to be happening, followed by nothing, followed by a small piece of attention that resets everything. And the intensity of your thinking tracks that pattern precisely, which is the tell: it spikes after the quiet stretches rather than after the warm ones.

What makes this root expensive is what people conclude from it. The reasoning goes: I have never felt anything like this, therefore this connection is exceptional. If the intensity is being produced by the unpredictability, that reasoning has the causation backward, and it can hold somebody in place for years.

The way to check it is to ask what happens when the ambiguity is removed rather than what happens when they are warm. Imagine a version where they say, clearly and kindly, that they are interested and available, and imagine the following month. Most people in this root notice something they were not expecting when they run that exercise honestly, which is that the imagined month is flat. If certainty deflates it, then certainty was never what you were pursuing.

The other check is historical. Look at the last two or three people who occupied you like this. If all of them were somewhat unavailable, and none of the available people ever produced anything close, then the common factor is the availability rather than the people, and that is a fact about a pattern rather than about any of them.

One further note. The habit of watching for signals belongs to this root more than to any other, and it has its own page. The looking, the counting, the small tally kept and never spoken aloud, are covered properly on the page about [checking the profile of someone you should leave alone](/patterns/checking-their-social-media/), which deals with the act of looking rather than with the thinking it feeds.

## What If They Are Holding a Life You Want Rather Than Being Themselves?

This version explains the strangest feature of the whole pattern, which is that the fantasy is elaborate and specific while the real conversations are thin and slightly disappointing.

Here, the person has become a container. What is actually being wanted is a version of your life, and they are the figure standing in it. This is why the imagined material is so rich in domestic detail and so poor in anything that would require knowing them. You have imagined the Sunday, the kitchen, the way of being spoken to, the ease. You have not imagined how they would handle being contradicted, because that would require information you do not have and would not enjoy.

The tells are worth sitting with.

The fantasy content is disproportionately about scenes rather than about them. When you catch yourself, you are more often somewhere with them than talking to them. Real contact tends to be mildly deflating, and the deflation is repaired quickly by reinterpretation. And the qualities you have assigned to them cluster suspiciously: they are almost always exactly the qualities you feel short of, or exactly what is missing from your current situation.

The useful exercise here is the noun list from earlier in this page. Write the five things you have imagined about them with no evidence, then ask, of each one, whether anything in your actual life currently supplies it. Most people find that at least three of the five have very little to do with a person at all, and a great deal to do with being at ease, being seen, or not spending another Sunday the way the last eleven went.

That is no reason to dismiss the feeling, and dismissing it would be the wrong move. What it is worth is noticing that the thing being pursued may be available through routes that do not depend on one specific person deciding something about you.

## What If This Arrived Because Something Else Became Unbearable?

This is the version where the preoccupation is doing a job, and it is the one where the standard advice does the most damage.

Look at the timing rather than at the person. If the thinking started within a few months of a loss, an ending, a move, a diagnosis in the family, a job that has become intolerable, or a stretch where you were more alone than you have ever admitted, then the timing is no coincidence and it is the most important fact available to you.

Preoccupation of this intensity is genuinely absorbing. While it is running, it occupies the space that the unbearable thing would otherwise occupy. It supplies anticipation to weeks that had none. It gives an otherwise flat Tuesday a shape, because they might be in. That is a real service, delivered reliably, at a moment when very little else was working.

Which is exactly why removing it without putting anything in its place is a poor plan, and why people who force themselves to stop usually find that something worse arrives within a fortnight. The preoccupation was the cover rather than the problem, and taking away a cover leaves you holding whatever was underneath, unchanged and now uncovered.

The tell for this root is what happens in the gaps. If you succeed in not thinking about them for a whole afternoon, and what arrives in the space is not relief but something heavier, then you have your answer. People in this root often report that the fixation feels less like a problem and more like the only good thing currently happening, and that description is accurate rather than dramatic.

Nothing about this makes the feeling less real. Two things being true at once is the whole subject of this page. The wanting is genuine, and it is also carrying weight that has nothing to do with the person carrying it.

## What If This Is a Real Connection That Circumstances Have Made Impossible?

This version deserves a different name from all the others, and giving it the wrong name is a cruelty people commonly perform on themselves.

Something actually happened here. There was real recognition, mutual and mutually acknowledged, and the reason it cannot go anywhere sits outside both of you. One of you is married. One of you is moving to another continent in August. There is a working relationship that makes it impossible. The timing was wrong by two years in a way that cannot be corrected.

What you are carrying in that case is grief, and it should be called that. Grief for something real and small and finished, which had no ceremony, which nobody around you counts as a loss, and which you therefore carry privately along with a second layer of embarrassment about carrying it at all.

The tells are distinguishable from the other four. The material you are rebuilding is real rather than constructed: they actually said the thing, and you are not reinterpreting it. There is no ambiguity to resolve, because you both know. The thinking has a mournful quality rather than a hopeful one. And, tellingly, certainty does not deflate it. Being told clearly that it cannot happen makes it sadder rather than smaller, which is the reverse of the uncertainty root.

Grief runs on its own schedule and does not respond to being managed. What it responds to is being named accurately and, where possible, said out loud to one person. The second layer, the embarrassment about having feelings for someone you barely spent time with, does more damage than the grief and is the part that can be put down first.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, and How Would You Know?

The distinguishing question is different for each, and none of them is about how strong the feeling is.

| The root | The question that separates it | What you would notice |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary attraction running hot | Is it milder than it was six weeks ago? | It moves on its own, responds to real information, and nothing in your life has narrowed |
| The uncertainty is doing the work | What happens in the imagined month where they say a clear yes? | The imagined month is flat, and the last three people like this were all somewhat unavailable |
| They are holding a life you want | Are you picturing scenes or picturing them? | Elaborate domestic detail, thin conversational detail, real contact mildly deflating |
| Something else became unbearable | What arrives in an afternoon where you do not think about them? | Something heavier than relief, and a start date within months of something specific |
| A real connection made impossible | Does certainty make it sadder or smaller? | Real material rather than constructed, no ambiguity to resolve, a mournful register |

The right-hand column is the one to read slowly. Most people find one row lands quite differently from the other four, and that row is the one worth working with.

Two notes on using this. People frequently sit in two rows at once, most commonly the first and the second, and that combination is entirely coherent. And the row that fits can change over a year without any decision being made, which is why this is worth revisiting rather than settling.

## Why Do No Contact, Blocking, and Distraction Keep Failing?

Because all three are aimed at the delivery, and none of them touches what the delivery was carrying.

**Removing contact removes information.** This is the part nobody mentions. Thin information is what the preoccupation runs on, and cutting contact makes the information thinner. People routinely report that the first fortnight after going quiet is the most intensely occupied period of the whole episode, and then draw the conclusion that they have failed at it, when in fact the mechanism performed exactly as it should.

**Blocking works on access, and access was never the engine.** The thinking arrives with no access at all. It arrives while you are asleep, in a building they have never been in, months after the last exchange. Removing the ability to check does remove one of the chosen acts, which is worth something, and it does nothing about the thinking.

**Distraction competes and loses.** An unmet goal outlasts a film. It resumes at the moment the film ends, at exactly the point it stopped, which is the clearest evidence available that this was never a focus problem.

**And being told the feeling is fake is worse than useless.** You are being asked to disbelieve something your own experience reports accurately. The feeling is real. What is uncertain is the picture of the person, and those are different claims. Anybody who collapses them will lose your trust and deserve to.

There is also a quieter reason these attempts fail, which is that they start from a false premise. They assume the preoccupation is the problem. For at least three of the five roots above, it is nothing of the sort, and an intervention aimed at a non-problem feels pointless because it is. For the roots where something does need to move, the target is the small chosen acts and the thing underneath, neither of which is addressed by any amount of blocking.

What actually moves this, in the versions where anything needs to move, has two parts. Counting the chosen acts honestly for a week. And looking at what the preoccupation has been supplying, in plain nouns, then asking whether anything else in your life currently supplies any of it.

## What Does This Actually Cost, If Anything?

For a good number of people, close to nothing, and an honest answer has to start there rather than manufacturing a cost to justify the page.

Where a cost does appear, it appears in specific places and it is countable.

| What gets spent | The ordinary version | The version worth looking at |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Thinking during commutes, showers, and gaps that had no other claim | Losing what a friend told you at dinner because you were reconstructing |
| Time | Minutes assembled from moments that were already idle | Arriving early, routes chosen, evenings arranged around a possibility |
| Decisions | None affected | A job kept, a move declined, or a relationship left unaddressed because of a maybe |
| Other relationships | Unchanged | Somebody real being half-attended to for months without knowing why |
| Self-regard | A private oddity you find faintly funny | A running verdict about your own seriousness as a person |

The last row is the one people underestimate. The preoccupation itself is usually survivable. The daily conclusion that you are pathetic for having it does more measurable damage than the thinking does, and it is entirely an addition. Nothing in the evidence supports it.

And there is one cost that has nothing to do with any of the above, which almost everybody carries: the hiding. Almost nobody says this out loud, so almost nobody discovers how common it is, and the concealment converts an ordinary experience into a private defect. That conversion is done entirely by the silence, and it can be undone the same way.

## Where Does This Stop and Something Else Begin?

Four neighboring patterns get confused with this one, and separating them is worth a paragraph each because the wrong name leads to the wrong work.

**Looking at their profile.** The act of checking, the eleven-second visit, the tally kept privately for months, has its own mechanics and its own page. It is a chosen act that feeds the thinking rather than the thinking itself, and it is covered on the page about [checking the profile of someone you should leave alone](/patterns/checking-their-social-media/).

**Authored fantasy.** If the inner world is built by you, populated by characters, running on music and pacing, and continuing across years as a story you are writing, that is a different pattern with a different name and a different set of roots. The page on [disappearing into daydreams](/patterns/daydreaming/) covers it, including the version where a real acquaintance is rewritten into the plot.

**Bonds with people who broadcast.** Attachment to a podcaster, a streamer, or a performer runs on real disclosure delivered to thousands, and it is usually calm and low-cost. The structural asymmetry is the defining fact there rather than the intensity of the thinking. That is the territory of the page on [feeling closer to strangers than to people you know](/patterns/closer-to-strangers-than-to-people-i-know/).

**Being on the other side of it.** If you are the one supplying occasional warmth to somebody you have no intention of choosing, that is the mirror image of this page and it is dealt with on the page about [keeping someone interested with no intention of choosing them](/patterns/breadcrumbing/). And if the person occupying you is your actual partner and the thinking is about threat rather than pursuit, the page on [jealousy in relationships](/patterns/why-am-i-so-jealous-in-relationships/) is the right one.

What this page owns, and what the others deliberately leave alone, is the involuntary thinking itself: the rebuilding of thin material about a real person you barely know, the mood dependency, and the gap between knowing it is disproportionate and being able to do anything with that knowledge.

## What Does the Settled Version Look Like on an Ordinary Thursday?

Less different from now than people expect, which is the part that tends to disappoint and is worth stating anyway.

They are still someone you notice. If you are in the same building, you will still know when they are in, and there will still be a small lift on those days. That does not vanish and there was never a good reason for it to.

What changes is a handful of small things.

The thinking arrives and does not get fed. It shows up on the walk to the station, runs for a bit, and is not followed by a route decision, a check, or twenty minutes of reconstruction. Unfed, it is much shorter, and the shortening happens without any effort to stop it.

The second layer goes. You think about them and there is no follow-up thought about what kind of person that makes you. That layer was doing most of the damage, and it is the first thing to become available.

Ambiguity stops being processed. Something they say lands as either warm or ordinary, and stops being tested against two readings for a whole afternoon. Most people notice this change about a month after it starts happening, and only in retrospect.

And there is generally something else in the week that has anticipation attached to it, which is the quiet condition for all of the above. As long as they are the only source of anticipation you have, no amount of technique will make any difference.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

It depends entirely on which of the five you are in, and confusing them is why people conclude that nothing works.

If this is ordinary attraction running hot, the timeline belongs to the attraction and not to you. Weeks to a few months, and it will move whether or not you do anything. The only thing worth changing in the meantime is what you think it says about you, and that can change in the time it takes to read a paragraph.

If the uncertainty is doing the work, it lasts as long as the ambiguity does, which is why these can run for years. The thing that ends them is almost always information rather than effort. That is worth knowing so you stop grading yourself on willpower for a situation that was never about willpower.

If they are holding a life you want, this moves as the life moves, and not before. Months, and the movement is indirect: people notice about a year later that the fixation went quiet somewhere along the way, without being able to say when.

If something else became unbearable, the timeline belongs to that thing, and any attempt to work on the preoccupation while leaving the other thing untouched will fail predictably. That is the version where outside help is most likely to be worth it.

If this is grief for a real and impossible connection, it takes what grief takes, which is longer than anybody wants and shorter than it feels at the time. Naming it correctly is most of the available relief.

One thing works immediately in every version, which is separating what arrives on its own from what you choose. That is available today and it does not depend on knowing which root is yours.

## What Is Worth Doing This Week, Before You Change Anything?

Nothing, for seven days, except watching.

Change no behavior. Do not decide to stop, do not go quiet, do not block anything, and do not make any decisions about your life. Just note, each time you notice the thinking has started, three things: roughly what time it was, what you were doing in the minute before, and whether anything you chose to do that day made fresh material available. Walking past. Checking. Arriving early. Steering a conversation. Rereading.

At the end of the week you will have two lists. One is the times, which will mostly be gaps that had no other claim on them, and that list is not actionable and is not supposed to be. The other is the chosen acts, and that list is short, specific, and the entire workable surface here.

Then write down, in plain nouns, what the thinking actually gives you. Anticipation. A reason for Tuesday to be different from Monday. The feeling of being interesting to somebody. A sense that a better life is nearby. Beside each noun, write whether anything else in your life currently supplies it, and be honest rather than generous.

If the list comes back full, you almost certainly have the first root, this will settle on its own, and you can stop reading about it. If most of the nouns have nothing beside them, the thing worth working on is sitting underneath the preoccupation rather than in it, and no amount of managing your behavior toward one person will reach it.
