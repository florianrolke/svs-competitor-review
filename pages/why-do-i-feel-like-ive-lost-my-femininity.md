# Why do I feel like I've lost my softer side?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

The softer side is still running. It gets released where your nervous system reads safety, which is why it shows up with a dog, a niece, or an old friend and goes missing with a partner. What you built instead is a protective posture that never went off duty. Access comes back by testing the threat it was built for, inside the relationship you are withholding it from.
</div>

<div class="identify-box" markdown="1">
These are the sentences that turn up when someone finally says the quiet version of it out loud.

- "I'm about 99% masculine."
- "I think the truth is I just don't let anyone in."
- "Yeah, I'm broken. But I function."
- "I don't want to be stronger."
- "I'm already bored of being strong."

Sentences like those describe a posture running at full power and charging you the exact closeness it was built to protect.

</div>

A partner reaches for your hand across a restaurant table and your hand is already busy. It picks up the phone, or turns the fork over, or delivers the joke that arrives half a second before the moment would have.

Nobody at the table notices. You do, about a week later, on the morning you register that you corrected, redirected, and improved three of his decisions before lunch, and that none of it felt like control while you were doing it.

Then there is Sunday, which is the part that makes no sense. You were completely unguarded with a niece, or a dog, or a friend you have known since you were nineteen. Silly, physical, easy, in a way you have not been with the person you are dating in months, and it cost you nothing.

Here is the flat version, before any of the explanation. The capacity is intact. It is being withheld from one place in particular by a system doing its job correctly, on evidence that may be thirty years old and may not be current.

People arrive at a page like this having typed something first-person and slightly ashamed. *I lost my femininity.* *I had to be the strong one and now I can't stop.* *Why can't I be soft with a partner.* Almost nobody types a symptom list, because this arrives as a loss of self rather than as a symptom, noticed late, and usually in a restaurant or a kitchen rather than in a doctor's office.

One plain line before the rest of it. If what is keeping you hard right now is a live situation where you are being hurt, controlled, or frightened, that belongs with a licensed professional and with people who can help you get somewhere safe, and the next section says more about where that boundary sits.

If you came here from the childhood end of this, [what happens when you had to grow up too fast](/patterns/i-had-to-grow-up-too-fast/) covers the origin story in far more detail than this page will. If the sharper edge of it is being seen at all rather than being soft specifically, [the fear of letting anyone see the real thing](/patterns/why-am-i-afraid-to-be-vulnerable/) is the closer fit, and [fear of intimacy](/patterns/fear-of-intimacy/) covers the version where closeness itself is what triggers the retreat. This page is about one narrower thing: a warmth that demonstrably still works, aimed everywhere except the relationship you most wanted it in.

## When Does This Belong With Someone Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Right here, near the top, because this line matters more than every practice further down put together.

If the reason you cannot soften is that softening is currently unsafe, no amount of inner work is the right tool. That includes a relationship where warmth gets restored the moment you back down and withdrawn the moment you assert something, where silence is used to make you smaller, or where another person controls your money, your movements, or who you are allowed to see. Those situations need a licensed professional and people who can help you get somewhere safe. A page cannot help with them, and a week away will not either.

The same holds from the inside. If you are carrying an addiction, a diagnosed condition you are currently managing, a hopelessness that has not lifted for months, or any thought of harming yourself, however quickly it passes, bring in a clinician first. Nothing here is a diagnosis and nothing here treats anything. It describes a pattern and what tends to move it.

There is a third boundary worth naming, because it gets missed. Softening toward a partner who is genuinely unkind, or who would use the softness against you later, is a different project from the one on this page, and doing it in the wrong order costs a great deal. Work out which situation you are in before you work on yourself. [Whether to stay at all](/patterns/should-i-leave-my-relationship/) is its own question and deserves to be asked on its own terms.

## Is Your Softer Side Gone, or Is It Being Withheld?

Withheld, in almost every version of this that anyone describes out loud, and the test takes about ten seconds.

Name one living being you are completely unguarded with. A child. A dog. A grandmother. A friend from school who has seen you at your least impressive and stayed anyway. If a name arrives, the capacity is present and running, and every claim about it having drained away is contradicted by your own last week.

The instinct at this point is to file that relationship as a special case. He is a child, that does not count. It is a dog, obviously that is different. That filing is the single most expensive move available, because the safe relationship is the strongest evidence you have, and dismissing it as an exception leaves you with a story about being broken and nothing to check it against.

Two other things get confused with this constantly, and they sit at opposite ends of the spectrum.

| What it feels like | What it might actually be | The thing that tells them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Something in me broke and the soft part is gone | A capacity gated by relationship, working normally everywhere it feels safe | Whether warmth still arrives, unbidden, with anyone at all |
| I feel nothing, about anything, with anybody | [Emotional numbness](/patterns/emotional-numbness/), which is global by definition | Numbness does not spare one relationship while flattening every other |
| I have nothing left to give anyone | [Emotional exhaustion](/patterns/emotional-exhaustion/), a supply problem rather than a gate | Rest changes one of these considerably and leaves the other exactly where it was |
| I don't recognize the person I have become | [A self that went out of use](/patterns/i-dont-know-who-i-am-anymore/) across a decade of one role | Whether the missing part reappears on holiday, or with old friends, or half asleep |
| I am just a masculine person, that is my nature | A posture that generalized past the domain it was built for | Whether it drops instantly, without effort, in one specific low-stakes relationship |

The selective quality is the whole diagnosis, and it is good news that reads as bad news. A gate can be re-examined. A trait that eroded cannot, which is exactly why so many people prefer the erosion story without noticing they have chosen it.

## What Does This Sound Like From the Inside?

Blunter than most descriptions of it, and considerably funnier, in the way that very tired people are funny.

The sentences below are how this actually gets said, at the level it runs, rather than smoothed into a description of a mood. Read them slowly. What matters is whether one of them lands word for word, with no translation required.

- "Well maybe I'm the weak one."
- "I didn't associate a feminine or a masculine. I associated power."
- "My mother was weak, totally weak."
- "I can blame my mother for teaching me polarity and that there's strength in weakness."
- "It was the first time in years that I actually felt my heart again, so I know I have a heart now, and I now know that I have a soul."

Notice what the middle three have in common. Softness gets filed under weakness in all of them, and weakness gets filed under danger, usually with one specific person attached to the memory. When a child watches an adult be soft and be destroyed for it, the lesson she takes has nothing to do with femininity. The lesson is that this particular setting gets you hurt, and the child files it accordingly and moves on with her life.

The last sentence is the one worth sitting with. Someone saying that has just had contact with a part of herself she had assumed was gone, and the phrasing gives away how completely she had written it off. *So I know I have a heart now.* That is what a gate opening sounds like from the inside. Something that had been shut simply got let through.

## Where Did the Hardness Actually Come From?

From conditions where softness was unsafe, unrewarded, or simply unavailable, and it was built by a version of you who was doing exactly the right thing at the time.

Four routes keep recurring. Most people recognize one clearly and half of a second.

1. **A parent who wanted a different child.** Approval was available for winning and unavailable for anything else, so control and performance became the price of love rather than a preference. The child who learned this does not experience it as a strategy. She experiences it as the way the world works, because inside that house it genuinely was.
2. **A household or a culture that treated girls as expendable.** Where being female already reads as a deficit, relentless competence stops being ambition and becomes a survival requirement. Being twice as good was a rate quoted to her early, and she paid it every year without renegotiating once.
3. **An absent or overwhelmed parent, and a child who filled the gap.** Self-reliance arrived before it was chosen, along with responsibility for a household, a sibling, or a parent's emotional state. That specific route has its own page: [what parentification costs later](/patterns/parentification/) covers what happens to an adult who was never allowed to be a child.
4. **A real period of unsupported responsibility.** Solo parenting. Financial survival. Running a business or a household with nobody to hand any of it to. This one is different from the other three, because it happened to an adult who had already grown up, and it hardened her anyway, correctly, because the conditions demanded it.

Every one of those worked. That is the part that makes this stubborn. The posture solved a genuine problem under genuine constraints, and it kept solving it, and nobody ever sent a letter announcing the conditions had changed. This is why arguing with it fails and always will. You cannot talk somebody out of a strategy that has an unbroken record of success.

## Why Does the Posture Keep Running Once the Danger Has Passed?

Because it still pays, every single day, in currencies that are easy to count.

It wins at work. It keeps people at a distance you can manage. It means never again being found insufficient by anyone whose opinion could reach you. Those are real returns, arriving reliably, which is why the honest version of this conversation starts by crediting the posture rather than diagnosing it.

What it does badly is stay in its lane. A setting built for one specific negotiation, usually with one specific person, gets applied to everybody who comes afterward, including a partner who was never that person and never made that demand. And it forecloses the two things most people actually want out of a relationship: being fully known, and receiving love rather than only supplying it.

D.W. Winnicott, writing around 1960, put a name on this shape long before anyone was searching for it online. He described a true self and a false self, the second built to satisfy an environment's demands, compliant and performance-oriented, and convincing enough that it gets mistaken for the whole person. That is the clinical ancestor of a distinction worth making plainly here: the hard, controlling mode is a costume that has been worn for so long it stopped feeling like one.

Two things reveal it as circumstantial. The first is contrast. Put a genuinely strong, genuinely safe presence in the room and the posture visibly loosens within minutes, which is not what happens to a personality trait. The second is the low-stakes relationship, where it drops instantly and without any decision being made. Most people never get either contrast, so the costume goes unexamined for decades and eventually gets introduced at parties as who I am.

There is a version of this that deserves more respect than it usually gets. Toughness earned through genuine necessity is not a defect anyone should be embarrassed about. A woman who survived a year with no backup built something real, and it is hers. The only claim being made here is narrower: a strategy built for a specific set of conditions kept running for years after the conditions ended, which makes it a strategy with an expiry date rather than evidence about her nature.

## What Is It Costing You Right Now?

More than it looks like, and almost all of it in places that will never generate a complaint.

| What it looks like from outside | What it is actually doing | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| A run of relationships, none lasting | Keeping every relationship shallow enough that losing it stays survivable | Each one ended around the same month, for a reason that sounded specific each time |
| Choosing someone steady and undemanding | Selecting a partner who cannot fully claim you or easily leave | You knew, quite early, that he would not go |
| Going quiet when something hurts | Running a loyalty test with no pass condition attached to it | His matching withdrawal gets read as proof he was never serious |
| Being wonderful with the dog and clipped with him | Rationing warmth to the one relationship where rejection carries no cost | You can hear the difference in your own voice within one hour |
| Moving cities, changing jobs, staying mobile | A protective habit narrated as a chosen way of living | The word restless does a lot of work in your own account of yourself |
| Doing more, earning more, taking on more | Answering a feeling of being under-loved with escalating competence | The more you do, the less met you feel, and this never gets questioned |
| Losing respect for a patient partner | Reading his tolerance as inferiority | The patience you selected for is the exact quality you now hold against him |

Read the right-hand column for the one that made you wince. That is usually the accurate one.

The item most people argue with is the fourth. Rationing feels like nothing at all from the inside, because you are not aware of withholding anything. You are just being normal, and separately, you happen to be delightful with your sister's kid. Put the two in the same sentence and the rationing becomes visible in about four seconds, which is why it is such a useful sentence to say out loud.

## Why Does the Standard Advice Slide Straight Off?

Because it treats softness as a switch and the switch is not what is broken.

Search this topic and you get a fairly consistent prescription: reconnect with your feminine energy, dance, wear something flowing, do less, let him lead, practice receiving. Three specific things go wrong with it.

1. **It aims at the wrong layer.** Softness is a risk-gated capacity, so a practice performed in isolation never touches the assessment doing the gating. You can complete every exercise on the list on Saturday and brace at his hand on Sunday, because your system is reading the actual relationship rather than obeying an instruction.
2. **It recreates the original dynamic.** Being told to perform a role correctly in order to be approved of is precisely the arrangement that built the posture in the first place. Run it again and you get compliance, delivered competently, with the same person watching herself from outside to check she is doing it right. Compliance is the opposite of what was being aimed at.
3. **It skips the credit step.** Telling a woman her hardness is a problem to fix, without first naming what it accurately protected her from and why building it was intelligent, lands as an attack on her competence. What comes back is a defense, immediate and well-argued, and the unwinnable test she runs on partners gets run on the advice instead. Nothing moves, and the advice was probably half right.

The order matters more than the content here. Credit first, then question. Skipping the first half is why so much of this material bounces off exactly the women it was written for.

## Does Any of This Have to Do With Being a Woman?

Less than the phrasing suggests, and the research on that is old, public and checkable.

In 1974 Sandra Bem, at Stanford, built an inventory that measured masculine-coded and feminine-coded traits as two independent scales rather than as two ends of one dial. People scored high on both. That finding is the direct rebuttal to the version of this question that assumes a slider: if the traits were never opposite ends of the same axis, then gaining competence never cost you warmth, and no amount of decisiveness was ever spending down a finite reserve of softness. Whatever happened to your access, it was not a trade.

Alice Eagly's social role theory, developed from 1987 at Northwestern, makes the second half of the point. A large share of the behavioral differences people attribute to gender traces to the roles men and women actually occupy and what those roles demand. Put anyone at all in the role of single parent, primary earner, or first-in-family achiever and the role will produce control-coded behavior, because the role requires it. That describes what a job does to whoever holds it, and it says nothing whatsoever about who women are.

Which is why the essentialist framing gets abandoned on this page. *I am 99% masculine* is a sentence describing a posture, wearing the language of identity. The posture is workable. An identity is something you have to defend, and defending it is how twenty years go by.

## Why Is There One Relationship Where It Still Runs Freely?

Because your nervous system scores every relationship separately, and one of them already passed.

Stephen Porges has argued since 1994 that access to calm, socially engaged states depends on how the nervous system reads safety cues in a given context, rather than on a decision made at the top. His model is debated among researchers. The observation it organizes is not in dispute by anybody who has lived it: warmth is fully available in one room and unavailable in the next, and no amount of willpower crosses the gap.

Look at what the safe relationship has in common, whichever one yours is.

- Nothing is being evaluated. A dog has no opinion about whether you were impressive this year.
- There is no possibility of the specific rejection you are guarding against. A six-year-old niece is not going to decide you are too much and leave.
- Nothing you show can be used later. That is the quiet one, and for many people it is the load-bearing one.
- The history is settled. A friend of twenty-five years already saw the worst version and stayed, so the question has an answer.

Your partner fails all four, structurally, by being a partner. He can evaluate. He can leave. He can use it. And the history is still being written. So the score comes back differently and the gate stays shut, which is a system applying its own rules correctly to different inputs while looking, from outside, like a personality that keeps contradicting itself.

The useful consequence: the gate opens on evidence rather than on effort, which narrows the target considerably. One relationship, and the specific belief that keeps scoring it as unsafe.

## Is Gripped Strength Actually Stronger Than Relaxed Strength?

No, and the reverse holds under real load, which is counterintuitive enough that most people have to feel it rather than be told it.

Strength maintained by constant contraction restricts its own capacity. It feels like trying hard, because it is, and effort is the most misleading signal in the whole area. Strength that can move, soften, absorb and still hold is quieter from the inside and holds far longer, which is why the more effortful state is the more fragile one.

| Under real pressure | Strength held by gripping | Strength that can move |
|---|---|---|
| What it feels like from inside | Effortful, braced, correct | Quieter, and easy to mistake for not trying |
| What happens in a normal week | Holds, and looks impressive doing it | Holds, and nobody notices |
| What happens the week everything lands at once | Fails suddenly and completely | Bends, absorbs, comes back |
| What it costs to maintain | Constant, whether or not anything is happening | Occasional, in proportion to the actual load |
| What other people feel standing near it | Managed | Met |
| Where it shows in the body | Jaw, shoulders, a held breath you stopped noticing | Available, and slower |

The last row is where this stops being theoretical. Someone can describe herself as fully integrated and completely at peace, and a few direct questions about what she does when things go against her will show up in her body before her answer arrives. That is worth knowing without shame attached. The body is honest earlier than the account is, and it is on your side.

Burnout is the predictable end of the left-hand column rather than a separate misfortune. Constant contraction has a running cost, paid whether or not anything is happening, and eventually the account is empty on an ordinary Wednesday for no visible reason at all.

## Can Love Be Completely Real and Still Not Land?

Yes, and this is the most useful sentence on the page for anyone who has been told she is cold by somebody she would take a bullet for.

Care, loyalty, effort and sacrifice can all be entirely genuine and still arrive as absence, because the person receiving them experiences the delivery rather than the feeling behind it. Love delivered as correction lands as criticism. Love delivered as logistics lands as management. Love delivered without kindness attached lands as pressure, and the person on the receiving end reports, accurately, that he does not feel loved, while you sit there having reorganized your entire life around him.

The mistake underneath is assuming love is self-evident to the person it is aimed at. Love gets inferred, entirely, from how it arrives, which makes the delivery mechanism the whole thing rather than an optional finish on top of it. [Why real affection sometimes fails to register at all](/patterns/feeling-unloved/) covers the receiving end of this, and it is worth reading whichever side of the exchange you are on.

Two questions make this concrete tonight. What does the person you love actually experience when you express it? And do you know what specifically makes him feel loved, in his terms rather than in yours, or have you been delivering yours at volume for years and wondering why the room stayed cold?

## Why Does Needing to Matter Most Make Closeness Harder?

Because significance and intimacy compete for the same seat, and only one of them can sit there.

If being singularly important sits at the top of what you need, partnership becomes structurally difficult regardless of how much love is present, because partnership requires treating somebody else as singularly important rather than only being seen that way yourself. More achievement should intuitively produce more calm and more felt love. Instead it runs in a self-sealing loop, where no quantity of external proof reaches an internal, relational fear. [The need to feel important](/patterns/need-for-significance/) has its own page, and it is the one to read if that paragraph stung.

The version that hides best is significance relabeled. Someone reorders her priorities toward growth and contribution, genuinely means it, and every action she offers as evidence still cashes out as being admired, sought out, or first. The label changed. The need underneath did not, and renaming a priority is not the same as having resolved it.

The tell is in what the pain is about. *He has never once joined me in any of this* can mean I miss him, or it can mean this proves I am not good enough and not loved. The first is about him. The second is about you, and it will not be resolved by anything he does, because the loop was never taking his behavior as input.

## What Does It Mean If a Partner Stays No Matter How You Treat Him?

Less than it seems to, and reading it correctly is uncomfortable in both directions.

A partner who reliably stays while being kept at a distance may be staying because leaving is genuinely beyond him. That is information about his resources and his resolve, and it is not a verdict on your value, your power, or how loved you are. Persistence intuitively reads as devotion. It can equally mean he was selected, consciously or otherwise, for an inability to go.

This lands hard, so hold both halves at once. It does not mean he loves you less. Plenty of people who cannot leave also love enormously, and plenty of steady partners simply chose you and keep choosing you, which is a different thing entirely and worth being able to tell apart. What it does mean is that his staying is a poor instrument for measuring anything about you, and if his staying has been carrying that job, the answer it gives you will always be unsatisfying, no matter how many years it repeats.

The related move is reading his patience as weakness. Watch the sequence, because it is neat and slightly grim: the quality that made him safe enough to choose becomes the quality you lose respect for, and losing respect justifies more distance, which requires more patience from him, which lowers your respect further. Nobody in that loop is behaving badly. It just runs downhill on its own.

The way out of it starts with a question rather than a decision. Ask him what the distance actually costs him. Then stay quiet long enough to hear an answer you may not enjoy.

## What Actually Moves It?

Three steps in a specific order, run over months, in one relationship rather than in general.

Before the sequence, the polarity, because a page describing only the hard end leaves you with an accurate account of a problem and nowhere to walk.

| The protective version | The other end of it | What it looks like on an ordinary Tuesday |
|---|---|---|
| Correcting a decision before he has finished describing it | Letting a decision stand | He picks the restaurant, and it stays picked |
| Warmth rationed to the one safe relationship | Warmth spent where it costs something | You are as silly with him as you are with the dog |
| Going cold and waiting to see whether he follows | Saying the thing on the day it happens | I felt far away from you last night, said on Tuesday about Monday |
| Answering nothing while planning your exit from the conversation | Naming the actual state | I'm upset, and I don't want to talk about it yet |
| Doing more when you feel under-loved | Asking to be met | You request the specific thing instead of earning it sideways |
| Strength held by gripping | Strength that can move | You cry at the wedding and stay in the room |
| Reading his patience as weakness | Reading it as a choice he keeps making | You ask what it costs him, and you listen to the answer |
| Independence as proof you cannot be hurt | Independence you actually chose | You accept help on Tuesday and mention it Wednesday |

Now the sequence. The order is the active ingredient.

1. **Credit the posture before asking it to change.** Name where it came from, what it protected, and what it got right. Out loud or on paper, in specific terms rather than general ones. Richard Schwartz's Internal Family Systems model, developed from the 1980s onward, treats a controlling or guarded part as a protector that took the job for a real reason and can be worked with rather than removed, and that framing is doing something practical here rather than being decorative. A protector that gets credited will negotiate. A protector under attack digs in, and every defense it mounts will be intelligent, because it was built by you.
2. **Use the safe relationship as proof.** Name it explicitly. Say the sentence: with him I am completely soft, and with my partner I am not. That single comparison removes the most common exit available, which is I don't have it in me. You do. The evidence walks past you every morning wanting breakfast.
3. **Test the actual belief, with the actual person.** This is the part everybody skips. A belief like he is too weak, or he'll leave eventually, or he cannot handle the real me, survives indefinitely as long as it is only ever examined privately. It dies, or gets confirmed, in about ten minutes of real conversation. Pick the smallest true thing you have been withholding and say it. Not the whole history. One sentence, delivered without a joke attached and without checking his face for the verdict.

Two dynamics will surprise you along the way.

The first is that removing the pressure moves things faster than applying it. Being told plainly that nothing has to change, that you may keep living exactly as you have been, tends to produce more movement than any amount of insisting, because resistance needs an external target to push against. Take the target away and the only remaining reason to act has to be generated internally, which is the only kind that ever holds.

The second is that setbacks arrive on schedule and mean less than they appear to. Under stress almost everybody defaults to their hardened mode, whatever their natural setting. So a bad fortnight where you go cold, control everything and say nothing true is the pattern running, on time, in the conditions it was built for. Don't judge yourself for it. Judge the trend across a season instead.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

Months, measured as a different default becoming visible in one relationship, and it is rarely a straight line.

Recognition arrives quickly. Most people can catch the reflex within a few weeks of paying deliberate attention, and catching it counts even on the days you go ahead and do it anyway. Watching yourself deliver the deflecting joke while knowing exactly what it is for feels like failure and is genuinely a step forward.

The reflex itself takes considerably longer, because it fires before conscious thought does. The realistic progression runs: noticing an hour afterward, then twenty minutes afterward, then during, then early enough that a choice exists. Each of those transitions needs a stack of real instances rather than imagined ones, and real instances only occur in real relationships on days that actually mattered.

Is any of it comfortable? No. It takes real, freaking persistence to say one soft true sentence on an ordinary Tuesday to somebody who has watched you be impressive for three years, and to keep saying them through the stretch where nothing visibly improves. That difficulty is the mechanism rather than a sign of failure. The capacity being rebuilt is precisely the tolerance for being seen without a defense up, and tolerance only grows against something.

One more honest note about scope. This is a genuinely different project from softening toward a partner who is unkind, unsafe, or who would take the change as an opening. If that is your situation, the work described here is the wrong work in the wrong order, and the previous section on professional support is the part of this page to reread.

## Where Does This Thinking Come From?

From five public bodies of work, every one of which can be checked without going through me.

Sandra Bem, at Stanford in 1974, built the inventory that measured masculine-coded and feminine-coded traits as independent scales and found people scoring high on both, which is the checkable basis for refusing the single-dial framing of this whole topic. D.W. Winnicott, writing for the British Psychoanalytical Society around 1960, described a true self and a compliant false self built to satisfy an environment's demands, which is the clinical ancestor of the costume distinction used here. Richard Schwartz's Internal Family Systems model, developed from the 1980s onward, supplies the protector part and the practice of crediting a defense before asking it to stand down. Stephen Porges, from 1994 onward at Indiana University and later the University of North Carolina, argued that access to calm, socially engaged states depends on the nervous system's read of safety cues in a given context. His model is contested among researchers and I would rather say so than pretend otherwise. Alice Eagly's social role theory, developed from 1987 at Northwestern, holds that much of the observed difference in men's and women's behavior traces to the roles they occupy and the demands those roles place on them.

The rest of it is mine and carries no research weight at all. I spent five years and more than five thousand hours doing this kind of work on myself before I built anything around it, and more than fifty thousand dollars on seminars, therapy, books and teachers, some found in the jungles of Vanuatu, some in the jungles of Peru. I hold no clinical qualification of any kind. None of the researchers named has any connection to this page or to the seminar, and where their published work and my own experience disagree, theirs is the one to go and check.

## What Is One Thing to Do Today, Before You Decide Anything About Anybody?

Open a note on your phone and write three lines, in this order, and let the order do the work.

First line: the era the hardness was built in, and what it correctly protected you from, stated as plainly and factually as you can manage. No self-criticism in it. Something closer to *between nineteen and twenty-six there was nobody to hand anything to, so I stopped asking.* Second line: the name of the one living being you are still completely unguarded with. Just the name. Third line: one true sentence you have not said to your partner, specifically because saying it would require you to be soft while you said it.

Do not send the third line. Write it, look at it, and notice how small it is compared to the machinery keeping it unsaid. Most people expect something enormous and find a sentence of nine words. That gap, between the size of the sentence and the size of the defense around it, tells you more in five minutes than another year of thinking about this will. It costs you nothing and it works whether or not you ever go anywhere with anyone.
