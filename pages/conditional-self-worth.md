# Why is my worth tied to what I produce?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Because worth was made purchasable early, and output became the currency. In this pattern worth is genuinely present and genuinely felt, and it only shows up while you are delivering, then drains between deliveries. That is what separates it from low self-esteem, which stays flat regardless. It loosens when you collect enough direct evidence that nothing collapses on the weeks you produce nothing.
</div>

<div class="identify-box" markdown="1">
You will know quickly whether this page is about you. These are the lines it says in the first person.

- "I am only as valuable as my latest accomplishment."
- "I'm only worth something when I'm being productive."
- "If I'm not working hard, I feel worthless."
- "People only value me for what I can achieve."
- "I'm nothing without my achievements."

The cost is a valuation of yourself that rises and falls with the scoreboard and drains on every week that produces nothing to show.

</div>

Somebody tells you the project came out well, and before they have finished the sentence you are already listing what is still wrong with it. The correction arrives on its own, ahead of any decision to make it.

If that one landed, some of the rest will too. You take a sick day and spend part of it drafting the emails you will need to send once you are back, because resting empty-handed feels unearned. You tell a parent about the promotion and they ask what is next, the same evening, and you notice you were already three steps into answering that question before they asked it. You scroll past a friend's celebratory post and find yourself checking your own week's output to see whether you are keeping pace. Something finally ships and what arrives is a flicker of dread rather than relief, because the version of you that mattered was the one still working on it. Somebody asks what you do for fun and you go blank, because almost everything filling your time can be justified as productive if anybody asks.

One plain thing before the rest. If what you are carrying includes hopelessness that has not lifted for months, an exhaustion that sleep no longer touches, or any thought of harming yourself however briefly it passes, that belongs with a doctor or a crisis line today rather than with a page like this one. There is a fuller version of that boundary a few sections down.

Everything below is written for the everyday version: the compliment that got corrected, the sick day that got worked through, the promotion that felt flat. What makes this particular pattern so hard to spot is that the worth is real. You do feel valuable. You feel it most clearly on the days you deliver, which is why the arrangement reads from the inside as simply being a person who cares about doing things properly.

## What Does Conditional Self-Worth Sound Like From the Inside?

It sounds like a running valuation of yourself, updated continuously against your most recent output, stated so flatly that it stopped registering as an opinion decades ago.

The sentences below are the lines people run in their own heads, in their own phrasing rather than a tidied-up version. They recur with an unsettling consistency across people who have never met, who work in completely different fields, and who would describe their lives as having nothing in common. That consistency is the first clue that this got installed rather than chosen:

- "My worth is defined by my achievements."
- "I need to prove my worth constantly."
- "My value depends on others' approval."
- "I always need to be doing something productive, or I feel worthless."
- "I'm only loved or valued for what I achieve."
- "My sense of self is tied to how much I can accomplish."
- "I'm defined by my accomplishments, not by who I am."

Read them slowly. What matters is the small physical jolt when one of them turns out to be yours word for word, in your own phrasing, with no translation required.

Notice how many of them read as work ethic. Several would be said out loud in a job interview and be received as a strength. That is a large part of why this can run for thirty years unexamined, and why the people around you have been rewarding it the entire time. Nobody goes looking for help with being reliable.

Notice also what the sentences are measuring. Almost every one of them issues a verdict on your value as a person, priced against your output and recalculated on a schedule you never agreed to. Very few say anything at all about whether you can actually do the job. That distinction turns out to be the whole map, and it is where most writing on this subject gets lost.

There is a last line worth sitting with separately, because it points at something quieter than any of the others: "I'm defined by my accomplishments, not by who I am." Plenty of people carrying this pattern cannot answer the question of who they are outside what they produce, and the reason is structural rather than a failure of self-knowledge. There has never been a version of them that was not being evaluated, so there is nothing else to consult.

## When Does This Belong With a Professional Rather Than a Page Like This?

Right here, before any of the practical material, because this boundary matters more than every technique below it put together.

If the pressure has turned into hopelessness that has not lifted for months, if you are having any thought of harming yourself, or if you are managing a diagnosed condition, that belongs with a doctor or a licensed professional rather than with a self-directed practice. Contact a crisis line today if that is where you are. Nothing on this page substitutes for a person in the room.

Two less obvious situations belong there too. If the driving is running your body into the ground, months of six-hour nights, a stomach that has stopped working properly, an exhaustion that a weekend no longer dents, that is a medical matter first and an insight matter second. And if somebody in your life right now is actively producing this by keeping your standing conditional, a manager who withdraws contact when the numbers dip or a partner whose warmth tracks your usefulness, that is a live dynamic rather than an old pattern, and it usually needs support from people who can help you handle it or leave it. [The exhaustion side of this has its own page](/patterns/emotional-exhaustion/), and asking for help in month one costs far less than asking in month twelve.

## Is Your Worth Low, or Is It Conditional?

Conditional, if it moves. The single most useful distinction on this page is between worth that sits flat and low regardless of what you do, and worth that rises and falls with the scoreboard.

Most people arrive here having read a great deal about low self-esteem and having found that almost none of it fitted. That is because low self-esteem describes a deficit that holds steady: the person feels worthless on the good weeks and the bad ones alike, and a promotion changes nothing about the reading. What you may be carrying instead is a value that is genuinely present, genuinely felt, and available only while you are delivering. On a strong week you feel solid. On a week where nothing shipped, the same self reads as close to nothing. Same person, same abilities, entirely different valuation, and the only variable that changed was output.

That difference is practical, because the repairs point in opposite directions. A flat deficit responds to being received: relationships where you are wanted without producing anything, over a long time. A conditional valuation responds to something more specific, which the last third of this page is about. [If the flat version is the one that lives in you, it has its own page](/insights/root-cause-of-low-self-esteem/), and it is worth reading instead of this one rather than alongside it.

There is a cluster of neighbouring complaints that get named interchangeably online and behave completely differently in a life. Find the row that matches your last three months rather than your worst evening.

| What you notice | What it usually is | The question underneath |
|---|---|---|
| "I feel worthless whether or not I deliver" | Low self-worth, a flat deficit that does not track output | Am I worth anything at all? |
| "I feel solid while delivering and hollow within days of stopping" | Conditional self-worth, the pattern on this page | Does my worth survive a week with nothing to show? |
| "I am not equipped for what is in front of me" | Feeling inadequate, a verdict about capability | Can I actually do the thing? |
| "They will eventually work out that I am faking it" | Impostor feelings, doubt aimed at the competence itself | Do I belong in this particular room? |
| "Nothing is ever enough, so I set another target" | The achievement treadmill, chronic dissatisfaction with the score | Why does the target keep moving? |
| "It has to be flawless before anybody sees it" | Perfectionism, the strategy protecting whichever of these sits underneath | How do I make sure nobody can criticize this? |
| "I deserve to be disliked for who I am" | Active self-attack, which behaves like an assault rather than an account | Why am I like this? |

The third and fourth rows separate more cleanly than people expect. You can feel completely capable, know exactly how good your work is, hold no doubt whatsoever about your competence, and still feel that your value as a person is riding on the next outcome. [Doubt about capability is a different pattern with a different repair](/patterns/why-do-i-feel-not-good-enough/), and getting these two mixed up is why so many people spend years collecting evidence of competence and wondering why it changes nothing.

The last two rows point at neighbors rather than at this. [Perfectionism is the strategy](/patterns/how-to-stop-being-a-perfectionist/), the operational method for making sure the verdict comes back clean, and conditional self-worth is frequently what it is protecting. [Active self-attack is heavier and behaves differently](/patterns/self-loathing/), because an attack does not wait for a bad quarter. If both descriptions fit, start with the one that shows up more often in an ordinary week.

## Which Conditions Did You Actually Get?

Different households charge in different currencies, and knowing which one you were asked to pay in explains why your version of this looks unlike somebody else's.

This forms wherever affection, attention, or approval arrived attached to something rather than given as a baseline. What varies is what it was attached to. Most people recognize one currency strongly and a second one faintly.

**Output.** High expectations were the household norm, and the bar was set at a height that could not actually be reached. What gets learned in that environment is an equation rather than a skill: worth equals achievement, recalculated continuously. Because approval never arrived and stayed, there was never a moment of being told that the account was settled. [The parental version of this, including the standard that moved every time you reached it, has its own page](/patterns/unrealistic-parental-expectations/).

**Usefulness.** In some homes love and acceptance were conditioned on a caregiving role, so being needed became the price of belonging, and worth ended up measured in how much you carried for other people. Adults from this route are often superb in a crisis, deeply uncomfortable being helped, and describe their exhaustion long before they describe their doubt. [The name for that early arrangement is parentification.](/patterns/parentification/)

**Staying in favor.** A third version prices worth in relational standing rather than output specifically. Worth becomes based on the conditions of relationships and other people's approval, so the currency is agreement, availability, and not being a problem. This is the version that produces an adult who can tell you the mood of everyone in the room and nothing about what they themselves want. [Where the whole family system was built without separateness, that has a page of its own](/patterns/enmeshed-family/), as does [the habit of keeping everybody else comfortable](/patterns/what-is-people-pleasing/).

**Your own read, discounted.** Underneath several of these runs a second thread. Where feelings, thoughts, and perceptions were regularly dismissed or corrected, your own assessment of your own worth stopped counting as evidence, because your assessments in general stopped counting. The scoring then has to come from outside, since there is nothing inside that anybody ever treated as reliable. [The mechanics of that dismissal are set out separately.](/patterns/emotional-invalidation/)

In every version the same thing happens next, and it is the part that outlives the household. The external verdict never lands on a stable "enough", either because the bar keeps moving or because the approval was conditional from the beginning. So the child installs an evaluator internally, where at least the scoring can be kept consistent. That evaluator keeps running long after anybody is actually watching, which is why leaving home, leaving the job, or leaving the relationship changes the volume and leaves the mechanism entirely intact.

Not every case traces back to a parent. A school where standing was published in rank order every term. A first job under a manager who spoke only to correct. A sport where selection was announced weekly on a noticeboard. An industry that measures people in visible units and says so. The mechanism holds across all of them: value arrived attached to performance, often enough that the attachment became invisible.

## Why Does Achievement Buy So Little Time?

Because what gets purchased is a verdict rather than a standing balance, and the verdict expires on contact with the next task.

Watch what happened the last time you finished something significant. There is a window, sometimes an hour, sometimes an afternoon, where it nearly lands. Then the accounting starts. The achievement gets reclassified as the new baseline within about a day, which means repeating it next quarter would now count as holding steady and failing to repeat it would count as decline. The praise gets filed as politeness. The result gets attributed to timing, or a weak field, or somebody else's groundwork. And the next target is already running before the current one has been felt at all.

That reclassification is the pattern working exactly as designed rather than a malfunction. If worth is earned through performance, then it can in theory always be re-earned, which is enormously preferable to the alternative the whole arrangement exists to avoid. The alternative is that worth might simply not be there, independent of anything provable. Testing that directly is the one experiment the system will not run. So performance stays the safer bet, and the striving continues by default rather than by choice.

There is a second reason more achievement fails, and it is arithmetic. An account that discounts every deposit on arrival will not balance no matter how many deposits you make. The harder you work to settle it, the more clearly you demonstrate to yourself that working is what you are for. People describe reaching the exact position they aimed at for a decade and finding it flat within a fortnight, then quietly concluding that they must have chosen the wrong target.

## What Is All the Producing Actually Protecting?

Two things at once, and they pull in the same direction, which is why the pattern is so stable.

The first is a bid. Producing, achieving, and staying useful is an attempt to obtain the love that was made conditional in the first place. That bid is old and it is still live, which is why a piece of professional recognition can land with an intensity that seems out of proportion to anybody watching. The recognition is answering a much older question than the one it appears to be answering.

The second is a shield. As long as you keep delivering, the question of whether the self underneath the output would be enough on its own stays permanently unasked. Every completed project buys another stretch of not finding out. Seen that way, the pattern is doing something protective and doing it with real efficiency, which is why arguing with it logically has never worked for anybody, including everybody who has tried it on themselves at three in the morning.

There is a third function that people rarely notice in themselves. In the versions built on caregiving or on staying in favor, being valuable through what you provide is what keeps you attached to particular people. Stopping would put the connection itself at risk, or it would feel that way from the inside, which produces the same result in practice. Finding out whether you would still be wanted while producing nothing is riskier than never finding out, so the question stays closed.

## Where Does It Show Up in an Ordinary Week?

In six arenas, and most people recognize two or three before recognizing the rest.

| Where it shows up | What it looks like from outside | The sentence underneath |
|---|---|---|
| At work | Chronic overdelivering, difficulty delegating, volunteering for the next thing before catching breath from the last | "I need to prove my worth constantly." |
| With rest | A sick day worked through, a weekend that needs justifying, downtime that produces guilt instead of relief | "I always need to be doing something productive, or I feel worthless." |
| With praise | A compliment met with a correction, a redistribution of credit, or a joke that levels the exchange | "I am only as good as the praise I receive for my achievements." |
| With a partner | Casual remarks scanned for disappointment, care received on an unproductive day as something owed back | "I'm only loved or valued for what I achieve." |
| Around other people's wins | A flinch at a peer's good news, followed by shame about the flinch, followed by an audit of your own output | "I am not worth as much if I am not outperforming others." |
| With your own identity | A blank when somebody asks what you do for fun, a CV that answers questions about who you are | "I'm defined by my accomplishments, not by who I am." |

The last row causes the most quiet damage over a lifetime, and it causes it slowly enough that nobody notices it happening. A pattern that only registers time as well spent when it produces something gradually removes every activity with no output attached. That is how a person arrives at forty with an impressive record, a narrow life, and no idea how the two became the same thing. [Finding out who you are underneath the role is its own separate work.](/patterns/i-dont-know-who-i-am-anymore/)

The fifth row deserves a word, because people are ashamed of it and it is close to universal here. The flinch at a colleague's promotion is information about how the scoring works inside you rather than evidence that you are a bad friend. On a shared scoreboard, somebody else's rise reads mechanically as a movement in your own standing. [The comparison habit underneath it has a practical repair of its own.](/insights/how-to-stop-comparing-yourself-to-others/)

## Why Can You Not Take a Sick Day Without Working Through It?

Because being unproductive removes the only proof of worth you currently trust, and it removes it precisely when you have the least capacity to tolerate the gap.

This is the sharpest single test of the pattern, sharper than any amount of introspection about achievement, because illness removes the usual justifications. You are entitled to the day. Nobody is asking for anything. Your own body has made the decision for you. And still, some hours into it, you find yourself drafting the emails you will need to send when you return, or tidying something, or planning the recovery of the schedule. Resting empty-handed feels like standing in the open with nothing in your hands.

What is happening is that rest was filed early as something you earn rather than something you need. In the original environment there was no moment of neutral, unpaid rest to observe: downtime either had to be justified or it was the moment worth went unproven. So an hour of unearned rest still trips the alarm decades later, and the alarm is quite specific. It rarely arrives during a genuinely busy week. It arrives on the quiet Saturday, in the middle of the holiday, on the third afternoon of a break, precisely when there is nothing to point at.

The practical cost compounds in a way that is worth naming plainly. Recovery gets deferred until the body enforces it, which means the rest you eventually take is far more expensive than the rest you refused. And because the guilt is loudest when you most need the break, the pattern systematically withholds recovery from you at exactly the point you cannot afford to go without it.

## What Does This Do to the People Closest to You?

It makes being cared for surprisingly hard, and it does so in a way that reads to everyone else as modesty or independence.

Start with receiving, which is its own distinct difficulty and the one people miss in themselves for longest. Compliments get doubted or deflected, because if the compliment turned out to be untrue the whole structure holding up your worth would wobble. Deflecting keeps the account consistent, which is why the rebuttal arrives before you have decided to make it. There is a second reason people rarely name: accepting praise at face value raises the bar you will be expected to clear next time, so agreeing with it feels like signing something.

Then there is the harder version. Being cared for on a day when you accomplished nothing is genuinely uncomfortable for people in this pattern, occasionally close to threatening. If your place has always felt earned, warmth arriving without a corresponding contribution creates a debt rather than a comfort, and the reflex is to balance the exchange fast: a returned favor that evening, a self-deprecating remark, a change of subject. Partners frequently experience this as being held at arm's length by somebody who is otherwise extremely generous, which is confusing enough that it goes unmentioned for years.

The scanning does the rest of the damage. A neutral remark gets read for disappointment. A quiet evening gets read as withdrawal. Affection gets experienced as contingent on continued success, and here is the part worth testing rather than assuming: usually you are the one applying the condition and then hearing it back in their voice. Ask directly what would change between you if you produced nothing for a year. Most people in this pattern have never once asked, and the rehearsal is considerably worse than the conversation.

## Can You Be Genuinely Confident and Still Feel This?

Constantly, and the combination is common enough to be closer to the default presentation than to the exception.

In adult life this rarely reads as a wound. It reads as a high performer. Credentialed, dependable, hard to fault, the one who catches the thing everybody else missed, and privately running on empty. The competence is real and frequently accurate. Many people carrying this can tell you exactly how good they are at their work, with no false modesty and no doubt about it, while feeling that their value as a person is entirely contingent on continuing to demonstrate it. Two separate instruments, two separate readings, and only one of them is broken.

That is what makes this so hard to raise with anybody. Say out loud that you need your work in order to feel like a worthwhile person and you expose that the identity feels borrowed. Most people carrying this have never said it plainly, because saying it invites either reassurance that misses the point or an argument about whether it is true. The version that helps is somebody who simply does not flinch.

I spent five years and more than five thousand hours doing this kind of work on myself before I built anything around it, and more than fifty thousand dollars on seminars, therapy, books, and teachers, some found in the jungles of Vanuatu, some in the jungles of Peru. What took me longest to accept about this particular pattern is that no result touched it. Every one was supposed to be the one that settled the question, and each one bought about a week before the accounting resumed. What actually moved it was smaller, slower, and much less interesting than any of that, and I would rather say so than sell you a version where the next achievement finally does the job.

## What Does Unconditional Self-Worth Actually Look Like?

Considerably quieter than most people imagine, and the clearest way to see it is in a week when nothing ships.

Unconditional self-worth keeps the caring about good work exactly where it was and takes the weight off it, so that the caring stops being load-bearing. Worth becomes a background fact rather than a daily verdict: present before the first task of the morning, still present after a project falls short, roughly constant across a bad week and a great one. That constancy is the marker. Somebody at this end of the axis still enjoys recognition and still prefers doing things well. What has changed is that neither one decides whether the day was allowed to count.

Here is that translated out of abstraction and into specifics.

| The conditional end | The unconditional end | What it looks like in a week when nothing ships |
|---|---|---|
| Worth tracks the most recent result | Worth sits at roughly the same level regardless | Monday feels much like it did during the good quarter |
| Rest has to be earned with output first | Rest is taken because you need it | A day off gets taken without a justification drafted first |
| Praise gets corrected before it lands | Praise gets accepted as given | Somebody says the work was good and you say thank you, adding nothing |
| A mistake is a referendum on the self | A mistake is information about the work | You note what went wrong, adjust, and sleep normally |
| Leisure has to be paid for before it is enjoyable | Leisure needs no payment | An afternoon with nothing to show for it registers as an afternoon |
| A finished thing gets filed and the next begins | A finished thing gets felt before the next begins | You sit with it for ten minutes and the ten minutes are not unbearable |
| Being cared for creates a debt | Being cared for is simply received | Somebody does something kind and you do not balance it that evening |
| Identity is the record of what you produced | Identity includes things nobody scores | You can answer "what are you into" without leading with your job |
| Somebody else's win moves your standing | Somebody else's win is their win | A peer's promotion produces a straightforward "good for them" |
| The evaluator holds the final vote | The evaluator is one opinion among several | The critical voice runs and does not get the last word |

Read the right-hand column carefully, because it is deliberately unimpressive. Nobody in it is achieving anything, announcing anything, or having a realization. Somebody in that column has bad weeks, misses targets, and produces mediocre work with reasonable frequency. What has changed is that none of it moves the underlying reading.

Don't judge yourself if that column reads as slightly out of reach today. The left-hand column took decades of daily repetition to build, and nobody crosses to the other side in an afternoon. What makes it approachable is that the right-hand column is made of small, specific, repeatable behaviors rather than a change of personality, which means there is somewhere to start.

## What Actually Moves This?

Repeated mismatches between what you produce and how you get treated, collected deliberately, over a long enough stretch that they cannot be dismissed as flukes.

This one is slow, and the reason is worth understanding before you start. Nobody consciously decided to only feel worthy while performing, so there is no single belief sitting there to argue with. The mechanism runs in the background, underneath the level where reasoning operates, which is why insight about it produces so little change on its own and why people who understand this pattern perfectly still work through their sick days. What shifts it is evidence of a very particular kind: doing less and being treated the same, resting and not falling apart, being cared for on a day when nothing was accomplished, and noticing slowly, only after enough repetitions, that the collapse in worth you expected simply did not happen.

1. **Run one small, deliberate underdelivery a week.** Send the good-enough email. Leave the thing at ninety percent. Decline the extra piece nobody asked you to take. What you are collecting is direct experience of producing less and observing what actually changes in how people treat you, which is almost always far less than the alarm predicted.
2. **Take an hour you have not earned.** Put it in the calendar on a day when nothing has been achieved. The guilt will arrive roughly on schedule. Stay in the hour, do nothing about the guilt, and notice that it passes without you having to produce anything to end it. That noticing is the entire exercise.
3. **Sit with a finished thing for ten minutes before starting the next.** Set a timer, because you will otherwise begin the next thing inside ninety seconds and never notice you did. The discomfort that shows up around minute three is the actual material here.
4. **Accept one piece of praise a week with nothing added.** Thank you, full stop. No correction, no redistribution of credit, no returned favor that evening. For anybody whose place has felt earned, this repetition is harder than any confession and it works on the same root.
5. **Practice being cared for while producing nothing.** Let somebody do something for you on a flat, unimpressive day and resist balancing it. This is the exposure the pattern is most organized to prevent, which is exactly why it carries the most information.
6. **Separate the work verdict from the self verdict, on paper.** After feedback, write one line about the change requested, in the imperative, about the work. Then check whether anything in your line is a statement about you as a person. Almost never is. The sorting step happens automatically in most people and in this pattern was never installed.
7. **Add one thing with no output attached.** Something you are not good at, that produces nothing, that nobody scores. The point is having somewhere in your week that the accounting cannot reach, and the fact that it feels like a waste of time initially is the reason it works.
8. **Treat the evaluating voice as a specific installation rather than as the truth.** Approaches that work with it directly, parts work and schema-focused therapy among them, tend to help precisely because they address it as something that got put there by particular people at a particular time, which is a thing you can negotiate with. Arguing with it as though it were an objective assessment goes nowhere, as you have probably established already.

Is any of this comfortable? No. It is small and freaking repetitive, and it is exactly the kind of thing that still works in month nine, long after the insight has stopped feeling interesting. [Why understanding a pattern so rarely changes it is worth reading separately](/patterns/why-insight-doesnt-change-anything/), as is [the practice side of holding your own worth](/patterns/how-to-love-yourself/).

One honest caveat, and it matters more than any item on the list. Some environments genuinely do withdraw warmth the moment output drops. A job where your standing visibly tracks your last quarter, a family that goes quiet when you have nothing impressive to report, a field that measures people in public units. Where that is the case, part of the work is about choosing better environments rather than about correcting a belief in isolation, because you cannot collect evidence that nothing collapses while standing somewhere that things genuinely do.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

Years rather than weeks, and the honest answer runs at two different speeds.

Recognition arrives quickly. Most people can catch the accounting mid-sentence within a few weeks of deliberate attention: the moment the achievement gets discounted, the moment the sick day gets converted into a work day, the moment the compliment gets corrected. Catching it counts fully even on the days you go ahead and do it anyway. Watching yourself deflect a genuine compliment while knowing precisely what you are doing feels terrible and is a real step forward.

The valuation itself takes far longer, because it was rehearsed daily for decades and it fades at the pace of accumulated experience rather than understanding. Reading this page changes remarkably little on its own. Fifty unearned hours of rest, fifty accepted compliments, and a couple of years of small deliberate underdeliveries change a great deal. Expect it to move in one area first, expect it to regress hard during any period of financial pressure or high visibility, and expect a stretch where you can see the whole mechanism clearly and remain unable to interrupt it. None of that means the work stopped taking.

Does it disappear entirely? Most people describe it loosening rather than vanishing. The gap between the urge to prove something and acting on it gets longer. Recovery after a poor week drops from weeks toward days. And at some point you notice you took a genuine day off, produced nothing, drafted no emails, and thought about it afterwards only because this came to mind. That is the change arriving in behavior rather than in understanding, and behavior is where it counts.

## What Is One Thing to Do Today, Before You Take On Anything Else?

Open a note and answer one question in writing: what would you have to be, or do, this week, for it to be acceptable to have produced nothing? Write the actual conditions as they occur to you, in your own words, however unreasonable they look on the page. Most people produce a startlingly specific list within two minutes, and most of it is somebody else's terms, repeated back verbatim by an evaluator who has been enforcing them without ever being asked to justify them once.

Then read the list back as though somebody else handed it to you. Seeing your own conditions written in your own phrasing does something that a month of trying to feel differently does not, because a term you have already written down twice is much harder to obey on the third occasion. Keep the note. Add to it for a fortnight. It costs you fifteen minutes and nothing else, and it is worth doing whether or not you ever go anywhere or buy anything.
