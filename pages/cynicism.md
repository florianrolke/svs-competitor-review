# Why have I become so cynical?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Cynicism is a conclusion about people that got reached in advance and now filters everything arriving afterward, so good news gets explained away and bad news counts as confirmation. It usually installs after a specific betrayal, a house where the danger came from inside it, or years of carrying more than your share. It softens as evidence accumulates, slowly, in specific directions.
</div>

<div class="identify-box" markdown="1">
Four sentences people carrying this pattern say to themselves. If one of them is yours, you will know it before you reach the full stop.

- "Everyone has an angle. You just have to work out what it is."
- "People let you down. It is only a question of when."
- "If I do not expect anything, nothing can be taken away."
- "I am not cynical. I am realistic."

Run those as a standing policy and the cost is a read on people that no person can change.

A friend tells you they are back together with an ex, and they look genuinely, uncomplicatedly happy about it. Before you have finished arranging your face, the thought has already arrived in your own voice, fully formed: give it six months.

You did not decide to think that. It was already there, waiting.

Most people who type this question are pointing at the word in the middle of it. Become. Something used to be different. There was a version of you that took a compliment and then simply carried on with the day, that could hear about somebody working things out with their father and feel moved rather than mildly corrected. That version did not resign. It got replaced, gradually, by something that works better in the world you turned out to be living in, and the replacement has never once announced itself as a problem, because from the inside it presents as having finally got wise.

One thing before anything else. If this stance went up because somebody harmed you, or because the house you grew up in was one where the danger came from inside it, that material deserves qualified support alongside anything you read here, and there is a fuller note on that a few sections down.

Everything below is about seeing the mechanism clearly: where it installs, what it genuinely protects, why it is so hard to argue anybody out of, what it quietly costs, and what the other end of it looks like on an ordinary Tuesday.

## What Does Cynicism Sound Like From the Inside?

It sounds like good sense, spoken in your own voice, with a track record behind it. That is the entire difficulty of the subject.

The sentences below are the lines people actually run in their own heads, in their own phrasing rather than a tidied-up version of it. They recur with unnerving consistency across people who have never met and whose lives have nothing in common, which is the first clue that something got installed rather than something anybody simply is. This first set is the standing read on people:

- "Relationships are just power struggles; someone always gets hurt."
- "Give it six months."
- "What do they actually want?"
- "Knew it."

A second set runs specifically around hope, and this is the group doing the most work, because it governs what you are willing to want out loud:

- "That kind of thing happens to other people."
- "That only works in stories."
- "Hoping is how you get caught out."
- "Better to see it coming."

And a third set defends the whole arrangement, which is why the pattern is so durable. These are the lines that arrive the moment anybody suggests the stance itself might be the problem:

- "I have just been paying attention."
- "I used to be like you."

Read them slowly, and notice which ones land with a small physical jolt because they turn out to be yours word for word, with no translation needed.

Then notice how few of them sound like pain. Not one of them describes an injury. They describe competence, pattern recognition, and a reasonable adjustment to how things demonstrably are. Almost nobody experiences this as damage from the inside, which is a large part of why it can run for twenty years without ever being examined. There is no obvious reason to go looking for help with being hard to fool.

## When Does This Belong With a Professional Rather Than a Page Like This?

Right here, near the top, because this boundary matters more than every practical suggestion below it put together.

Some of the histories that install this stance need qualified support in their own right. If the generalizing started because somebody close to you harmed you, or because you grew up in a house where violence, constant conflict, or the aftermath of infidelity set the weather, that belongs with a licensed professional rather than with a reading list. [What that particular childhood installs is covered separately](/patterns/growing-up-with-violence/), and none of it should be worked through alone.

The same holds if the situation you are reading this about is live. If somebody is actively deceiving you right now, or uses your trust to control your money, your movements, or your contact with other people, that is a current problem rather than an old pattern, and it deserves people who can help, including people who can help you leave if leaving is what it takes.

And the same holds from the inside. If you are carrying an addiction, a diagnosed condition you are currently managing, a hopelessness that has not lifted for months, or any thought of harming yourself, however briefly it passes through, that needs a clinician. One further marker is worth naming plainly, because it gets missed constantly. A bleakness that has spread past people and institutions to cover everything, including yourself and your own future, is a different thing from a guarded read on other people's motives, and a doctor is the right person to tell those two apart. Asking in month one costs far less than asking in month twelve.

## Where Does Cynicism Actually Come From?

From a break in trust that did not stay contained to whoever caused it. Three routes lead there, and they arrive at the same endpoint by different roads.

**The first is a single event.** A partner, a friend, an authority figure, or an institution did something specific, and the finding did not stay local. It generalized outward into a standing judgment about relationships and about people in general, and it generalized inward into distrust of your own judgment for having missed it the first time. That second half is the one that outlasts everything else, and it has [its own page](/patterns/when-someone-close-betrays-you/), because losing confidence in your own read of a room is a distinct injury from losing confidence in one person.

**The second is an environment rather than an event.** Growing up around violence, frequent parental conflict, or infidelity produces the same conclusion with no single incident to point at, because the danger came from inside the house and from the people whose job was to be safe. There is nothing dramatic to remember and nothing to name, and the read gets built anyway, which is why so many people carrying this describe a childhood that was fine and mean it.

**The third runs through having been handed too much, too early.** A child given adult-sized responsibility, who covered for an adult, managed a household, or carried a sibling, arrives at a conclusion that is entirely reasonable given the evidence available: people cannot be relied on to carry their share. That hardens over decades into a general expectation that others will fail or disappoint, and it usually comes bundled with an exhausting competence. [The childhood that gets traded for that competence is worth reading about on its own.](/patterns/i-had-to-grow-up-too-fast/)

Whatever the route, the underlying logic is identical. A nervous system that got blindsided once reorganizes itself around never being blindsided again, and the cheapest available guarantee is to stop expecting good outcomes at all. If nothing good was ever expected, nothing good can be taken away. Nobody chose that as a philosophy. It arrived as an arithmetic the body did, quickly, on the evidence in front of it, and it has been running ever since without being asked to show its working.

Which is also the answer to the word "become". You did not develop an opinion about human nature. A protective setting got installed at a moment when it was accurate and necessary, and it stayed on afterward, the way a house alarm stays armed long after the neighbourhood changed.

## What Is the Cynicism Actually Protecting?

Hope, specifically. Not the bad outcome, which arrives whether you expected it or not.

This is the part that surprises people, so it is worth stating in one flat line: hope is treated as the exposure, because hope is what made the original disappointment possible. Being let down by somebody you had no expectations of costs almost nothing. Being let down by somebody you had allowed yourself to count on is the thing the whole apparatus exists to prevent from happening twice. So the apparatus does not target liars, or betrayal, or bad people. It targets the part of you that would have looked forward to something.

There are three further layers underneath that one, and they tend to be invisible from inside.

The first is self-image. Cynicism lets you experience yourself as the person who sees clearly and cannot be fooled, rather than the person who was fooled once and might be again. That is an enormous upgrade in how the original event feels, and it is available at any moment, at no cost, simply by expecting the worst. Nobody gives up a position that flattering because somebody pointed out its downsides.

The second is the removal of dependency. If nothing is expected of anybody, nobody is in a position to fail to deliver. That produces the refusal of freely offered help, the pride in handling things alone, and the quiet equation that relying on somebody means handing them a lever. [Where that hardens into distance from feeling generally has its own page.](/patterns/emotionally-unavailable/)

The third is distance itself, kept at a workable range. Sarcasm, guardedness, and a preference for keeping conversation on safe ground all do the same practical job: they hold people at a point short of where they could add more weight or disappoint you again. Every one of those is socially acceptable. Several of them are actively admired. That combination is why the pattern survives so comfortably in adult life.

## Is It Cynicism, or Is It Just Realism?

The difference shows up in what happens when new evidence arrives. Everything else about the two is identical, including how certain they feel.

This is the pattern's most stubborn disguise, and the confusion is genuinely reasonable. Both claim to be seeing clearly. Both point at real experiences as their justification. Both are frequently correct, which matters, because people do behave badly, companies do perform their values, and a good number of couples getting back together genuinely do not last six months. Accuracy is a poor test here. Plenty of accurate readings come out of a mechanism that was never going to produce a different reading.

The functional difference is whether the read can be moved. Realism updates. It can be surprised. It revises its assessment of a specific person or a specific situation when the facts change, and it will happily conclude that somebody it doubted turned out to be reliable. Cynicism is a conclusion reached in advance that then filters what comes in afterward. Good news gets explained away, usually within a second: they want something, they are performing, it will not hold. Bad news gets filed immediately as confirmation: knew it. Nothing that arrives can change the verdict, because the verdict is what decides how each arrival gets processed.

| What to ask of the read | Realism | Cynicism |
|---|---|---|
| Where the conclusion sits | After the evidence, provisionally | Before the evidence, settled |
| What it does with good news | Weighs it, sometimes revises | Explains it away as motive or luck |
| What it does with bad news | Weighs it, adjusts the specific read | Files it as confirmation of the general one |
| Whether it can be surprised | Regularly, and does not mind being | Almost never, and treats surprise as a lapse |
| Who it applies to | Particular people, for particular reasons | Everybody, in advance, including strangers |
| What being wrong costs | An updated opinion | The whole position, which is why it stays closed |

The single most useful row is the fourth. If nothing anybody has done in the last five years has genuinely surprised you, the thing running is a closed loop rather than an assessment. A read that cannot be wrong is also a read that cannot be right in any meaningful sense, because it was never contingent on the world in the first place.

There is a further complication that keeps the loop supplied. The stance manufactures a portion of its own evidence. Warmth held back tends to come back at the same temperature, which then reads as coldness in the other person. Trust extended in a form nobody can pass gets failed by ordinary people having ordinary weeks. Relationships kept at arm's length do stay shallow, which then confirms that closeness was never on offer. None of that is anybody lying to you. It is a prediction quietly arranging the conditions that prove it.

I spent five years and more than five thousand hours doing this kind of work on myself before I built anything around it, and more than fifty thousand dollars on seminars, therapy, books, and teachers, some found in the jungles of Vanuatu, some in the jungles of Peru. The thing I conceded last, and most reluctantly, was that a read I was quietly proud of, the one that let me see what other people apparently could not, had stopped being a read at all somewhere along the way and become a setting. Where it sits today is worth saying plainly: the reflex to check what somebody wants has quieted a great deal, it has not gone to zero, and I would rather tell you that than describe a finished version of something nobody finishes.

## Is This Cynicism, or One of Its Neighbors?

Worth checking, because five fairly different patterns produce a similar-looking week, and the thing that shifts each one differs completely.

| What you are seeing | What it might actually be | The thing that tells them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Your mind landing on the worst outcome of a specific coming event | Catastrophizing | Forecasting aims at a future event and can be checked against what actually happens. This is a settled background stance about people, with no particular Tuesday attached |
| A life organized around a specific harm that was done to you | A hardened account of that harm | That story keeps one event at the center. This can run as pure policy toward people who never did anything, without any story being consciously involved |
| Anger that still rises at a name, years later | Resentment | Resentment has a target and a charge. This has neither, and often feels calm, which is why it gets mistaken for perspective |
| A guard that goes up around closeness specifically | One of several distinct trust patterns | Trust behavior varies: scanning, testing, leaving first, expecting betrayal. This is closest to the last one, generalized past any single relationship |
| A blanket verdict that nobody at all can be relied on | Distrust running as a standing policy | That verdict is about relying on people. This is about what you expect from people and the world in general, which is wider and quieter |
| A well-judged read of a genuinely bad situation you are currently in | An accurate alarm | If it is currently true, it is working correctly. Check whether it also applies to people outside that situation |

Each of those has its own page, and each responds to something different. [The forecast that lands on the worst case before any evidence arrives is a separate mechanism.](/patterns/catastrophizing/) [A present-tense account of what was done to you, still running long after the event closed, works differently again.](/patterns/victim-mentality/) [Anger that had nowhere to go and hardened into a position needs a different route out.](/patterns/resentment/) [The four distinct patterns that hide under the single label of trust issues are worth sorting before anything else](/patterns/trust-issues/), as is [distrust that has become a standing policy aimed at everybody](/patterns/why-cant-i-trust-anyone/).

Most people find one row dominant, one present, and the rest belonging to a version of them from five years ago. That ranking is what makes the practice further down usable rather than generic.

## Where Does It Show Up When You Would Not Call It Cynicism?

In eight arenas, and almost nobody recognizes themselves in more than two or three at first.

| Where it shows up | What it looks like from outside | The sentence underneath |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting new people | Slow to warm, pleasant, holding the conversation on safe topics for months | "Everyone has an angle." |
| Compliments and praise | A joke, a correction, or a returned favor, and then a day spent working out the motive | "What do they actually want?" |
| A conversation turning real | The register changes to humor within a sentence, and neither person notices who moved it | "Hoping is how you get caught out." |
| Somebody else's good news | A congratulation that is genuinely meant and a private forecast attached to it | "Give it six months." |
| Reconciliation and forgiveness stories | Polite interest, an internal filing as unrealistic or as somebody else's luck | "That only works in stories." |
| Work and institutions | A mission statement skimmed as performance, with no specific reason required | "Knew it." |
| Offers of help | Declined smoothly, or accepted and immediately balanced out | "If I do not expect anything, nothing can be taken away." |
| Your own future | Plans made without permission to look forward to them | "That kind of thing happens to other people." |

The compliments row is the one people miss in themselves for longest, because it reads as modesty from every angle including the inside. A colleague says something genuinely appreciative in a meeting and instead of the remark simply landing, a second process starts underneath it and runs for the rest of the afternoon. Nobody watching that would call it a symptom of anything.

The institutions row deserves separating out, because it is the least testable and therefore the most durable. A read on one person can be checked against what that person does next week. A read on an entire sector, or on people in general, has no next week attached to it and no way to be wrong, so it sits there permanently, accumulating the small confirmations that any large system produces and none of the corrections.

## Why Does Other People's Good News Land Badly?

Because it contains a claim about what is possible, and the claim quietly applies to you too.

This is the part almost nobody says out loud, and it is worth saying because the shame around it is disproportionate. Somebody tells you their relationship worked out, or their business did, or they finally repaired something with a parent, and what surfaces has an edge of "sure" on it, something much closer to irritation than to happiness for them. Then, usually about a second later, a second reaction arrives about the first one, which is that you have apparently become a person who cannot be pleased for a friend.

The mechanism is simpler than the guilt suggests. If good outcomes in trust, love, or repair are broadly unavailable, then not having reached them is a fact about the world. If somebody standing in front of you has reached them, they are a fact about you. The flash of bitterness is the accounting adjusting, and it is aimed at what their story implies about your own situation rather than at them.

There is a specific version of this that shows up around reconciliation stories in particular. Hearing that somebody forgave a parent, or worked things out with an ex, tends to produce a private verdict that they are naive, performing, or telling themselves something convenient. That verdict is doing a job. If genuine repair does not really happen, then not having managed it yourself stops being a personal failure and becomes a reasonable position.

Don't judge yourself for the flash. Almost everybody carrying this pattern has it, almost nobody admits it, and its presence tells you that some part of the system still wants the thing it claims to have written off. A system that had genuinely stopped caring would feel nothing at all.

## Why Is Sarcasm the Only Register Left for Anything Real?

Because it is the one exit nobody objects to.

A conversation with somebody close starts turning emotionally real, and a joke arrives to move it back onto safer ground before either of you has consciously registered the shift. Everybody laughs. The moment passes. Nothing about that exchange looks like avoidance from the outside, and this is exactly why it survives for decades while more obvious defenses get named and dealt with. Nobody at a dinner table has ever complained about the person who made something serious funny.

The cost lands somewhere else entirely, and it lands slowly. People learn what a conversation with you can hold. After enough well-timed jokes, they stop bringing the real thing, and they do it politely, without a decision anybody could point to. The result is a large, warm, genuinely friendly social life in which not one person knows what happened to you last spring. [The ceiling that produces has its own page](/patterns/why-are-my-friendships-so-surface-level/), and it is worth reading alongside this one, because the loneliness that comes from it is usually blamed on the quality of other people.

Cynical humor also does something subtler. It lets a true thing be said with an escape hatch attached. If the bleak joke about your job, your marriage, or your prospects lands badly, it was a joke, and if it lands well, the point got across without you ever having claimed it. Over years that becomes the only channel available for anything that matters, which means the things that matter only ever arrive pre-diminished.

## Why Does Being Right Feel Better Than Being Treated Well?

Because being right is the only win the arrangement allows itself.

Somebody lets you down roughly on the schedule you predicted, and underneath the disappointment there is a small unmistakable flicker of satisfaction. Noticing that in yourself is unpleasant, and it is also completely coherent, because the prediction converts a bad outcome into evidence of your own clear sight, and that conversion is available every single time. A good outcome offers nothing comparable. It just makes you wrong.

That asymmetry is what keeps the loop fed. Each confirmed prediction tightens the read a little, each disconfirmation gets discounted as luck, an exception, or a person who has not shown their hand yet. Over enough years the ledger looks overwhelming, and it looks that way because of how entries were selected rather than because of what happened.

Look at what that trade actually buys. It buys the experience of being unfooled, at the price of being unreachable, and it treats being unsurprised as though it were the same thing as being unhurt. Those two came apart a long time ago. Predicting a disappointment accurately does very little to reduce it, as anybody who has correctly predicted the end of something they wanted can confirm.

## What Does Running This as a Policy Actually Cost?

More than it saves, and the costs land in places where they are hard to trace back.

The first cost is borne by whoever is currently trying to get close to you. A kindness that gets audited is experienced by the person offering it as a rejection, even when nothing is said. Guardedness reads as coldness. Warmth that arrives conditionally teaches people to stop offering. Almost nobody in this pattern intends any of that, and the effect is identical whether it was intended or not, which is why partners and old friends usually describe it years before the person does.

The second cost is the loss of the control condition. Because the guard never comes down, no evidence about what would have happened if it had ever gets collected. Every uneventful year gets filed as proof that the vigilance is working, rather than as proof that the vigilance was not needed. There is no way to be pleasantly surprised by a door you have never opened.

The third cost is the specific grief that stays frozen underneath. The general stance often stays in place precisely so the particular thing never has to be felt. A blanket verdict about people in general is far more comfortable to carry than the specific sentence about the specific person who did the specific thing, and as long as the blanket verdict holds, that sentence never has to be said. Which is also why arguing about human nature never moves anything: the argument is not where the material is.

The fourth cost is transmission. The read passes on by demonstration, to children, to partners, to younger colleagues who take a cue from how you talk about people. Nobody teaches it deliberately. It is absorbed from a thousand small asides about angles and motives, long before anybody involved can evaluate whether the asides are accurate.

## What Does Hopeful Realism Actually Look Like?

It looks like holding two things at once: a clear-eyed read of how a specific person or situation has actually behaved, and an unclosed question about what might still be possible.

The word doing the work in that sentence is "specific". This is not a return to blind trust and it does not require deciding that people are good. It requires that the read be about somebody in particular, based on what they have actually done, and that it stay available for revision in both directions. The guard stays. It gets narrower and considerably more accurate, aimed at real signals instead of at everybody by default, which is why people who reach this end usually describe themselves as harder to fool than they were before rather than easier.

| The cynical end | The hopeful realist end | What that looks like on an ordinary Tuesday |
|---|---|---|
| Compliments audited for the angle | Compliments taken at face value until there is a reason not to | Somebody praises your work and the rest of the afternoon is just the afternoon |
| A blanket policy applied to everybody | Specific reads of specific people | You can name the three people you actually do not trust, and say what each of them did |
| Trust as a single verdict, already delivered | Trust rebuilt in increments, in the direction of individuals | You hand somebody one thing that matters slightly, and see what comes back |
| Reconciliation stories filed as unrealistic | Reconciliation stories heard without a flinch | A friend describes forgiving their father and nothing rises in your chest |
| Other people's good news as a rebuke | Other people's good news as evidence something is available | You hear the news and the congratulation is the whole of your reaction |
| Sarcasm as the only register for anything real | Sarcasm used for fun again | A serious conversation runs its length without a joke rescuing anybody |
| Hope treated as exposure | Hope held as an open question | You say out loud that you want this one to work out |
| A guard aimed at everyone in advance | A guard aimed at actual signals | The alert goes off about one person, for a reason you could write down |
| Kindness read for the motive | Kindness read as kindness, provisionally | Somebody offers to help and you say yes without doing the calculation first |
| Being unsurprised as the goal | Being accurate as the goal | You get one read wrong, and it costs an opinion rather than the whole position |

Read the right-hand column carefully, because it is deliberately unimpressive. Nobody in it is having a breakthrough. There are no reunions, no restored faith in humanity, no decision to see the best in people. The far end of this pattern mostly consists of somebody whose read on a person can still be changed by that person.

There is no need to feel behind if that column reads as out of reach right now. The distance between the two took years to build and it does not close in an afternoon, and the third row is where almost everybody starts.

## What Actually Moves It?

Accumulated evidence, in specific directions, over months. Argument does nothing, including your own, because what is running is a prediction rather than an opinion, and predictions respond to experience rather than to reasoning.

1. **Separate the earned caution from the blanket policy.** Two lists, written down. On one, the people who have an actual track record of taking something from you, with what they did next to their name. On the other, everybody your guard currently covers. The gap between those two lists is the pattern, stated in a form you can look at. Most people are startled by how short the first list is.
2. **Track the disconfirming evidence deliberately.** Keep a running note of every occasion somebody had a clear chance to take advantage, cut a corner at your cost, or let you down, and did not. This has to be written, because the discounting happens in under a second and memory will not preserve what got discounted.
3. **Stop letting the entries be explained away.** Luck, exception, not yet, they had their reasons. Each of those is a small piece of accounting that keeps the ledger balanced in favor of the existing verdict. Notice the move as it happens and let the entry stand anyway.
4. **Narrow every read to one person at a time.** When the general thought arrives, ask what this particular person has actually done, and answer with behavior rather than with an assessment of their character. A read about somebody in particular can be checked. A read about people in general never can.
5. **Deal with the specific thing underneath.** Whatever betrayal, household, or years of overload installed this, the general stance frequently persists as a way of never feeling the particular grief. [Rebuilding trust with somebody specific runs on its own timetable and its own rules](/patterns/how-to-rebuild-trust/), and it is a different job from softening a worldview.
6. **Let one small hope be visible.** Say out loud, to one person, that you want something to work out. Not a large thing. The point is the exposure rather than the stakes, and the discovery that nothing punishes you for it.
7. **Take one compliment without processing it.** Say thank you and add nothing at all. No joke, no correction, no returned favor that evening. For anyone running this pattern, that repetition is harder than any conversation and it works on the same root.
8. **Catch the joke a second before you make it.** You will feel the swerve coming. Once a week, let the serious sentence stand instead. That is a serious practice, and each time contradicts a rule installed a long time ago.

Is any of this fast? No. It is unglamorous, freaking slow work, and it is the only kind that holds here. Expect one new disappointment to reset the whole system temporarily somewhere in the early stages, and expect that to feel like proof the whole idea was naive. That reset is the pattern doing exactly what it was built to do, at the exact moment it is being asked to stand down.

## What If People Really Are Like That, Though?

Sometimes they are, and this page does not ask you to pretend otherwise.

Some people do take advantage. Some organizations do perform their values while behaving differently. Some relationships that look happy at the announcement do end within the year. A read built on that evidence is often correct, and being told to think more positively about it is both useless and slightly insulting, which is the reason most advice on this subject bounces straight off.

The claim here is narrower than "people are good". A policy costs more than it saves, for three specific reasons. It charges every person you meet the price of what one person did, which is expensive and lands mostly on people who were never involved. It removes the one procedure that could tell you whether the policy is still needed, since a guard that never comes down produces no information. And accuracy about the average tells you nothing at all about the individual currently standing in front of you, which is unfortunately the only person you actually have to make a decision about.

There is a version of this that is genuinely current rather than historical. If you are inside a situation right now where deception or exploitation is actually happening, the read is doing its job and the task is the situation. The test is whether the same stance also covers people entirely outside it, including people you have known for years and people you met on Tuesday. A guard that discriminates is working. A guard that does not has stopped being about anybody in particular.

## How Long Does This Honestly Take?

Months rather than weeks, and the honest answer runs at two speeds.

Recognition arrives quickly. Within a few weeks of deliberate attention most people can catch the read as it lands, watch the private forecast attach itself to somebody's good news, and know exactly what is happening while it happens. That counts, even on the days you believe the forecast anyway.

The reflex takes considerably longer, because it was installed by experience and it only responds to more of the same. Reading this page changes very little on its own. Fifty entries in a disconfirmation log, collected over a few months, change a great deal, and they change it the slow way, by making the general verdict harder to hold in the presence of specifics.

The realistic marker of progress is worth getting right, because most people track the wrong thing and conclude they are failing. The goal is not the absence of a guarded thought. Guarded thoughts will keep arriving for a long time. What moves is how quickly one can be checked against a particular person's actual behavior instead of being treated as a settled fact about people, and that interval shortens from days, to hours, to somewhere around a minute. Is that frustrating to hear? Yes, and I would rather say it than sell a faster route that does not exist.

## What Is One Thing to Do Today, Before You Try to Trust Anybody More?

Run a disconfirmation log for seven days. Open a note on your phone. Every time somebody has a genuine opportunity to take advantage of you, cut a corner at your expense, break a small promise, or simply let you down, and does not, write one line: who it was, and what they could have done instead. No conclusions, no assessments of their character, no notes about what they might want later. Just the entry.

Then read the whole list on day eight, in one sitting.

This works because the mechanism it interrupts is filing rather than perception. Disappointments get recorded automatically and permanently. Ordinary decency gets processed and discarded within a second, categorized as luck, as an exception, or as somebody who has not been tested yet. The log removes the discarding step and nothing else, which is why it is more useful than any amount of deciding to think better of people. Most people are genuinely surprised by the length of the list, and either result tells you something worth knowing. A long list is evidence the read has been running well ahead of the facts. A short one is specific information about who is currently in your life, which is a different problem with a much clearer solution.
