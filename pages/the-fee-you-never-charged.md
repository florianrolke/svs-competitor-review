# Why do I always charge less than I know I'm worth?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Because the price was yours to set and you set it alone, before anybody argued with it. Nobody turned you down. Nobody was even asked. What is running is a self-estimate being read as a market fact, and it shows up in four places: the number you name, the hours you never log, the money you never chase, and the order in which everybody gets paid.
</div>

<div class="identify-box" markdown="1">
These are composites rather than anybody's exact words, and they sit close to what actually gets said.

- "I already know the number is too low, and I say it again anyway."
- "I take something off the price before anyone has asked me to."
- "Asking to be paid feels like asking for a favor rather than collecting something I already earned."
- "The money is owed to me and I still haven't sent the message asking for it."
- "I can't explain why the bigger number will not come out of my mouth."

</div>

Somebody asks what it would cost.

You already have a number. It arrived before they finished the sentence, and it was not a wild one, because it came from what the work takes, what the result is worth, and what you know other people get for the same thing. In some cases you have been sitting with it for a day.

Then your mouth opens, or your thumb moves, and something smaller comes out. Not wildly smaller. A fifth off, or a third, or the round figure just below the one you meant. Attached to it, unasked for, is a piece of insulation: and if that is too much we can work something out, or that includes the extra bit, or honestly, whatever you think is fair.

Nobody argued with you. That is the part worth stopping on, because it is the part that gets skipped every time this comes up. There was no negotiation. Nothing crossed anybody's face. The smaller figure was produced by you, alone, in the gap between having a number and saying one, and it was produced so fast that it does not register as a decision at all.

One word needs handling before anything else, because it does two jobs on this site. This page uses discount and invoice literally: money you could have asked for and did not, and a real bill for real work that is really owed. Elsewhere here those words carry the other sense, the reflex of talking down a compliment or the feeling that help turns up with a bill attached to it. [Talking your own achievements down has its own page](/patterns/impostor-syndrome/) and does that work properly. This one is about a figure, in currency, that a real person was going to pay.

There is also a whole category of advice this page deliberately does not give, and the absence is a decision rather than an oversight. Nothing below tells you what to charge. No formula, no multiplier, no rule about tripling anything, no wording to reach for when somebody pushes back. All of it is kept out on purpose, because reaching for it moves the question from what is true about you into how you ought to be positioned in a market, and that swap is the fastest available way to spend a year on the wrong problem. What you say out loud is a copy of an estimate you already hold about yourself, and a formula cannot reach an estimate.

## Who Actually Turned You Down?

Nobody, and that single fact is what separates this from every neighboring pattern here.

Look at what is going on around it. Asking an employer for a raise runs on somebody else deciding. Waiting for an authorization runs on somebody else signing. Putting work in front of an audience runs on the audience coming back with a verdict. In all of those, a second person holds the outcome, and the fear you feel is a reasonable response to a real risk you cannot control.

Now look at this one. The price was yours to set. You set it low, alone, in a room where nobody had yet objected to anything. The hours were yours to log and you did not log them. The bill is already earned, already owed, already yours, and the message asking for it is two sentences you have not sent. The money coming in is yours by definition, and it finds a job before it finds you.

In none of those moments did anybody refuse you. In most of them nobody was even asked.

That is what this page is about: a transaction with one participant, and the participant loses. Rejection cannot be the mechanism, because nothing was ever put to anybody. What is running instead is a self-estimate being read as a fact about the market. You behave as though an authority still has to be granted to you, in the one area of life where you are unambiguously the person who decides.

The four surfaces are the same act standing at four distances from the money.

**Before it: the figure.** The gap between the number you hold and the number you say.

**During it: the hours.** Work done and never counted. The extra bit added because it was quicker to do it than to mention it. The Saturday that never appeared on anything.

**After it: getting paid.** Money already earned, already agreed, sitting somewhere else, and no message sent about it.

**Underneath it: the order of payment.** Everybody who touched the thing gets paid, and then, if anything is left, you do.

Most people recognize two of those four and have never once seen them as the same move. Somebody can be forceful about the figure and hopeless at getting paid. Somebody else names a number without blinking and has not counted a working hour in years. Put the four next to each other and the common element stops hiding, because there is only one: in every case you are the person on both sides of the decision, and the side that gives way is always yours.

The difference against everything nearby, in one line. Almost every other page here is about what somebody else might do to you. This one is about what you have already done to yourself, before anyone else was involved.

## Is a Low Price Ever the Right Decision?

Often, and this needs saying immediately, before the rest of the page makes you suspicious of every number you have ever named.

Plenty of people set a low figure on purpose and are entirely correct to. You are new at something and buying repetitions. You want the case, the reference or the photographs more than you want the money this month. You are opening a door with somebody you have wanted to work with for years. You have decided that the first dozen of anything are cheap because a dozen is what it takes to get good. You are doing it for family, or for somebody having a bad year, and you would rather have the relationship than the difference. Every one of those is a real decision, made by an adult, with a reason that survives being said out loud, and this page has no standing whatsoever to second-guess it.

So the two have to be separable, because they look identical from outside. Two people name the same figure on the same afternoon for work of the same quality. One of them chose it. The other could not get the bigger one out.

Four things tell them apart, and you can run all four on your own last quote without asking anybody.

**A chosen price has an ending.** The person who decided it can name the date, the number of clients, or the event at which it stops. If the answer to when does this stop is a feeling rather than a marker, it was never chosen.

**A chosen price came before the moment.** It was settled in advance, sitting somewhere written down, and the same figure gets said to everybody who asks that week. A figure that changes depending on who is asking and how they sounded was decided in the exchange, not before it.

**A chosen price does not drift down.** A deliberate introductory number is stable. The other kind keeps finding new reasons to fall: they seem stretched, it took less time than expected, they were nice about it.

**A chosen price leaves nothing behind.** You say it, they pay it, you think about something else. The other kind is still with you that evening.

If you ran those and the answer came back chosen, you can close this page with a clear conscience. The rest is written for the version where the number left your mouth on its own.

## Where Does This Show Up If You Never Send a Bill in Your Life?

In most of a week, and only a small share of it involves anything you would call a fee.

This is where the pattern gets missed. People read a page like this, decide it belongs to the self-employed, and skip it. The behavior has nothing to do with being self-employed. It shows up wherever a number about your own worth has to leave your body and land in front of somebody, and that happens to the reliable one at work, the one in the family who fixes things, the one holding a group together, and anybody who does it for a living, in the same week and often on the same day.

| Where it happens | What it looks like from outside | What is running underneath |
|---|---|---|
| A quote for work you do for a living | The round figure just under the one you meant | The self-estimate gets reported, and the report arrives faster than any thought about the job |
| A relative asks you to do their accounts | Don't be silly, nothing, honestly | Naming a figure would make the skill an exchange, and both cannot be held at once |
| An employer asks what you would need to take something extra on | A number you would have accepted anyway | The budget was theirs and you priced it from yours |
| Selling something you own | Listed below what it is worth, and dropped again after the first reply | Any hesitation is read as a verdict on the price rather than as ordinary hesitating |
| A group or a club where you keep the books | Hours that go on no list, including yours | Unpaid work has no rate attached, so the hours become free by default and stay that way |
| The extra thing you added to the job | Done in an evening and never mentioned | Mentioning it would need a number, and the number is the part that will not come |
| A friend asks what you charge | The real figure, said easily, to somebody who is not buying | With no exchange attached, nothing consults the self-estimate at all |
| An arrangement running on a figure set years ago | Never revisited, by either of you | Raising it now would mean the old number was wrong, and saying that out loud is the actual cost |

Read the last two rows against each other, because together they do more work than the rest of the table. The same person, the same skill, the same week. One conversation is effortless and one is impossible, and the only thing that changed between them is whether money was going to move. That puts the difficulty somewhere other than your knowledge of what things are worth, which you have, and which you use fluently the moment nothing is at stake.

## What Are Money Scripts, and Is That What This Is?

Money scripts are the beliefs about money a person carries and mostly never examines. It is the closest named thing to what this page describes, it is genuinely useful, and it covers about half of what is going on.

The idea is straightforward. Long before anybody hands you a figure to name, you have absorbed a set of propositions about what money is, who is allowed to have it, what wanting it says about a person, and what happens to people who ask for more. They were absorbed rather than decided. Almost nobody can recite theirs. They behave like facts about the world rather than like opinions, which is exactly why they never get argued with.

A 2011 paper in the *Journal of Financial Therapy* put the first published version of this into a form other people could use. It gathered 422 people, had them rate their agreement with 72 statements about money, and found four belief patterns in the answers: money avoidance, money worship, money status and money vigilance. Three of the four were significantly correlated with the income and the net worth the same people reported.

I am naming the paper rather than the researchers behind it, and that is deliberate. Two of its four authors share a surname and are two different people, so any single name attached to it would hand one person credit for work that four did. The year, the journal and the sample size are enough for anybody to find it and read it themselves, which is the only thing an attribution is for.

Take the four names loosely, because their usefulness is in recognition rather than in classification. Money avoidance is the belief that money is somehow unclean, that wanting it is a character flaw, and that people who have it got it dishonestly. Money worship is the belief that more of it would fix what is wrong. Money status is the belief that what you have is what you are worth. Money vigilance is the belief that it must be watched constantly, never discussed, and never quite enjoyed.

Look at the first one for a moment, because it is the one that sits directly under this page. If wanting money is faintly shameful, then a full price is a public act of wanting, and a lower one is the cleaner thing to be seen doing. The person is not being generous. They are avoiding an accusation that nobody in the room has made or is going to make.

The folk word for the outcome is **underearning**: consistently taking in less than your skill, your hours and your market would support, with no outside reason for the gap. It is a plainer word than the research one and it points at the same country. It also has no measure behind it, nothing scores it, and nobody records it anywhere, so treat it as a useful description of a pattern rather than as something a person can be found to have.

Now the honest limits, which matter more here than the definitions do.

**The evidence is people describing themselves.** Everything in that 2011 paper comes from people rating statements about their own beliefs. That is a reasonable way to find out what somebody thinks and a poor way to find out anything they do not know about themselves.

**The sample was not the general population.** 422 people who agreed to answer is a real number and it is not a random slice of anywhere. The four patterns are what fell out of that particular set of answers.

**The associations are associations.** Three of the four belief patterns tracked with income and net worth. Nothing in that tells you which way any arrow points, and belief following circumstance is at least as plausible as the reverse.

**The follow-up work is narrow.** A 2016 study in the same journal tested a shortened version on 326 college students and reported high reliability and weak to moderate agreement with an older measure of money attitudes. Useful, and a long way from establishing that these categories describe you.

**And the largest limit of all.** The term names the what and it never names the why. It can tell you that you hold a belief about money being faintly shameful. It cannot tell you whether that arrived from a household where money was never mentioned, from a stretch of years where there genuinely was none, from one humiliating exchange at nineteen, or from watching somebody you loved get treated badly over a figure. Those four people will score identically and need completely different things.

That is the whole reason this page keeps going after the name. A name can be looked up in an afternoon. Which of the reasons below is yours cannot be looked up at all.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

In five situations, and none of them is the ordinary version everything else here is written for. This section sits ahead of anything practical on purpose, because the practical part is useless and occasionally harmful if one of these is what is actually happening.

**If money has stopped being a subject and become an emergency.** Payments going unmade, an amount owed that grows faster than anything coming in, letters left unopened for weeks. That is a situation rather than a pattern, and it needs somebody with the relevant training and a look at the actual numbers. Free services exist for exactly this in most countries and they are considerably better at it than any article. Sort the emergency first. The pattern will still be here afterward and will be much easier to see once the noise has come down.

**If somebody else decides what you may spend, and asking about it costs you.** Where your access to money is controlled by another person, where a question about it produces a consequence, or where leaving would be financially impossible, none of this page applies. That is a safety matter and it belongs with somebody qualified now rather than later. Please do not read a pattern into a situation that is being done to you.

**If a flatness has sat over everything for months.** Regardless of what you do, on the good days as well as the bad ones, with sleep that has stopped returning. Or if there is any thought of harming yourself, however briefly it passes through and however reasonable it sounds while you are having it. That goes to a licensed professional today rather than at some point. Nothing on this page is urgent and all of it can wait.

**If the low figure is simply accurate.** Some markets genuinely do not pay more, some employers genuinely have a ceiling, and some regions genuinely price the same work at a fraction of what it fetches elsewhere. If you have checked what comparable people actually receive and the answer is close to what you receive, then what you have is an economic fact rather than a self-estimate, and the useful move is about where you are rather than about what you believe. The tell is straightforward: your figure is the same as everybody else's, and it does not drop further when the person asking sounds hesitant.

**If it arrived suddenly with no change to attach it to.** Something you have done since you were twenty-two behaves differently from something that started in March. A distinct change in how you handle money, with no corresponding change in your circumstances, is worth a conversation with a doctor rather than an article about a pattern.

For everybody else, which is nearly everybody reading, the rest of it applies.

## What Are the Five Reasons a Number Comes Out Low?

Five, and the reason it matters which one is yours is that they produce an identical figure and need completely different things.

Nothing below is a sequence and nothing below has to be true before anything else can be. These are five separate reasons a person arrives at the same behavior, and most people find one of them strongly with a second running faintly underneath. Reading all five and recognizing none is also a result worth having, because it usually means the figure was chosen and the earlier section applies.

| The reason | What the low number is actually doing | How you would know it is this one |
|---|---|---|
| The figure is a report on your own budget | Pricing the work at whatever you personally could stand to hand over | You can name your own limit instantly, and it sits suspiciously close to what you ask for |
| The low number is buying something | Purchasing a yes that was never going to be refused | Acceptance produces relief rather than satisfaction |
| Nobody ever demonstrated naming a price | Building the sentence live, every time, with nothing to copy | You cannot recall one occasion of anybody saying a figure out loud in front of you |
| A bigger number is a claim you would have to meet | Promising less, so that less can be held against you afterward | The reluctance scales with how well the person knows you and how long they will remember |
| Giving is fluent and receiving is not | Keeping the whole exchange pointed in the direction that works | It shows up identically in situations with no money in them at all |

Two of those are about the figure, one is about a skill that was never available to copy, one is about exposure, and the last one has nothing to do with money and shows up in money because money is where it gets measured.

That distribution is worth holding onto, because almost everything written about charging too little addresses the fourth one and addresses it exclusively. Confidence, self-belief, knowing your value. It is written for the person whose difficulty is the exposure, and for that person some of it is genuinely useful. For the other four it lands as a description of a character flaw they do not have, which is why so many people read it, agree with all of it, and name the same figure the following week.

## Why Do I Price Things By What I Could Afford to Pay?

Because the only price you have ever had unrestricted access to is your own, so it becomes the ruler, and a ruler does not feel like an opinion.

Watch what actually happens in the second before a figure comes out. You are not calculating hours multiplied by anything. You are running a fast check against a much older question: would I pay that? And whatever you personally would find steep is the number that arrives as too much, whatever your own circumstances happen to be that month, and whatever the person asking earns.

That makes the figure a report about your relationship with money rather than about the work in front of you. It travels with your own situation. People in this reason can watch their own prices track their bank balance rather than their skill, dropping in a lean quarter and creeping up in a good one, for work that did not change at all.

The cleanest way to see it running is to price for somebody else. Take a friend with your skills and your years and describe their situation to yourself, then name what they should ask. Almost everybody produces a bigger figure instantly, defends it without effort, and feels no discomfort at all. Same information, same market, different subject. If your number for them is comfortably above your number for you, the extra variable is not in the market.

The move here has nothing to do with confidence. It is arithmetic that has been running with one number substituted for another. What a thing is worth to somebody who wants it, and what you personally would find comfortable to spend, are two separate quantities, and only one of them has any business setting a price.

## What Am I Actually Buying With the Lower Number?

Proof that somebody wanted it, bought at a price low enough that turning it down would have meant nothing either way.

The difficulty is what kind of yes it is. Somebody agreeing to a full price has told you something: they weighed it and decided the thing was worth that. Somebody agreeing to a figure well under the going rate has told you almost nothing, because at that price they would have agreed to a lot of things. So the purchase is real, the currency is real, and the item delivered is worthless for the purpose it was bought for.

Which is exactly why it has to be bought again. Evidence that settles a question stops needing to be gathered. This kind never settles anything, so the next quote goes out low as well, and the one after that, and a decade later there is a long record of people saying yes and not one occasion where anybody paid full price for something of yours.

Doing it for nothing at all is the same transaction with the price taken all the way down. The reasons offered for free work are usually sound in isolation, and the tell sits somewhere other than the reasoning. Look at what happens afterward. Work given away as a genuine decision has an edge on it, an ending, and a person who feels fine about it. Work given away in this pattern extends, quietly, and generates a small residue of resentment nobody can quite justify.

Four tells, and the first is the one to trust.

**Acceptance produces relief rather than satisfaction.** A yes at a fair figure feels like a good outcome. A yes at this figure feels like getting away with something, and the difference is unmistakable once you have felt both.

**The number falls further the moment they pause.** Not after an objection. After a pause. The silence in which somebody is reading their calendar gets read by you as a verdict on the price.

**You cannot remember the last refusal.** Ask yourself when somebody last declined a figure of yours. If the honest answer is that you cannot recall one, that is a record of never having tested anything rather than a record of persuasive pricing.

**The feeling afterward is worse rather than better.** Something got confirmed and the wrong something. You proved you could be afforded.

## Why Have I Never Heard Anybody Say a Price Out Loud?

Because in a great many households money was settled somewhere else and then announced, so there was simply nothing to copy.

Think about what was actually available to watch. In some houses the money conversation happened behind a door and arrived as a decision. In some it was the tense subject, so everybody handled it briefly and moved on. In some there was never enough, which made every figure a source of worry rather than a thing to be named calmly. And in some it was simply never mentioned, on the understanding that mentioning it was coarse.

None of those supplies a demonstration. What a demonstration looks like is one adult saying a figure to another adult, in a normal voice, and then stopping talking while the other person decides. If you have never seen that done, you are not blocked from doing it. You have never seen it done.

The way to tell this apart from the reason after it is to check whether the sentence exists at all. Somebody with the fourth reason knows exactly what they want to say and cannot get it out in front of a particular person. Somebody here has never had a settled sentence in any room, with anybody, including people they are entirely comfortable with. Same silence, different cause.

## Why Does a Bigger Number Feel Like a Claim I Would Have to Live Up To?

Because it is one, and the discomfort is an accurate reading of what you are about to take on rather than a failure of nerve.

A figure is a statement about what is going to be delivered. Say a large one and you have told somebody what to expect, in a form they will remember precisely, and against which the result can be compared afterward by a person with a good reason to compare it. Say a small one and you have promised very little. If the work turns out beautifully, that is a bonus. If it turns out ordinary, nobody feels short-changed. The asymmetry is not imaginary, and a person who has once charged well and then had a difficult month afterward is not being cowardly. They are pricing a risk they have already met.

So the low figure is doing something quite precise: it gets the doubting in first, which saves everybody else the trouble of doing it later. The same move exists in a version with no money in it at all, where the currency is the size of the claim you make about your own work, and [that page states the mechanism more cleanly than there is room for here](/patterns/i-cant-say-what-i-actually-do/). From there it runs on to [the running commentary people attach to their own answers](/patterns/putting-myself-down/), which is the same instinct wearing everyday clothes.

The tells for this reason are unusually specific, which makes it the easiest of the five to confirm.

**The reluctance scales with memory.** It is at its worst with people you will see again, mildest with somebody passing through, and almost absent in writing to a stranger in another country. Nothing about the work changed between those. What changed is how long the figure will be remembered and by whom.

**You can write it and not say it.** The same number typed into a message goes out with mild discomfort. Spoken, in a room, to a face, it will not form. That gap points at the audience rather than at the arithmetic.

**There is a specific previous occasion.** Most people in this reason can name it without having to think. One time they asked for a proper figure, got it, and then something about the work went sideways, and the memory of explaining that is doing more work in the present than the actual risk warrants.

## Why Is Giving Easy and Receiving Hard?

Because one direction has been practiced for most of your life and the other has not, and money is simply the place where the imbalance finally produces a number.

This is the reason with the most evidence available to you, and almost none of the evidence is financial. Watch what happens across an ordinary week. Somebody compliments something you made and you find the flaw in it out loud before they have finished the sentence. Somebody offers to help you move something and you say you have it, while carrying it badly. Somebody reaches for the bill and you get there first, quickly, with something close to urgency. A neighbor offers a lift and you would genuinely rather walk in the rain.

None of that is about money and all of it is the same machinery. Sending things outward is smooth, automatic and mildly pleasurable. Taking anything in is effortful, and it comes with a small debt attached that you begin repaying before the thing has even landed. [The general difficulty with asking has a page of its own](/patterns/why-cant-i-ask-for-help/), and [the version where an offer arrives and gets turned down reflexively has another](/patterns/refusing-help/). Both go further into where this comes from than there is room for here.

What money adds is measurement. Refuse enough offers of help and there is nothing to count, so the pattern stays invisible for decades and passes as being self-sufficient, which is a compliment. Do the same thing with fees and unlogged hours and money owed, and it produces a figure. Every year that goes by, that figure is bigger, and at some point somebody works it out and gets a shock.

## Why Haven't I Sent the Message Asking to Be Paid What I'm Owed?

Because sending it feels like asking for something, and everything in you knows the difference between asking and collecting even when the facts say collecting.

Take the situation exactly as it stands. The work is finished. The figure was agreed in advance by both of you. The date it was due has passed. The message required is two sentences long, contains no request, no argument and nothing anybody could object to, and would take almost no time to write. It has not been sent. In many cases it has been drafted, left, opened again in the evening, improved, and left again.

I mean invoice literally here, and it is worth saying so plainly, because elsewhere on this site the word does other work. There is no metaphor in this section. Somebody owes you actual money for actual work and the message about it is sitting unsent.

What makes it heavy is a change of role that the sending performs. Up to that point you have been the person who did the thing, which is a comfortable position. Send the message and you become the person who wants something, which is not. Nothing about the money has changed. The whole cost is in the reclassification, and it happens the moment you press send rather than when they reply.

The delay then makes itself worse in a way most people notice and cannot stop. After a week the message would be routine. Nine weeks later it appears to need an explanation, so you compose one, and the composing is what stops it going out for a third month. [What happens to a message once the first one has gone unanswered has its own page](/patterns/the-second-message/), and it covers the general version of that in social and working life, well beyond money. What stays here is the one specific act of asking to be paid for work already delivered, because it is the only version where you are collecting a fact rather than making a request.

Here is the tell that settles it. Imagine the identical money is owed to somebody you care about, and the identical message needs writing. Most people find they would write it without thinking twice, warmly, with no discomfort at all, and would think nothing more about it. Same facts, same wording, different person owed. Whatever is running is not about the message.

## Why Does Everyone Else Get Paid Before I Do?

Because everybody else in the arrangement produces an invoice or a due date, and you produce whatever is left over, which is not a payment at all.

Look at the order of events in almost any month. The people who helped are paid. The supplier is paid. The tax is set aside, the subscriptions go out, the shared costs get covered, the person who needs it this month gets helped. Then you look at what remains and take that. Nobody decided you should go last. It was never a policy. What happened is that everyone else in the sequence had a fixed amount attached to a date, and you had neither, so you became the part of the arrangement that flexes.

This reaches well past anybody running a business. It shows up identically in a household where one person's share is quietly the elastic one, in a family where the same relative absorbs every shortfall, and in any group where somebody is covering the gaps and has stopped counting. The folk name people use for it is profit last, and it is a good name for a specific reason: it describes a position in an order of payment rather than a fault in a person.

The trouble with being the flexible part is that flexible has no floor. A fixed amount either gets paid or does not, and the failure is visible. A residual can be nothing at all, several times in a row, and nothing anywhere records that it happened. There is no bad month on paper. There is only a person who has not been paid in a while and has not mentioned it.

Then there is what the arrangement does to your self-image, which is the part that keeps it in place. Going last looks like integrity. It reads as a person who takes care of everybody, holds the risk, and does not complain, and there is something real in that reading. The problem is that it operates as one old default nobody has ever looked at, wearing the clothes of a virtue, rather than as a decision taken afresh each month, and virtues are notoriously difficult to reconsider.

Two neighbors handle the pieces this section touches and do it better. If what you recognize is money going the other way, the shortfall that makes hiring anybody impossible, and the work that therefore stays with you forever, [that page owns it properly](/patterns/the-work-i-never-hand-over/). And if the money does arrive and then vanishes without anything to show for it, so that a raise or a good year leaves no trace anywhere, [that is a separate mechanism with its own page](/patterns/where-did-the-raise-go/).

## Is This the Same as Not Being Able to Ask My Boss for a Raise?

No, and the difference is the whole reason this page exists separately, so it is worth a short detour.

Asking an employer for more money has a second person in it who holds the answer. You state a figure, and then you wait, and somebody with authority over your situation decides. The waiting is the hard part, the decision is genuinely outside your control, and what you are exposed to is a refusal that will still be true on Monday. That is a real and difficult thing, [and it is answered properly on the page about being unable to ask even when you have clearly earned it](/patterns/fear-of-rejection/), which treats it as a case of anticipating a refusal, because that is exactly what it is.

Nothing in this page has that structure. The number here is yours. Nobody has to approve it, nobody grants it, and at the moment the loss happens there is no second person in the room at all. That is why advice built for the raise conversation lands so oddly on this: it is all about how to hold your position while somebody considers it, and here there is nobody considering anything. You already lost the money before the conversation started.

The quick way to tell which you are in on any given day: ask who would have to agree with you for the money to change. If the answer is a person, that is the other page. If the answer is nobody, that is this one.

## Is This About My Worth as a Person, or the Price of the Work?

The price of the work, and keeping those two apart is most of the useful work available here.

They get fused constantly, and the fusing is what makes a routine commercial exchange feel like a referendum. A figure gets refused, or even just questioned, and what registers internally is not that a budget did not stretch. What registers is a verdict on you. That reading is available at speed because the two quantities have been treated as one for a very long time.

[The page on worth attached to output goes into this properly](/patterns/conditional-self-worth/), including a distinction worth borrowing before you read any further here: one kind of worth behaves like a price, because it describes what you bring to somebody who needs it, and the other kind carries no price at all, because it does not depend on what you produce. Everything on this page concerns the first kind. A figure is a claim about what a piece of work is worth to a person who wants it, on a particular week, in a particular market.

That distinction has a practical edge to it. If a price is a fact about an exchange, it can be wrong in either direction without meaning anything about you. Quote too high and you find out. Quote too low and you find out later, expensively. Both are information, both are ordinary, and neither belongs in a case about your value as a person.

## Where Does This Hide When You Would Not Call It Money at All?

In seven places, and most of them read as good qualities, which is why the pattern survives being looked at directly.

**In being easy to work with.** No discussion of terms, no awkwardness about what is included, nothing to negotiate because there is nothing to push back against. People genuinely appreciate this and say so. It is also a complete way of never having a conversation about money, and the appreciation makes it self-reinforcing.

**In always rounding downward.** The figure that arrives is invariably the round number below rather than the one above, every single time, which is a coin that has landed the same way for the last several years and ought to have been noticed by now.

**In doing the extra thing instead of mentioning it.** Something was outside what was agreed, and raising it would take a sentence containing a number, so you do it in an evening and say nothing. Each instance is small and defensible. The set of them across a year is not.

**In never revisiting an old arrangement.** A figure set years ago that has quietly become a fraction of what the work is now worth, kept in place because changing it would mean saying out loud that the current one is wrong.

**In the family rate.** Often entirely genuine and entirely fine. The tell is whether you feel anything about it afterward, and whether it has spread from the people you love to their friends, their colleagues, and people you have met once.

**In not counting hours anywhere.** No record exists, so no comparison is possible, so nothing ever surfaces. People in this pattern often describe record keeping as unnecessary for the kind of work they do, and it is the one thing that would make the whole thing visible in a month.

**In only ever working with people who could not have paid more.** This is the one to sit with, because it is invisible from inside and it settles the question permanently. Choose the situations carefully enough and you will never meet evidence that contradicts your estimate, which makes your own reading of it feel like knowledge about the market rather than a belief about yourself.

Read the last one against the first. Both are choices that feel like personality. Neither has ever been examined. And between them they arrange a working life in which the question this page asks can never come up.

## What Has One Old Low Figure Already Cost You?

More than the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is already worse than most people expect.

**The money is the smallest item and it is not small.** Take the gap between the figure you name and the figure you meant, multiply it by everything you have done in a decade, and the number is uncomfortable. Almost nobody in this pattern has ever run that calculation, and the reluctance to run it is itself part of the pattern.

**Your own old numbers become the ruler.** This is the part that compounds. Next time you need a figure, the strongest available reference point is what you asked last time, which was low, and the new one gets set just above it. A decade of that produces a schedule of prices entirely disconnected from what the work became, built out of nothing but your own earlier estimates of yourself.

**The volume goes up to compensate.** A low figure means more of it is needed, which means longer weeks, which means no room to get better at the thing, which keeps the figure where it is. It is a closed loop and it can run for years without anybody noticing that the cause and the symptom are feeding each other.

**Other people take you at your own valuation.** Not out of any calculation. A figure carries information about how the work should be regarded, and people absorb it without deciding to, then treat the work accordingly. The supervision, the last-minute changes and the casual rescheduling that follow all read as disrespect and are usually just the price being believed.

**The resentment has nowhere to go.** It builds anyway, and there is no legitimate target for it, because nobody did anything. They paid what was asked. So it settles on the client, the family member, the organization, or on yourself, none of which is accurate, and all of which quietly damage something.

**And the information never arrives.** After all these years, you still do not know what your work is actually worth, because you have never once put a real figure in front of somebody and found out. That is the item that never appears on any list, and it is the one that would have been most useful.

## Where Does This End and a Different Pattern Begin?

At six borders, and getting the border right matters because each neighbor needs a different thing from you.

**The thing that was never begun at all.** If there is a set of numbers you worked out properly once, wrote down, and have shown to nobody, the difficulty is in the starting rather than in the figure. [That page owns the whole territory of waiting for an authorization that is not coming](/patterns/waiting-for-permission/), and it is a better use of the time than the rest of this one. The two are close to being opposites. There, the number was reasonable and never left the room. Here, it left the room and arrived smaller than it started.

**The agreement that got made before you decided anything.** If the recognizable event is the word yes arriving before you have thought, so that you are committed to something and only then work out what it costs you, [that page names its own scope precisely and owns that moment](/patterns/saying-yes-when-you-mean-no/). Money is often what the yes turns out to have cost, and the mechanism is in the speed of the agreement rather than in the figure.

**Declining a request and holding the line afterward.** If what you cannot do is say no and then stay there while somebody reacts, [that is covered properly elsewhere](/patterns/how-to-set-boundaries/), including why the scripts that circulate for it do not work. This page does not go near that, because the hard part here happens with nobody reacting to anything.

**Talking down what you have already achieved.** Praise, results, credit, a compliment about the work. If the reflex you recognize is the deflection rather than the pricing, [that belongs on the page about feeling like a fraud](/patterns/impostor-syndrome/), where the currency is recognition rather than money.

**Not being able to say what the work even is.** If the sentence that fails is the description rather than the figure, so that you cannot compress what you do into anything a stranger can file, [that is a different difficulty with its own page](/patterns/i-cant-say-what-i-actually-do/), and it frequently sits underneath a pricing problem, because it is hard to price something you cannot name.

**Money that arrives and then disappears.** If the earning is fine and the trouble starts once it lands, so that a good year leaves no trace anywhere, [that mechanism runs the other way and is worked through separately](/patterns/where-did-the-raise-go/).

One more, and it needs care rather than a link alone. There is [a page here about the many ways people get in their own way](/insights/why-do-i-self-sabotage/), and money appears on it as a single line in a table. Read it if it is useful. Do not carry its heading back to this behavior. The word people reach for there is one they use about themselves, and handing it back to somebody as a category for what they do with a price is the one move on this whole subject that would be genuinely unkind. Nothing described on this page is anybody attacking their own interests. Every one of the five reasons is a person doing something that made sense at the time and has kept running past its usefulness.

## How Good Is the Evidence Behind Any of This?

Thin in the places that matter most, and mapping the thinness precisely is more useful to you than any amount of confidence would be.

**Nothing here was measured on this behavior.** There is no study of people naming a figure lower than the one in their head. The research that exists nearby is about beliefs people hold about money in general, and carrying it across to the moment a price leaves somebody's mouth is my extension rather than anybody's finding. Weigh it as an argument.

**The money beliefs work is descriptive and it is self-described.** People rating statements about themselves tells you what they think and very little about anything they do not know. The 2011 sample was 422 people who agreed to answer, which is a real number and not a slice of any population. Its correlations with income and net worth are correlations, and a belief following circumstance is at least as plausible as circumstance following belief.

**The follow-up sits in a narrow group.** 326 college students, high internal reliability, weak to moderate agreement with an older measure. That establishes that the shortened version behaves consistently. It does not establish that these four categories carve up the world.

**The four category names are useful and slightly too tidy.** Real people produce mixtures, contradictions, and beliefs that switch depending on the month. Any set of four names for something this large is a convenience, and the researchers who built it would say the same.

**Underearning has no evidence behind it at all.** It is a folk description. It circulates widely, it is often attached to programs that charge money to fix it, and nothing measures it. Useful as a word, worthless as a verdict.

**The five reasons are mine.** They come from listening to roughly seventeen to twenty different people describe this behavior in detail, which is a small number, gathered in one setting, from people who had already decided to come and talk about themselves. No figure exists for how common each reason is, and inventing one would be exactly the sort of thing this section is here to complain about.

**What survives all of that** is modest and checkable against your own last several months. That people carry unexamined beliefs about money that behave like facts. That a figure you set alone, with nobody objecting, cannot be explained by a fear of being refused. That the same low figure comes from several different places. And that pricing for somebody else and pricing for yourself produce different answers from the same person on the same afternoon. None of that is dramatic, and all of it is more than the confident advice in this territory usually supplies.

## Where Does This Thinking Come From?

From one small body of published work anybody can check, from several things I have deliberately left out, and from a much larger amount of listening to people describe this in rooms.

On beliefs about money, the paper this page uses is the 2011 study in the *Journal of Financial Therapy* that produced the first published set of money script statements. It gathered 422 people, had them rate their agreement with 72 items, and reported four belief patterns, three of which correlated significantly with the income and net worth those people reported. A 2016 paper in the same journal examined a shortened version with 326 college students and reported high reliability along with weak to moderate agreement against an older measure of money attitudes. Both are open access and both are short.

I have named those two papers by year, journal and design rather than by author, on purpose. The 2011 paper carries two authors who share a surname and are two different people, so attaching one name to it would credit four people's work to one of them, and describing it as one researcher and colleagues would do the same thing more quietly. Naming the year, the journal and the sample size is enough for anybody to find it, which is what an attribution is for.

Several things are deliberately absent. I have not used the well-known line of work on what merely handling money appears to do to how people behave afterward, because its best-known researcher is already named on another page here for a completely different finding, and using the same surname twice would make two separate arguments look like one reading list. I also measured a handful of researchers working on money attitudes who have never been cited anywhere on this site, and left every one of them unused, on the grounds that a fresh name is worth more to a site this size than a longer bibliography is worth to a single page.

The first-person lines in the box near the top of this page are composites. They were written to the shape of what people in this pattern say, and no individual's wording, situation or detail appears in any of them. That is a deliberate departure from how the quoted lines on most pages here work, and it is not going to change on this page, because the material behind it came from conversations in which nobody was speaking for publication. The behavior counts are handled the same way: roughly seventeen to twenty distinct people, and nothing more precise than that, because a more precise figure would be a step toward identifying somebody.

The five reasons are mine and carry no research weight at all. I spent five years and more than five thousand hours doing this kind of work on myself before I built anything around it, and more than fifty thousand dollars on seminars, therapy, books, and teachers, some found in the jungles of Vanuatu, some in the jungles of Peru. I hold no clinical qualification of any kind, and I am not a therapist. None of the researchers behind the papers described above has any connection to this page or to the seminar. Where their published work and my reading of it disagree, theirs is the one to go and check.

## What Actually Moves This?

Five things, in this order, and not one of them is a price.

Nothing below is a tested protocol and anybody offering you one for this would be overselling it. It is worked out from how the failure happens, and it is built so that one person can start this week without telling anybody, changing an arrangement, or having a single difficult conversation.

1. **Record the two numbers.** For one month, every time a figure leaves you in any direction, write down what arrived in your head and what came out of your mouth, plus who was asking. Change nothing. Attempt no improvement. The gap between the two columns is the entire finding, and almost nobody has ever seen it written down. What surprises people is how steady the gap is, and how little it varies with the work, the person or the month.
2. **Price somebody else on paper.** Once, deliberately, in writing. Take a person with your skills and your years, describe their situation as if it were somebody else's, and write down what they should ask for. Then put your own figure next to it. That comparison isolates the extra variable faster than any amount of reflection, because it holds everything constant except who the subject is.
3. **Work out which of the five is running.** Use the tells rather than introspection, because every one of them points at something that has already happened and can be checked. Do you feel relief or satisfaction when somebody accepts. Can you remember a single occasion of anybody naming a figure out loud in front of you. Does the reluctance change with how well the person knows you. Two of those will agree with each other, and where they agree is your answer.
4. **Count what is already sitting there.** Unlogged hours, extras done in an evening, money outstanding, an arrangement that has not moved in years, the total of what you have handled for free this quarter. One list. No action taken on any item. The counting is itself the change, because this pattern has always run on the absence of a record, and a record is unusually hard to argue with.
5. **Practice the physical act where nothing is at stake.** Saying a figure in a normal voice, and then stopping talking while the other person thinks. Selling something you own, asking what a job would cost, agreeing a split with a friend. This one is aimed squarely at the third reason, and if that is not yours, it will feel like a strange thing to be doing and can be skipped.

One thing that helps more than it should. Tell one person the gap. Not the figure, the gap: what you meant and what you said, on the most recent occasion, out loud, to somebody who will not repeat it. Something about the private version of this survives on never having been said, and a single ordinary conversation does more to it than a month of noticing on your own.

And one thing to leave alone for now. Do not change a price this week. Raising a figure before you know which of the five is running is how people put a new number in front of somebody, meet one entirely ordinary hesitation, drop below where they started, and walk away with the whole thing apparently confirmed. Find out what is producing the figure first. The figure will still be there in a month and will be much easier to move once you know what it has been reporting.

## What Does the Settled Version Look Like on an Ordinary Week?

Considerably duller than people expect, and it does not involve becoming somebody who is comfortable with money.

| Where it stands now | Where it goes | What that looks like on an ordinary week |
|---|---|---|
| The figure is decided in the moment | The figure was decided before the moment | You say the same number to the second person who asks that week as you said to the first |
| The number drops when they pause | The pause is allowed to be a pause | Somebody goes quiet, you say nothing, and they come back with a question about dates |
| Extras get done and never mentioned | Extras get mentioned, briefly, without an apology attached | One sentence, no preamble, and the conversation moves on |
| Nothing is counted anywhere | There is a record, kept for you rather than for anybody else | You know what you did last month without having to reconstruct it |
| An old arrangement is left where it is | An old arrangement gets one honest look a year | It goes up, or it does not, and either way it was considered |
| Money owed sits unmentioned for months | Money owed gets a short factual message | It goes out on the day you notice, and costs almost nothing |
| You are the flexible part of every payment | You are one of the fixed parts | Your share has an amount and a date, like everybody else's |
| A refused price reads as a verdict | A refused price reads as information | You are mildly interested rather than winded |

Look at the middle column and notice how little of it is about confidence. Nobody in it has become bold, none of them has learned a technique, and not one of them enjoys talking about money. What changed is that the figure stopped being generated live by a part of you that was never given the job.

Do not judge yourself if the left-hand column reads as an accurate description of the last several years. Every entry in it was solving something real at the time it was installed, and most of them were solving it well enough to keep being used.

## How Long Does This Take, Honestly?

Three different speeds, and running them together is why people abandon this in the third week.

The noticing moves first and it arrives at the wrong end. For the first couple of weeks you will catch it afterward, walking away, having already said the smaller figure. That feels like failure and it is the ordinary first stage. Somewhere around the fourth week most people start catching it during, in the second before they speak, which is a different experience entirely and does not yet change what comes out.

The behavior is slower and it moves in occasions rather than in weeks. The first time you hold a figure and stop talking will be genuinely unpleasant, and it will take about seven seconds, and you will replay it that evening. It takes several of those, spread out, before it stops being an event. What produces the change is having done it and survived, enough times that the nervous system stops filing it as news.

The private estimate underneath is the slowest of the three and it does not respond to a good outcome directly. Somebody can be naming a fair figure comfortably and still feel the old jolt for months, because the self-estimate was built across decades and one clean transaction does not overturn it. What tends to finally move it is external and slightly undignified: somebody paying a full figure without comment, or telling a third person what you charge in a tone that carries no surprise in it whatsoever.

Expect it to regress. A quiet quarter, a large unexpected expense, a job that goes badly, and the old figure comes straight back with a very good reason attached. That is what a change under load looks like rather than proof it never worked. The month after the pressure lifts is when it matters what you do.

Does it go away entirely? Most people describe it loosening rather than vanishing. The gap between the number you hold and the number you say gets narrower, the surfaces where you never noticed it start getting noticed, and at some point you catch yourself having said the figure you meant, without ceremony, and only realizing afterward that nothing dramatic happened.

## What Is One Thing to Do This Week, Before You Change a Single Price?

Write down two numbers every time money comes up, and change nothing at all.

Open a note on your phone. For the next seventeen days, every time a figure has to leave you in any form, a quote, a rate, a share of something, an asking price for a thing you are selling, write three lines afterward. What arrived in your head. What actually came out. Who was asking. That is the whole exercise. No improvement attempted, no resolution made, nobody told you are running it.

Seventeen days will give you somewhere between four and fifteen entries, and three things will be visible in them that no amount of thinking would have produced. The size of the gap, which is almost always steadier than people expect. Whether it moves with who is asking, which points at one of the five reasons rather than another.
