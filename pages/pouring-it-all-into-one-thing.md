# Why do I throw myself into work when something else in my life is wrong?

<div class="answer-box" markdown="1">
<span class="answer-label">The short answer</span>

Because the work is doing two jobs at once and both of them are real. It absorbs your attention, which keeps the other thing from arriving, and it produces something good, which is worth having. Neither fact is a defect on its own. The evening the project finishes is where you find out which one was carrying more.
</div>

<div class="identify-box" markdown="1">
This one usually gets recognized through a single sentence rather than through the general shape, so read these before anything else.

- "The best work of my life came out of the worst year of my life and I have never said those two things in the same breath."
- "I signed up for the half marathon nine days after she moved out. I told people it was a coincidence."
- "They gave a little speech about my commitment and I stood there knowing exactly what the commitment was standing on."
- "The campaign ended on a Thursday. By six-thirty on Friday I understood that nothing at home had changed at all."

</div>

Here is the shape of it, and the reason it is difficult to see is that every individual piece of it looks fine.

Something in your life goes wrong. A relationship ends, or does not end and becomes unbearable in a quieter way. Somebody gets ill. A parent starts declining. A friendship you thought was permanent turns out to have been conditional. Whatever it is, it is not the sort of thing that gets fixed inside a month, and there is a decent chance it is not entirely yours to fix at all.

And then, within a few weeks, the strongest working stretch of your career begins.

Not a coincidence, obviously, but also not the simple story. You do not sit down and decide to bury anything. There is no moment of choosing. What happens is that you find yourself able to work in a way you have never quite managed before. The distraction that used to eat your afternoons has gone. The reluctance that used to sit between you and a difficult task has gone. Twelve hours pass and feel like four. You are good at this anyway, and now you are good at it with nothing in the way, and the results start arriving in a form other people can see.

They notice. They say things. Somebody uses the word dedication, or discipline, or focus, and means it warmly. You take it and something goes slightly wrong on the way in, because you know a piece of context they do not have, and the piece of context is that this began nine days after the thing you have not told them about.

That is the pattern. Two facts, both true, both large, running at the same time in the same person, and never once permitted to stand next to each other. Not in conversation, which is understandable, and not even privately, which is the more interesting part.

Before anything else, one thing needs saying clearly, because most writing on this subject gets it wrong in the first paragraph and never recovers. **The pouring is usually fine.** In a hard season it is often the best available move, and the thing you built during it is genuinely yours and stays yours afterward. This page is not going to tell you that your work is a symptom, because for a good proportion of the people reading it, the work is the healthiest structure in the picture. What this page is interested in is one narrow moment, which is the evening the project finishes, because that is the only part of the whole arrangement you actually choose.

## What Does the Strongest Year of Your Career Actually Look Like From the Inside?

Frictionless, which is the part nobody warns you about.

The ordinary experience of working is that you have to push a bit. There is a small negotiation at the start of every session, and a smaller one every time you come back from a break, and across a week those negotiations add up to a real amount of effort spent on the business of getting yourself to work at all. During a stretch like this, that entire layer is missing. You sit down and you are already working. There is no gap between the intention and the action for anything to get into.

The second thing is the compression of everything else. Meals become functional. The messages you owe people sit unanswered for four days and then get answered in a batch, briefly and warmly, which is how nobody notices. The house gets slightly worse in a way you clock and file. Somewhere in here you stop reading anything that is not about the thing you are working on, and this feels like focus rather than like narrowing, because from the inside it is genuinely indistinguishable.

The third thing is that time behaves strangely. Weeks go by that you cannot really describe afterward. You know what you produced in them, precisely, because there is a record of that, and you would struggle to say what happened in them, because there is no record of that and nothing happened that needed one.

And the fourth thing, the one that makes the whole arrangement so stable, is that it works. The output is good. Often it is the best you have done. There is no point in a page like this pretending otherwise, because you have the evidence and you would stop reading. A story about hiding in work would predict mediocre work produced in enormous quantities. What actually happens is frequently the opposite, and the quality is precisely what makes the arrangement so hard to question.

## What Is the Name for This, and How Much of It Is Actually Evidence?

There is a name, it is old, and the honest answer about its evidence is uncomfortable enough that most pages using the word leave this part out.

The term is **sublimation**. It comes from psychoanalytic writing roughly a century old, and it describes taking an impulse that cannot be expressed directly and redirecting it into activity a culture values. Work. Art. Science. Sport. Looking after other people. The proposal was that this is what a person does with something that has nowhere else to go, and that unlike the other things a person might do with it, this one produces something. [Where the whole vocabulary of defense mechanisms came from, and who assembled it, is covered on the page about projection](/patterns/projecting/), which owns that history properly.

As a description of an experience, it is recognizable and useful, and it is why the word has survived for a hundred years while most of the theory around it did not. Something with nowhere to go arrives as effort. Most people who read that sentence know exactly what it refers to.

Now the part that has to be said plainly. **Sublimation has almost no experimental literature behind it.** It was never operationalized in a way that could have failed. There is no measurement of sublimation that could have come out the other way and forced anyone to abandon it. It was proposed, it was elegant, it explained a great deal, and it was absorbed into the general vocabulary on those grounds rather than on the strength of a result. If somebody tells you that sublimation is what you are doing, they have given you a word, and the word is doing all of the work in that sentence.

The claim you will meet most often is that sublimation is the **mature** defense, sitting at the top of a ranked list, with the crude ones at the bottom. That ladder deserves considerably more scepticism than it usually gets. It was assembled from clinical judgment about which defenses seemed healthier, and the standard instruments for rating a person's defenses ask trained raters to place them somewhere on that same ladder. The ranking is therefore an input to the measurement rather than an output of it. And maturity was defined partly by association with good adjustment in the first place, which makes the subsequent finding that mature defenses go with good adjustment do far less work than the phrasing suggests. [Compartmentalization gets ranked on the same ladder and is treated at length on the page about being confident at work and lost at home](/patterns/why-am-i-confident-at-work-and-lost-at-home/).

The researcher who did most to put any of this on an empirical footing is Cramer, who built a coding manual in 1991 that scored people's narratives for defenses with reported reliability between trained raters. It scores three: denial, projection and identification. Sublimation is not among them. Her 1998 paper in the Journal of Personality made the one distinction from this whole tradition that is worth carrying: coping is conscious and deliberately chosen, a defense is neither, and popular writing runs the two together constantly. By that definition, if you sat down in January and decided to put a bad year into a piece of work, what you did has a different name and a much better evidence base.

So the word names the shape of something accurately, and stops there. It says nothing about which of the five reasons below produced yours, and the five need entirely different things.

## When Does This Belong With Somebody Qualified Rather Than a Page Like This?

Earlier than most people in this pattern would ever consider asking, and the line is drawn by what the gaps feel like rather than by how many hours you work.

This page assumes something specific about your situation, and everything after this section depends on the assumption holding. It assumes the work is going well, that you are functioning, and that the difficulty in your life is the ordinary painful kind rather than the acute kind. If that is wrong, nothing below is the right instrument.

Read the next part carefully even if you are confident it does not apply. If the intensity has stopped producing anything and has become motion. If sleep has been broken for months in a way that has nothing to do with any particular deadline. If the empty intervals have gone from uncomfortable to genuinely frightening, or if you have started arranging your life so that no interval ever occurs. If something is being used to get through the working day or to come down from it in the evening. If the thing in your personal life involves your safety or somebody else's. In any of those cases the thing you are looking at has a different shape, and a page about how people work is the wrong tool for it entirely.

And if what you are carrying alongside this includes any thought of harming yourself, that belongs with a licensed professional now rather than later. Reaching early costs less than reaching after a year of decline, and nobody has ever regretted the early version.

One more, because it hides inside this pattern with unusual ease. If the hours are not actually yours to choose, if the demands are being set by somebody else and the difficulty at home is downstream of the schedule rather than the other way around, then what you have is a workload problem wearing a psychological costume. The correct move there is a conversation about the workload.

The rest of this is written for the ordinary version. The work is good, the year was hard, and both facts are sitting quietly in the same person without ever having been introduced.

## Is Pouring Yourself Into Something Actually a Problem?

Frequently it is the right call, and any page that opens by treating it as pathology has already lost the argument.

Consider what the realistic alternatives were. A person facing a situation that will take a year to resolve, or that will never resolve, or that belongs to somebody else entirely, has a limited menu. Sitting with it constantly is available and is not obviously better. Talking about it endlessly is available and reaches a point of diminishing returns faster than people admit. Numbing it is available and is worse in every direction. Against that menu, putting the year into something that produces a result is a strong option, and the result outlives the year.

There is also a straightforward practical version of this that has nothing psychological in it. Hard periods free up hours. A relationship ending returns you eight or ten hours a week that were previously spoken for. A difficult family situation often means you stop socializing, which returns more. Somebody with additional hours and an existing capacity for good work will produce more work, and that requires no inner mechanism at all to explain. A fair proportion of the overlap people find sinister is just arithmetic.

And there is a genuine argument, which almost nobody makes to people in this pattern, that changing how you live can move something that sitting and examining it will not. Working differently and living differently is a legitimate route, and it is a route that a lot of people have taken successfully without ever once naming what they were doing.

So the page does not proceed from the position that your work is a symptom. It proceeds from a much narrower observation: that the arrangement contains exactly one moment that is genuinely chosen, and that most people arrive at that moment with no idea it is the important one.

## What Do the Two Facts Look Like When You Put Them Next to Each Other?

Almost nobody has ever done it, which is the finding rather than the exercise.

Try it now, in whatever form is honest. The strongest professional stretch you have had, with its dates. The hardest personal stretch you have had, with its dates. Written down, in that order, one under the other.

For a large number of people reading this the two sets of dates will substantially overlap, and the more interesting reaction has very little to do with the overlap itself. What lands is the discovery that you have never assembled the joining sentence before. You knew both halves. You have told people about each half separately, probably more than once. The joining sentence, the one that contains both, has somehow never been constructed, not to a friend, not to a partner, and not silently to yourself on a long drive.

That separation is doing something. Whatever it is doing, it is not accidental, since the connection is not subtle and you are not unobservant. Two large facts about the same eighteen-month period do not stay unconnected in an intelligent person's head by chance.

This is also why the pattern almost never gets named from outside. The people who could see it only ever get one half. Colleagues have the output and know nothing about the year. The two or three people who know about the year are not watching the work closely enough to notice the timing. There is no single person in your life who holds both facts at once, unless you gave them to somebody deliberately, and the fact that you have not is part of the same arrangement.

## Why Does the Work Stop Working the Moment It Stops?

Because the mechanism doing the suppressing is the demand on your attention, and the demand ends when the work does.

The best-supported piece of this whole picture is also the least mysterious. Off-task thought falls as the demand on attention rises. This is one of the more reliable observations in the mind-wandering research, set out across the work of Smallwood and Schooler from 2006 onward, and it holds in an entirely unremarkable way: when a task requires more of you, less of your attention is available for anything else, including whatever is unresolved in your life. Absorption does nothing whatsoever to a problem. What it does is occupy the equipment the problem needs in order to present itself.

Two honest caveats attached to that, and both matter for how far you should push the idea.

The first is scale. Those studies measure minutes of laboratory task in undergraduates, with demands nothing like a fourteen-month project. Extending the result from a nine-minute task to a season of your working life is my extension, and it is a reasonable one, and it is not a thing anyone has actually measured. Treat it as a mechanism that makes sense of what you observe rather than as a proven account of it.

The second is that suppression during the task tells you nothing about what happens after. There is no reason from any of this literature to expect that occupied attention does anything to the underlying situation. And the everyday version of that is the thing people find genuinely shocking about the evening the project ends. The situation is not worse. Whatever was true in March is true in November, unchanged, at the same size, having waited without doing anything at all.

## What Is the Difference Between Engagement That Restores You and Engagement That Replaces Something?

The measured version of this distinction turns on the relationship between the activity and the rest of your life, and never on the activity itself.

The most useful research here is on the two forms a strong attachment to a self-defining activity can take, set out by Vallerand and colleagues in 2003. One form sits in harmony with the other parts of a life. It occupies a lot of room and leaves the other rooms standing. Persistence in it is flexible, so an interruption is an interruption rather than a threat. It ends without a crisis. The other form is taken on under a contingency, meaning something important about how you regard yourself is riding on it. Persistence in that version goes rigid. Conflict with other domains rises. Being prevented from doing it produces distress and a great deal of circling thought about it.

The important structural point is that both forms involve loving the activity, both involve very long hours, and both look identical from a distance. Somebody watching you work has no information that would separate them. Neither do you, if the only evidence you consult is how the work feels while you are doing it, because the work feels excellent in both.

**The separator is what happens when you cannot do it.** Not what you say about that, and not how you feel about it in principle. An enforced week away, an injury, a project killed above you, a machine that dies on a Sunday. One version is annoyed and reads a book. The other version finds the week genuinely unbearable in a way that is difficult to explain to anybody, and spends it circling.

The honesty note on this one: the scales are self-report, most of the evidence is cross-sectional correlation, and the two forms are separated by items that partly build the distinction into the measurement. It describes a real difference more reliably than it explains one. What it gives you that is genuinely useful is the location of the test. Not the hours, not the intensity, not the love, which are the three things everyone looks at. The stopping.

There is a related and better-established literature on what makes time away from demands actually restorative, and [it is covered on the page about staying up late](/patterns/staying-up-late/), which owns it. The short version relevant here is that detachment is the component that does the work, and detachment is precisely the thing a large absorbing project is best at preventing.

## Why Do Five People Pour Into the Same Project for Completely Different Reasons?

Because the visible behavior is one of the least informative things about a person, and this is a case where it is almost uninformative.

Five people, all working seventy-hour weeks on something they care about, all in the middle of a hard year, all producing excellent results. Identical from the outside, five separate things underneath, each needing a different response, and two of them needing no response at all.

## What If You Simply Love the Thing?

Then that is very probably the entire explanation, and it goes first because it is the answer most often steamrollered by an interesting theory.

There is a specific injustice that happens to people who work hard on something they love during a difficult period. Somebody who cares about them decides the work must be avoidance, says so, and cannot be argued out of it, because the pattern is unfalsifiable from outside: working a lot is evidence for the theory, and defending the work is also evidence for the theory. This happens constantly and it is a bad thing to have happen. A person can be extremely good at something, love it, spend enormous amounts of time on it, and be having a terrible year, with no connection between the two at all beyond the obvious one that difficult years leave more hours free.

The evidence for this root is historical and you already have it, which is why it is checkable in about four minutes.

Look at the years before the hard one. If the hours and the intensity were substantially the same then, this root is very likely yours, and the hard year changed the context rather than the behavior. Look at whether the intensity tracks the difficulty. Somebody whose work is carrying something has a curve that moves with the bad weeks. Somebody who loves their work has a curve that moves with the work.

Look at what happens on a genuinely enforced break. Not a holiday where you take the laptop. An enforced one, where you could not work if you wanted to. If those days are pleasant and slightly boring and you come back keen, that is a person who likes their job. Look at whether you talk about it. People carrying something with work tend to become quieter about the work as the load increases. People who love it tend to become tedious about it, which is a much healthier sign than it sounds.

And look at whether the work has ever been interrupted for something in your personal life, and whether the interruption was possible. If you have cancelled a deadline for a person in the past year and the world continued, the work is holding nothing up here. What you have is a thing you love that happened to run alongside a hard year, the two facts are unrelated, and everybody who has been giving you significant looks is wrong.

If this is you, the rest of this page is worth reading as information about other people, and there is nothing on it that you need to act on.

## What If You Are Buying Time You Genuinely Need?

Then you are running a holding pattern, you know it, and holding patterns are a legitimate thing to run.

This root gets almost no respect anywhere, and it deserves a great deal. Consider the situations it comes from. A parent whose decline will take three years and will end the way it ends, with nothing you do changing the trajectory. A marriage where you already know the answer and cannot act on it yet for reasons involving other people, most often children. A legal process with its own timetable that yours has to fit inside. A grief that is doing whatever grief does at its own speed while you wait for it to become smaller.

In every one of those, the thing has a duration and the duration is not yours to set. What are you supposed to do with the interval? Sit in it and look at it for three years? Nobody has ever shown that to be the superior form of coping, and in practice it produces a worse-organized version of the same waiting, with less to show for it at the end.

The distinguishing feature of this root, and the reason it is separate from the three below, is that the awareness is intact. You know what you are doing. You could say the sentence today: I am waiting for this to resolve and I have put the waiting into something useful. That sentence is the difference between a holding pattern and everything else, because a holding pattern has a known object and a rough end date, and the three roots below have neither.

Two things are worth watching if this is yours, and neither is a correction.

The first is drift. Holding patterns can outlive their object without announcing it. The thing you were waiting on settles, in March, quietly, and the structure that was built for the waiting carries on running for another year and a half because nothing ever asked it to stop. That is worth catching, and the only way to catch it is to have named the object in the first place, which you have.

The second is that a holding pattern occupies room. While it runs, other things do not happen, and some of them have their own clocks. That is a straightforward cost rather than a hidden one, and you are allowed to accept it knowingly. Accepting a cost knowingly is a completely different act from not seeing it.

## What If the Work Is the Only Route Your Feeling Is Permitted?

Then what arrives as effort arrives that way because effort is the only shape it was ever allowed to take.

This root starts early and it starts in a household with a particular arrangement in it. Feelings in that house were inconvenient. Not forbidden with any drama, usually, and often not even discouraged out loud. Simply not received. A child says something is wrong and gets a solution, or a redirection, or a slight tightening in the room that they read correctly and file. The same child does something impressive and the room changes temperature. Within a few years there is a route that works and a route that does not, and the working route gets used for absolutely everything, including things it was never designed to carry.

Nothing about that is a decision, and it is not a decision now either. Grown up, the mechanism is invisible from inside because it is fast. Something happens. Two days later you are at your desk at eleven at night on something that was not urgent when you started. There was no moment where you chose the desk over the feeling. The feeling turned into the desk somewhere upstream of anywhere you can watch.

The tell is timing, and it is unusually specific. Effort rises when feeling rises, at a lag of about a day or two, and the size of the rise tracks the size of the feeling. Look at the last time somebody said something that landed badly. Look at what you did with the following weekend. Look at the week after a difficult conversation with a family member, the week after a friend's news, the week after an anniversary you thought you had stopped noticing. If the pattern is there it is usually there several times over, and once it becomes visible it is very hard to stop seeing.

What makes this root particularly stable is that everybody around you is reinforcing it. The route works. It produced results at school, then at university, then at work. It has been rewarded continuously for twenty-five years by every institution you have passed through. Nobody has ever once said that the thing you did with your grief was slightly odd, because what you did with your grief was ship something impressive and everybody was pleased.

There is a real cost and the work has almost nothing to do with it. The cost is that the feeling never gets to be a feeling. It gets converted at the border, every time, and the version of you that could have had it simply does not get the chance. That is the thing worth looking at, and the reason for looking has nothing to do with your output being a problem. The reason is that you are missing a whole category of your own experience.

## What If the Output Is an Argument About Your Own Worth?

Then the work is evidence in a case, the case is the actual project, and no amount of evidence has ever closed a case.

This root looks like ambition from every angle and is a different animal underneath. Ambition wants the thing. This wants the verdict. The difference shows up in what happens after a win, which arrives, gets registered, and settles nothing, because the case was never really about that piece of evidence. There is a page here about [the win that never lands](/patterns/why-is-nothing-ever-enough/) and it covers that machinery in more detail than this page needs to.

The particular signature of this root, and the reason it belongs on this page rather than that one, is the audience. There is almost always a specific person the evidence is for, and it is frequently somebody who will not see it. A parent. A parent who has died. A version of a parent from when you were eleven who no longer exists in the person now alive. Somebody from school. An ex who has not thought about you in years. Ask yourself who would have to say something for the case to close, and most people in this root produce a name within a few seconds, along with a slight embarrassment about how fast it came.

The connection to the hard year is direct. Something that goes wrong in your personal life reads, inside this root, as evidence for the prosecution. A relationship ending arrives as considerably more than a loss. It lands as a data point in a longstanding argument about whether you are fundamentally all right. And the response to a data point for the prosecution is a data point for the defense, urgently, in the only currency you have ever been able to produce reliably.

Which is why the work gets so much better during exactly the period you would predict it to get worse. You are not coping badly. You are arguing.

The cost here is structural rather than emotional. A case built on evidence can always take one more piece, so there is no configuration of results that ends it. People in this root routinely describe reaching a target they set fifteen years earlier, at a level they would then have found absurd, and finding the argument entirely intact on the other side of it.

## What If the Absorption Itself Became the Point?

Then the subject is interchangeable, and you can check that against your own history in about a minute.

This is the cleanest of the five to identify and the most awkward to say out loud, because the individual projects are all perfectly good and there is nothing to criticize in any of them. The state is what you are after. Complete occupation of your attention, for hours, with the rest of your life politely outside the door. It is a genuinely good state. Anybody who has had it wants it again. There is nothing wrong with wanting it.

The evidence is in the sequence rather than in any single item. Three or four subjects across eight or nine years, each one total while it ran, each one dropped without much ceremony when the next arrived. The endurance sport, then the business, then the instrument, then the qualification. Told individually every one is a story about a person with wide interests. Laid out in a row with the dates attached, the intensity is the constant and the subject is the variable, and that is a different picture.

What connects this to the hard year is a supply question. The state does something specific: it removes you from your own circling thought for as long as it runs. During an ordinary period that is a pleasant feature. During a difficult one it becomes the reason, and the demand for the state goes up sharply, which is why people in this root can usually date the start of the current subject to within a fortnight of something they would rather not talk about.

The reason this root is worth separating from the other four is that the fix people reach for is always wrong. They try to give up the current subject, which is fine, and then the state reappears three months later attached to something else, because nothing in that manoeuvre touched the demand. The subject was never the thing.

## Which of the Five Is Yours, Using Only Evidence You Already Have?

Six questions, none of which requires you to introspect about your motives, which is the least reliable instrument in the box.

1. **What did your hours look like in the two preceding years, before the difficult thing started?** Substantially the same means root one. A visible step change means one of the others.
2. **Can you name, in one sentence, what you are waiting for?** A clean answer with a rough timescale in it means root two, and root two is a plan rather than a pattern.
3. **What happened in the week after the last time somebody upset you?** If there is a reliable relationship between emotional weeks and heavy work weeks, at a lag of a day or two, look at root three.
4. **Who would have to say something?** If a name arrives fast, root four is in the picture whatever else is.
5. **How many subjects have carried this intensity, and over how long?** More than two, dropped cleanly, means root five.
6. **What happened the last time you genuinely could not work for a week?** Annoyed and fine points one direction. Unbearable in a way you could not explain to anybody points the other.

Most people get two answers pointing the same way and one pointing somewhere else, and the honest reading is usually that two roots are running together. That is normal. A person can love their work, be running a holding pattern inside it, and still be arguing a case with the output. What the questions buy you is a shortlist rather than a verdict.

## Why Does Being Praised for Dedication Land So Strangely?

Because you are being given credit for a quality while holding a piece of information about the mechanism that the person praising you does not have.

The compliment is accurate. That is what makes it awkward. Nobody is being fooled about the output, which is real and was hard and did require something from you. What they are missing is the timing, and the timing is the part you cannot mention, partly because it would require explaining the personal thing and partly because it sounds, said out loud, like an unusually elaborate way of fishing for a larger compliment.

So it goes in sideways. There is a small internal correction attached to the thanks, something in the region of *if you knew when this started you would think about it differently*, and the correction has nowhere to go, so it just sits there. Over a long enough stretch it accumulates into a low-grade suspicion that your reputation and your reality are two separate objects, which is corrosive in a slow and unremarkable way and is very rarely mentioned to anybody.

Worth noticing that this reaction is itself diagnostic. Somebody in root one takes the compliment cleanly and enjoys it. The sideways landing suggests the work is carrying something, because you cannot get a guilty feeling about praise for a thing that is only what it appears to be.

## Where Does This Hide When You Would Not Call It Work?

Almost everywhere, and the versions that do not look like a job are the ones that go unexamined for the longest.

| Where it shows up | What it looks like from outside | What it can be doing |
|---|---|---|
| Training | A serious block starting within a fortnight of an ending | Structure, exhaustion, and a legitimate reason to be unavailable |
| Study | A qualification begun in a year with no obvious room for one | A future that is definitely arriving, unlike the other one |
| Looking after people | Total availability to everyone except the person it concerns | Being needed continuously, which leaves no gaps |
| A house | A renovation that expands to fill every weekend for a year | Visible progress on something, when nothing else is progressing |
| A cause | Sudden and total commitment to something outside your life | Effort that is unambiguously good, aimed away from home |
| Parenting | Every waking hour engineered around a child's schedule | The one role nobody will ever question you for overdoing |

The last two are worth a sentence each, because they are the hardest to look at. Nobody is going to tell you that you are doing too much for a cause or too much for your children, and both are genuinely good things to be doing. The worthwhileness of the activity was never the question here. What matters is how the interval feels when the activity is unavailable, which for a parent is the evening they are at their grandparents and for a volunteer is the week the project is between phases.

## What Actually Happens the Evening the Project Ends?

This is the part of the page that matters, and it comes down to roughly seventy minutes.

The work finishes. Whatever finishing means for your version: the campaign closes, the thing ships, the season ends, the qualification is awarded, the house is done. There is usually a short official period of people saying well done, and then that ends too, and then there is an evening.

Six-thirty on a Friday. Nothing scheduled. Nothing owed to anybody until Monday, and possibly nothing owed on Monday either. The specific quality of that quiet is worth describing accurately, because people rarely do: it arrives faster than you expect, it is not restful, and it has an odd attentiveness in it, like a room that has just gone silent because everyone in it is looking at the same thing.

And the thing you were not looking at is exactly where it was. Not worse. Not better. Unchanged in a way that is genuinely strange to encounter, because fourteen months went past and something normally happens in fourteen months. It waited without doing anything at all.

Here is why this evening is the diagnostic and everything else on this page is context.

Everything before it was not chosen. Nobody decides to be absorbed. Nobody selects the relief of an occupied mind. The work arrived, you were good at it, it took your attention, and the taking of your attention did the rest without consulting you. There is nothing to be responsible for in any of that, which is exactly why treating the pouring as a moral failure is both unkind and useless.

The evening is different. It is the first genuinely open interval in a long time, and what goes into it is up to you in a way that nothing else in this pattern was. Most people fill it inside about seventy minutes. A new plan gets made. A next thing gets identified, and identifying it produces an immediate and noticeable improvement in mood, which is the tell. Occasionally the filling is more literal and involves a drink, a screen or a long drive.

Nothing on this page says do not fill it. The suggestion is much smaller and it is the only suggestion here: **notice what you reach for, and notice how long it took.** That is it. Do not resolve anything, do not sit with anything, do not make any decision about your life at six-thirty on a Friday, which is the worst possible time to make one. Simply observe the reach and time it.

Because the five roots produce five completely different evenings, and this is the only place where they are distinguishable. Root one is flat and pleasant and starts thinking about the next project out of appetite rather than pressure. Root two knows precisely what the evening means, because the thing being waited on is now closer or has arrived. Root three has something turn up with nowhere to put it and converts it into a plan within the hour. Root four cannot stay in the room because an interval with no evidence being produced in it is exposure. Root five is simply looking for the next subject and knows it.

## Why Does the Next Thing Start So Quickly?

Because the interval is the expensive part, and beginning removes it.

The speed is remarkable once you look for it. People describe finishing something substantial and having the next commitment in place inside a week, sometimes inside a day, occasionally before the current one has been properly closed out. The transition is so smooth that nobody watching would think to comment, and the smoothness is doing a job.

[The general habit of moving straight to the next thing has a page of its own here](/patterns/why-am-i-always-busy/) and covers the mechanics of it. What this page adds is a question about what specifically got skipped, because two different things can be missing. One is the marking, which is [covered properly on the page about not celebrating wins](/patterns/not-celebrating-wins/). The other is the arrival, meaning the moment where the situation you have not been looking at becomes visible again because nothing is standing in front of it. Those look identical from outside and need opposite things.

There is a useful test buried in this. Try, once, to leave the gap slightly longer than is comfortable. Not a month. Four days. Notice what shows up on day two, because whatever shows up on day two is the thing the speed was for.

## What Does This Cost When the Output Is Genuinely Good?

Three things, and none of them are the ones people usually list.

The first is that the situation gets no attention during a period when attention might have mattered. Some things resolve on their own and some do not, and a proportion of the ones that do not are on a clock. A parent's remaining years. A child's specific age. A relationship that had a repairable window. The cost has nothing to do with failing to feel your feelings. The cost is that a decision with a real deadline sat undecided while you were busy, and the deadline stayed invisible from inside the work.

The second is the reputation gap described above, and it is a slow cost. Being known for a version of yourself that you privately consider inaccurate is tiring in a way that takes years to register.

The third is what happens to the relationships that ran alongside the stretch. People adjusted. They stopped expecting much, kindly, and made their arrangements accordingly. When the project ends and you look up, the adjustment does not reverse automatically, because nobody was told it was temporary. This is the cost people report as the surprising one, and it is not a matter of anybody being angry. They simply built a version of their life that did not require you, out of politeness, and it is still there.

## What Does the Version That Holds Look Like?

Considerably less dramatic than the pages about this usually suggest, and it does not involve working less.

The person who has moved this still works hard, often at the same intensity, and frequently on the same thing. What changed is small and structural. There is at least one person who holds both halves. Not a public disclosure and not a rearrangement of their friendships. One person who knows the dates of the hard thing and can see the output, so the joining sentence exists somewhere outside their own head.

The second change is that the interval at the end no longer gets filled by reflex. It might still get filled, and quickly, and that is allowed. The difference is that the filling is now visible to the person doing it, which turns it from a mechanism into a choice. Choices can be made the same way twice and still be choices.

The third is that the thing being waited on has a name and, where possible, a date. That applies even where nothing can be done about it. A situation you cannot change is easier to carry when it is a known object with an outline than when it is a general weight you have arranged your working life around without ever describing.

And the fourth, which people mention last and which seems to matter most, is that the feeling gets to arrive as a feeling occasionally, in its own shape, without being converted into a project at the border. Not often. Not for long. Just occasionally, and enough to know that the conversion was not compulsory.

## What Actually Moves This?

Four things, and the first three change nothing about how you work.

1. **Write the two sets of dates down.** The strongest professional stretch and the hardest personal one, on the same piece of paper, one under the other. That is the whole exercise. Most people have never done it and the doing of it is the intervention.
2. **Time the reach.** At the end of the next thing that finishes, note when the next commitment appears and how long the gap was. Do not extend the gap. Just record the number, because the number is information you have never had.
3. **Run the six questions during a real interval rather than now.** They are much more honest on the Friday than they are while you are absorbed and can construct any answer you like.
4. **Give one person both halves.** One conversation, once, with somebody who already knows about the hard thing, in which you also say what the work was doing during it. This is the smallest possible version of the change and it is the one that reliably produces a different next project.

Expect resistance at step four and expect it to arrive as practicality. You will find that there is nobody appropriate, or that it would be unfair to raise it, or that it would take too long to explain. Those are all reasonable-sounding and they arrive in exactly the way that reasonable-sounding objections do when something is being protected.

One thing not to do, and this page is unusual for saying it. Do not stop the project in order to deal with the underlying thing. Not because the underlying thing does not matter, but because for two of the five roots there is no underlying thing to deal with, and for the other three, removing the structure before you know which one you have reliably produces a much worse month with nothing to show for it.

## Where Does the Research on This Page Come From?

Four bodies of work, one of them much weaker than its reputation, and it is worth knowing which is which.

On sublimation itself, the source is psychoanalytic theory of the early twentieth century rather than any research programme. There is no key study to point you at, because there is not one, and any page that offers you a citation for sublimation as a finding is overselling something. It is a described model with a long history and essentially no experimental record.

On the measurement of defenses, Cramer published the coding manual for narrative material in 1991 and set out the case for empirical work on defenses in the American Psychologist in 2000. The distinction between conscious coping and unconscious defense is drawn in her 1998 paper in the Journal of Personality. The three defenses her manual codes are denial, projection and identification.

On the two forms of strong attachment to an activity, Vallerand and colleagues published the paper separating harmonious from obsessive passion in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology in 2003, and a large literature has followed it. The measures are self-report and much of the evidence is cross-sectional.

On attention and off-task thought, Smallwood and Schooler published their review of the mind-wandering literature in Psychological Bulletin in 2006 and a further review in the Annual Review of Psychology in 2015. The relationship between task demand and reduced off-task thought is well replicated at laboratory timescales, and nothing in it was designed to be extended to a project lasting a year.

The rest is mine and carries no research weight at all. I hold no clinical qualification of any kind, and I am not a therapist. None of the researchers named here has any connection to this page or to the seminar. Where their published work and my reading of it disagree, theirs is the one to go and check.

## How Long Does This Take to Change, Honestly?

The noticing takes one project cycle, because the diagnostic sits at the end of one, and there is no way to run it early.

After that it splits by root and the speeds are very different. Root one requires nothing and should be left completely alone, and the correct outcome for somebody in root one is that they finish this page reassured and go back to work. Root two resolves when the thing being waited on resolves, and the only useful addition is a note in a calendar to check, a year out, whether the structure is still doing the job it was built for.

Root three is the slow one. A route carved at seven has thirty years of successful use behind it and it does not move on insight. It moves on instances, meaning occasions where something is felt and named at roughly the time it happens and does not get converted, and the first several of those are awkward and unconvincing. Six months of that is a realistic picture. It does not require you to work less at any point.

Root four moves at the speed of the case rather than the work, and the case is usually about a specific person. That is a longer project and it is a different one, and it is the one where a page is least useful.

Root five moves fastest of the three, because the demand is for a state and states can be sourced elsewhere once the substitution is visible. What it needs is honesty about the sequence, which usually means writing the subjects down with their dates, at which point most people stop arguing with the reading.

Expect the loss before the benefit in all cases. Anything that was quietly doing a job leaves a gap when it stops doing it, and the gap is felt before anything replaces it. It feels like something is missing, and that is always the case with anything removed from a life, and it passes considerably faster than people expect.

## What Is Worth Doing Before Your Current Project Ends?

One thing, and it has to be set up before the ending rather than after it, which is why this section is here rather than at the top.

Put a marker in your calendar for the day after the current thing finishes. Not a plan for that day. A marker, with two words on it, telling you to notice what you reach for and to write down how long it took. That is the entire preparation, and it takes twenty seconds, and it will give you better information about yourself than any amount of thinking about your motives will.

If what landed hardest here was the two sets of dates, and specifically the discovery that you have never assembled the sentence that holds both of them, [our Sacred Valley seminar](https://florianrolke.com/the-soulsearching-experience/) is built for that layer rather than for the behavior on the surface. Five days is long enough to work out which of the five roots is actually running, and to build the structure that keeps the interval visible once you are home and the next project is already offering itself. Everybody is screened beforehand, which keeps the group small enough that somebody excellent at producing results cannot quietly spend the week producing results instead of looking at their own year. The weeks after the flight home are part of the design rather than something left to you, because as Florian puts it, no truth is ever going to change your life, and you will need to change your life.
